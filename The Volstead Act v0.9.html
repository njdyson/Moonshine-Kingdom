THE VOLSTEAD ACT

A 2-Player Prequel. Cold capital, a short fuse, and a bench that still believes in the law.

1920. The ink on the Act is barely dry and the judges still mean it. There are no Jobs to pull and no Respect to earn here, only two upstart outfits, a river of mash, and a doomsday clock in a federal office downtown. Strip the Kingdom back to its supply chain and see who can run it.

Where this sits: lifted out of the Rulebook v0.9 and parked here as a standalone variant. It is unplaytested, and it cuts two of the main game's pillars (Jobs and Respect), so it was pulled from the core book to keep the Kingdom's rules honest about what has actually been played. Everything it does not override is read straight from the Rulebook.

The Volstead Act: 2-Player Prequel

Before the Kingdom, there was the Act. Two upstart outfits, a river of mash, and a bench that hangs you for a loud night's work. When the city shrinks to two, backroom politics give way to cold capital, and the game becomes a tight supply-chain duel run on a doomsday clock. Everything named below overrides the Rulebook; anything it does not name is played exactly as written there.

Setup: Two Upstarts

  1. Two outfits only. Set the East Side Vipers aside, they haven't formed yet. Each player takes one of the remaining three Mobs (Sicilians, Irish, or Knights); draft or choose.
  2. Carve up the city as normal. Run the standard Setup in full: deal the Borough Deeds to establish home turf, garrison the Districts the Town Planner names, raise the Blue Wall, take your cash and Influence, and deal the Turn Tokens. Two upstarts on a four-Borough map: the empty half of the city is the point.
  3. No Jobs, no Respect. Return all Job Cards to the box; skip The Offers each morning and go straight to funding the Ledger. Respect is not tracked.
  4. One title stands. The Deeds have done their work: now return every Borough Deed to the box, and every Title but the Harbormaster (whoever holds the most Docks still sets Tomorrow's Mash). In this young city, only the river carries a name worth having.
  5. Start the clock. Place the Federal Crackdown Tracker by the Heat Track with a token on space 1. Every Police Raid advances it one space, exactly as in The Blood Oath (see the Rulebook).

A Stricter Bench

The law is green and twitchy, and it watches the docks like a hawk. Three rules bend for the era:

  1. No shakedowns. The Extort Play is unavailable. Every dollar must run through the still, the docks, and the speakeasy, there are no open rackets yet.
  2. The Greed Tax bites at three. Moving 3+ barrels in a single Play (by Unload or Peddle) draws Heat (down from 4+).
  3. The city boils over fast. The Heat Track is a 3-space fuse: the 3rd marker (or a Rat) triggers a Police Raid. Ignore the 4th and 5th spaces. A loud night gets the doors kicked in before dawn.

Two Roads Out

There is no legend to forge here, only two ways to end up on top:

  • Buy the Bench. The instant you hold all 10 Influence markers and your honor, the game ends and you win. The quiet road: bribe your way to the top before the Feds ever find you.
  • The Reckoning Count. The moment the Crackdown token reaches space 10, finish the current Day, then open the books. The richest honest Kingpin takes the city, loose change and all (include the value printed on your Playbook, exactly as in the main game). No two bankrolls can tie.

A Kingpin Must Have Honor: neither road crowns a Rat or a debtor. While you hold the Rat Card or a single unpaid Shylock's Mark you cannot take the city, however fat your bankroll. This overrides the Rulebook: in the Kingdom the brand costs a Rat 3 Respect, but there is no Respect in this young city to take, so here the Card bars the crown outright, exactly as a Mark does. It still leaves you only one way, when a rival Rats and takes it off you. 1920, and the bench still believes a rumour. Square up first, at $2,000 a Mark and a whole Play to make the drop: borrowed money spends like anyone else's, but it is not yours when the books open. Shylock lends in fifteens and collects in twenties, and this bench counts every penny. If the Count arrives with nobody clean, the Feds take the city and every outfit goes down with it.

The two roads fight each other on the same clock. The briber sells sparingly and keeps the streets quiet, stalling the count; the profiteer sells hard to bank the most cash, and every loud sale drags the Feds a step closer to ending the game on their terms. You cannot walk both roads at once, and each Raid that seizes a rival's staged booze is a payday burned before it could be banked. And watch the third clock: Shylock. A Mark is the fastest cash in the game and the only debt the bench won't overlook, so every loan you take is a Play you owe yourself later, at a worse price, on a deadline you don't control. Beg early, square up before the tenth space, or don't beg at all.
