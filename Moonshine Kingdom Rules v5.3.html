RULEBOOK

A Game of Bootlegging, Backstabbing, and Gang Warfare.

New York City, 1926. Prohibition was supposed to dry the city out. Instead it lit a fuse. Every basement is a brewery, every back room a bank, and four mobs are clawing for the throne of the liquor trade. Bootlegging, bribery, and intimidation are your tools. Forge fragile alliances, corner the market, and turn the Police loose on your rivals. This city has a thirst that can't be quenched, and only one mob will wear its crown. Carve your name into the skyline, or vanish like cigar smoke in a hurricane.

A Day in the Life of a Kingpin

2-4 Players | 90-120 Mins

Prohibition didn't stop the drinking; it just made the price go up.

In Moonshine Kingdom, you are a rising Kingpin in 1920s New York. Prohibition has turned off the taps, creating a gold rush for anyone ruthless enough to fill the glass. Four rival mobs claw for the boroughs, and this city isn't big enough for all of you. The game is played over a series of Days. Each Day unfolds in three brutal phases: from the fog of morning to the bloody consequences of night, outmaneuver your rivals to survive.


Morning: Shadows Phase

Dawn breaks over the Hudson. Before the city wakes, the boilers burn. Fire up your Stills, stake your black-market Contracts, and turn cheap mash into liquid gold.

Day: Operations Phase

The sun is up, and so is the heat. Command your Runners to smuggle product, seize turf, and strong-arm the competition. Every move costs Influence: your political clout. Use it to expand, but remember: violence is loud, and the Police are listening.

Night: Reckoning Phase

Twilight settles like a bruise. The guns go quiet, the blood washes into the gutters, and the ledger opens. Claim your turf, cash in your Contracts, and let the Police sweep up the careless. Did you make a killing, or just a mess?

The Only Crown Worth Having

Cash buys bullets. But Respect buys immortality.

You earn Respect by conquering Boroughs, running the docks and the nightlife, pulling off Contracts, and holding the most Ghettos to be named Ward Boss. The first mob to hold a Commission Card and 20+ Respect takes the crown.

Game Components: Tools of the Trade

Your whole damn operation in miniature. Every piece earns, bleeds, or burns.

The City Map (Game Board): A soot-stained blueprint of 1920s New York, carved into 25 Districts. It includes a shared 5-space Heat Track measuring Police pressure. The city's 8 Dock Districts are stamped with permanent Pier Numbers: 1, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 6. The harbormaster painted them on decades ago. Bribe him all you want; the numbers don't move.

Operations Boards (x4): Your personal command center. A slotted board used to manage your daily action economy and cycle your Influence.

Mob Pieces (x4 Sets): Each player claims a color and commands:

  • 1 Boss, 15 Runners, and 3 Safehouses: The brains, the muscle, and the hideouts.
  • 12 Influence Markers: The currency of the underworld. Tokens used to pay for daily actions, stake Contracts, and absorb Police Heat.
  • 1 Mob Playbook: The family secrets and your unique mob abilities.

The Ledger (Cards): The paper trail of your empire.

  • Contract Cards: High-risk, high-reward jobs that build your legend.
  • Borough Deeds (5): Proof of your dominion over the city's major territories.
  • Titles: The Ward Boss, King of Clubs, and Harbormaster: global crowns for the bosses who rule the Ghettos, the nightlife, and the waterfront.
  • Shylock's Mark Cards: Debt trackers. A lifeline with teeth.
  • The Rat Card (1): The ultimate sin in cardboard form. A hot-potato mark of shame that kills your street cred and freezes you out.
  • Favor Cards: Your binding ties to the city's corrupt elite.
  • Commission Cards: Your seat on The Commission, the council that crowns kingpins.

Still Tokens (25): Hidden boilers numbered 2-12. Flipped to produce hooch when the dice roll your way.

Turn Tokens (8) & Moon Marker: Track daily initiative, alternating between New Moon and Full Moon Days.

Liquor Barrels & Upgrades: The lifeblood of your operation.

  • 100 Liquor Barrels: Liquid gold in four flavors: Moonshine (Grey), Bathtub Gin (Pink), Whisky (Yellow), and Havana Rum (Brown).
  • 20 Still Caps: 14 Pink (Gin) and 6 Yellow (Whisky). Caps stack: a Whisky boiler wears a pink cap with a yellow cap on top.

The Dice (10): 5 White and 5 Red. One White and a fistful of Red run the morning brew. The rest settle grudges and run blockades.

The Bankroll (Cash): Dirty, untraceable bills in denominations of $100, $500, and $1,000.

The Law (Blue Pieces): The blue wall that wants to see you swing.

  • Precincts (5): Fortified station houses marking Police Turf.
  • Federal Crackdown Tracker (1): A 10-space pressure clock used in the 4-player team variant.

Key Concepts: The Underworld at a Glance

Your Mob: The Brains, the Brawn, and the Bastards

From the cold steel of the Sicilian Syndicate to the fiery grit of the Hell's Kitchen Irish, each mob brings their own brand of violence to the city's bloodstained ballet. You command:

  • A Boss: Your kingpin. The head of the snake. Bring him into a firefight to make your attacks deadlier, but if he catches a bullet, your crew is crippled until you crown a successor.
  • Runners: Your foot soldiers. Knuckle-draggers and wheelmen who haul hooch, guard your turf, and catch your bullets. You are strictly limited to 15 Runners. You cannot build a massive army to occupy the whole city. To expand, you must leave borders thin and rely on the threat of violence rather than an actual army.
  • Safehouses: Your fortresses in the chaos. They lock down Turf even when empty, provide a massive defensive advantage in shootouts, and are the only places you can Recruit fresh muscle.

The Caps: Everything caps at 5. Brewing, Smuggling, Blowbacks, Raid arrests, and Combat dice all read straight off the table. Brewing yields barrels outright; Smuggling sets the dice you roll for them (see Supply Lines).

The Map: Know Your Turf

Your empire is a broken city: 25 Districts across 5 Boroughs, each a flavor of poison and profit. Certain key Districts offer unique opportunities:

  • Speakeasies: The only spots where you can Unload Liquor for cash. 12 across the city: 3 Gin Joints, 3 Whisky Bars, 3 Moonshine Dens, 3 Rum Clubs. Each has a House Pour; feed them their favorite poison to earn Kickbacks.
  • High Society Districts: Each holds a Speakeasy with its own House Pour. Control the District and keep the right glasses full, and the local Power Broker may lend you their Favor (see Favors).
  • Ghettos: Gritty blocks where mob roots run deep. Hold the most at Reckoning to claim the Ward Boss card and collect Union Tax when rivals Recruit.
  • Docks: Each carries a printed Pier Number (1–6). More Piers means more dice for Smuggling; each Pier's number also serves as your private mash during the Brew. All Dock Districts connect across the water as if adjacent.
  • Stills: Every District contains one face-down Still token (numbered 2–12). Dormant until you Control the District and pay to Rig it (see Operations: Rig). Once face-up, it fires when its number is brewed, and risks Blowback when the leftovers match.

The Currencies of the Underworld

To rule the city, you'll need more than just bullets. You'll need to master its three core resources:

  • Cash: Expands your crew. Spend it to Recruit and Secure turf. Earn it by Unloading, Extorting, and completing Contracts.
  • Liquor Barrels: Your lifeblood. Brewed at Stills or smuggled from Rum Row. Each liquor has a set street value, paid out in full when you Unload it at a Speakeasy.

Influence: Currency of Power

Influence is your political clout. You start the game with 7 markers (earn more by winning Power Broker Favors). These markers cycle through your Operations Board:

  1. The Stash (Reserves): Uncommitted markers stack here. Use these to stake new Contracts.
  2. Fund (Shadows Phase): Move up to 5 markers from your Stash to your Operations slots. Excess markers stay in the Stash and cannot fund Plays today.
  3. Execute (Operations Phase): Spend markers from your Operations slots to make Plays.
  4. Resolve (After the Play): Quiet Plays return spent markers to your Stash. If the Play generates Heat, the marker is locked onto the communal Heat Track instead.

Kingpin's Tip: Capacity vs. Time

Extra Influence does not give you more daily actions; your board is strictly capped at 5 slots. A massive Stash simply gives you the capacity to absorb Heat, survive Police Raids, and stake multiple Contracts at once without crippling your daily hustle. You can't out-action your rivals; you have to out-think them.

Territory: Lines in the Concrete

An empire isn't built on bullets and blood alone; it's built on logistics. Moving product, and drawing borders your rivals are too terrified to cross. Because your muscle is spread thin, a cleverly placed Safehouse and a handful of guards are often enough to deter a turf war.

1. Control: Boots on the Ground

You can't tax what you don't hold. You Control a District if you have at least one Mobster (Boss or Runner) OR a Safehouse present there.

  • Instant Possession: Control shifts the moment your piece enters a District. (Moving through an unoccupied District claims it for that instant.)
  • Abandonment: A barrel of gin can't hold a street corner. If your last Mobster vacates a District and there is no Safehouse left to anchor the block, you immediately lose Control. Any Liquor left behind just waits to be stolen.
  • The Golden Rule of Turf: During the Shadows and Reckoning phases, there can be only one mob in a District. While you hold a District, rivals cannot enter without triggering combat.
  • Contested Ground: During a firefight, the original Occupier retains Control (and all defensive bonuses) until their last piece falls or retreats.

2. Safe vs. Hostile Turf

Knowing where you can move your hooch is the difference between a payday and a bloodbath.

  • Safe Districts: Any District you currently Control, plus any Defenseless District (meaning it contains no rival Mobsters or Police). Unattended Liquor and unguarded rival Safehouses do not make a District hostile; you can walk in and claim them (Commandeering the Safehouse).
  • Hostile Districts: Any District guarded by rival Mobsters or the Police. Stepping onto Hostile turf immediately leaves your Mobsters Pinned (see Combat: Pinned).

3. Connection: The Network

Knowing the layout of the city is key to flanking enemies and bypassing blockades. Districts are Connected if:

  • Land & Bridges: They share a physical border or are linked by a Bridge.
    (e.g., Williamsburg connects to Red Hook, Brownsville, Corona, and Astoria by land, and to Five Points by Bridge).
  • Waterways (Docks): All Dock Districts connect to each other across the water as if they were adjacent.
    (e.g., You can Move directly from West Side to Throggs Neck in one Play).

Your Goal: Earning Respect

Respect is the only currency that matters in Moonshine Kingdom. Earn it two ways: claim Titles by controlling the city, and complete Contracts: jobs too loud to ignore.

1. Titles: Carving Up the City

Titles are the proof that the street belongs to you. Borough Deeds, the Ward Boss, the King of Clubs, and the Harbormaster all follow the same hard rule:

Titles aren't owned, they're held. Every Reckoning Phase the city counts heads again and asks who actually runs each kind of turf. For each Title, the boss holding a strict majority (more Districts or Speakeasies of that type than any single rival) claims the card. Wear the crown one night and slip behind the next, and it passes straight to whoever now leads. If no single boss leads (rivals are tied at the top, or the turf has been abandoned to no one), the Title sits empty in the Supply until someone pulls ahead (see Stake Your Claim). Police do not count as rivals; Police-controlled Districts, Speakeasies, and Ghettos count as empty for Title checks.

Borough Deeds

Seizing a Borough turns muscle into empire. Hold more Districts in a Borough than any rival to claim its Deed. Boroughs are worth 3 Respect, except Staten Island at 2. Deeds unlock that Borough's Extort action.

King of Clubs

The city drinks where you let it. Hold more Speakeasies than any rival to claim the King of Clubs for 3 Respect, and unlock The Rounds: once a Day, send your collectors through every joint in town to shake your rivals down for their nightlife dues (see Title Plays).

Harbormaster

Nothing wet comes ashore without your say-so. Hold more Dock Districts than any rival to claim the Harbormaster for 3 Respect. Each morning the Title lets you lean on the day's brew, paying the bank to nudge the Public Mash before the draft begins (see Spike the Mash).

Ward Boss

The Boroughs make headlines, but the Ghettos decide who owns the block. Hold more Ghetto Districts than any rival to claim the Ward Boss card for 3 Respect, plus the Union Tax: whenever a rival Recruits, $100 per Runner from their hiring cost goes to you instead of the bank.

2. Contracts: Forge Your Legend

A mob's reputation isn't built on promises; it's built on results. Contracts are the high-stakes jobs that turn a corner hustler into a Kingpin.

Every Contract demands a specific Objective by a merciless Deadline. They come in three escalating tiers:

  • Gigs (1 Day / 1 Respect): Quick street hustles. Keeping the lights on and the cops paid off.
  • Rackets (2 Days / 3 Respect): Mid-level capers that take turf, muscle, and a solid plan.
  • Scores (3 Days / 5 Respect): Front-page heists. Bloody legends they write ballads about.

The Ticking Clock

When you stake a Contract, you're making a promise to powerful people. Place Influence markers from your Stash onto the card equal to the deadline (1, 2, or 3). These markers are locked up in planning. One marker returns to your Stash each Reckoning.

During the Reckoning Phase, if you pull the final marker off the card, The Deadline has arrived. The objective is checked against the board at that exact moment; turf you held yesterday counts for nothing. Your clients don't care about excuses.

  • Completed: If you meet the objective, you deliver. Add the card to your Respect pile and collect your Take.
  • Botched: If you miss the mark, you fail. Discard the card face-up into its deck's discard pile. In this town, nobody pays for 'almost'.

The Take

Pulling off a job doesn't just buy respect; it buys bullets. When you complete a Contract, the client immediately pays out a one-time cash reward:

  • Gig: $500
  • Racket: $1,500
  • Score: $3,000

The Commission

Any street thug can knock over a Speakeasy, but building a diverse portfolio proves you run an empire. Completing a Gig, Racket, and Score earns you a Commission Card: your seat on The Commission, and a strict requirement to win the game (see Winning the Game).

Setup: Carving Out Your Corner

  1. Prepare the City:
    Unfold the Game Board and lay out the Cash and Dice. Find the Stills listed on the Town Planning Ledger and set them aside; the rest of the city is seeded after the Deeds are dealt (see Establish Turf).
  2. Stack the Ledger:
    Shuffle the Gig, Racket, and Score decks and place them face-down beside the board. Keep the Shylock's Mark cards close by; someone will need a loan soon. Leave all other decks in the Supply.
  3. Claim Your Crew:
    Shuffle the Playbooks and deal one to each player to assign their family. Take your command center (Operations Board) and your muscle: 1 Boss (Sicilians take a spare, kept off the board until promoted via Capo), 15 Runners, and 3 Safehouses.
  4. Establish Turf:
    Nobody starts on Staten Island; return its Deed to the box. Shuffle the four remaining Borough Deeds and deal one to each player. This is your home turf. Deploy your starting forces to the three Districts listed on your Deed: place 1 Safehouse, 1 Boss, and 2 Runners in your Ghetto. Place 3 Runners at your Dock, and 3 Runners in your Speakeasy.

    Start the Boilers: Consult the Town Planning Ledger: for each Borough in play, place its listed Stills face-up in its three starting Districts, exactly as written. They brew Moonshine. Every empire starts in the gutter.

    Shuffle any unused Ledger Stills back into the supply with the rest, then seed one Still face-down in every remaining District. Nobody knows what's sleeping in those basements until somebody pays to find out.

    The Blue Wall: The law holds whatever ground the mobs haven't claimed. Place 1 Precinct in each of the five Boroughs, in the first Unoccupied District on its Town Planning Ledger list. With starting turf deployed, that's the High Society District of every home Borough, and the Ghetto of any Borough without a boss: Stapleton on Staten Island and, in a 3-player game, the undealt Borough. The cops always cover the block no boss bothered to claim.

  5. Get Your Cut:
    The bankroll for a rising kingpin. Take $1,500 cash and 7 Influence markers. Slot 5 markers onto your Operations Board, and drop the other 2 into your Stash.
  6. Turn Order (Who Strikes First):
    Randomly deal one New Moon Turn Token to each player (#1 upwards) to set the morning's initiative. Set the Moon Marker to New Moon and keep the Full Moon tokens nearby.

    In this city, the fast eat the dead. When you run out of Influence or choose to Lay Low, immediately swap your token for the lowest numbered available Turn Token of the opposite moon phase (e.g., snagging #1 ensures you shoot first tomorrow).

Gameplay: Run the Streets

Days unfold in three brutal phases:

Phase 1: Shadows

Dawn breaks over the Hudson. Before the city wakes, the boilers burn.

1. The Roll & The Draft

The boss holding Turn Token #1 rolls the communal pool: 1 White Die, plus Red Dice equal to Player Count + 1 (e.g., 4 players: 1 White, 5 Red). The White die is The Public Mash, the communal swill the whole city is cooking today. It stays on the table all morning. (Before the draft, the Harbormaster may Spike the Mash; see opposite.)

In Turn Order, each boss drafts exactly 1 Red die. Choose carefully: the last Red die left on the table sets the Blowback, and the last boss to draft chooses a victim.

2. The Brew

Every brew starts with a mash. Add your Red die to one of the two on offer to get your Brew Number:

  • Your Private Mash (a Pier You Control): that Pier's printed number. Your own dockside recipe, nobody else's business. (Control several Piers? Pick any one.)
  • The Public Mash (the White die): A boss with no Docks drinks with the rabble; they must use this.

Every face-up Still you Control matching your Brew Number fires. Brew Liquor of each Still's type using The Muscle Ratio. Your brew is yours alone; rival Stills stay cold. (If the barrel supply runs dry while several of your Stills share the number, you choose which of them get the last barrels.)

3. The Blowback

Add the leftover Red to the White: this is the Blowback Number. Copper pipes burst and basements burn. The Blowback is communal: every face-up, Controlled Still matching it suffers the consequences, no matter which mob owns the block:

  • Gin & Whisky Stills: No casualties; the equipment takes the hit. The Still downgrades one step by popping its top cap back to the Supply: a Whisky Still loses its yellow cap and reverts to Gin, a Gin Still loses its pink cap and reverts to raw Moonshine. Because caps stack, there's always a lower tier waiting underneath. Quality copper costs a fortune, but it's cheaper than coffins.
  • Moonshine Stills: Tally casualties using The Muscle Ratio (Runners take the shrapnel first; an unprotected Boss is killed), then flip the Still face-down. It produces nothing until somebody pays to Tap it again, and face-down Stills never explode. Cheap pipes, big bang. You can't burn down a rumor.

4. Grease the Wheels

  • Stake Contracts: Reveal Contracts from your hand. Place Stash markers equal to each card's Days to Deadline (1, 2, or 3). You may stake as many as you can afford. These markers are locked; they cannot fund Plays until returned at Reckoning.
  • Fund Operations: Move up to 5 markers from your Stash into your Operations slots. Markers left in your Stash cannot be spent on Plays today.

Example: A Morning on the Hudson

Setup: Three players. The boss holding Turn Token #1 rolls the communal pool: the White die (The Public Mash) shows 3; the four Red dice show 2, 4, 5, and 6.

1. The Draft

  • The Irish (Token #1): They Control Pier 5 and a face-up Gin Still numbered 9, so they draft the Red 4 (Pier 5 + 4 = 9).
  • The Vipers (Token #2): No Docks, so they must brew on the Public Mash. They draft the Red 5 (White 3 + 5 = 8), matching their Moonshine Still 8.
  • The Knights (Token #3): Only the 2 and the 6 remain. The Knights Control Pier 1 and a face-up Gin Still numbered 7, so the 6 fires their boiler (Pier 1 + 6 = 7), and the 2 they leave behind suits them fine. They draft the 6.

2. The Brew

  • Irish: Still 9 fires. With 3 Mobsters in its District, they brew 2 Bathtub Gin.
  • Vipers: Still 8 fires. With 5 Mobsters there, they brew 3 Moonshine.
  • Knights: Still 7 fires. With 2 Mobsters there, they brew 1 Bathtub Gin.

3. The Blowback

The leftover Red 2 joins the White 3: the Blowback Number is 5. Across town, the Vipers Control a face-up Moonshine Still numbered 5, guarded by 3 Mobsters. Cheap pipes, big bang: 2 Runners go up with the boiler (The Muscle Ratio), and the Still flips face-down.

The Vipers brewed rich at Still 8 and still lost a basement at Still 5. Your Brew fires only your own matching Stills, but the Blowback hunts every Controlled, face-up Still in the city. And note the Knights' draft: leaving the 6 behind instead would have set a Blowback of 9, downgrading the Irish Gin Still. The last seat picked its victim.

Spike the Mash

Before the draft begins, the boss holding the Harbormaster Title may lean on the day's brew: pay the bank $500 to nudge the Public Mash (the White die) up or down by 1. The numbers don't wrap: a 1 can't drop to a 6, and a 6 can't climb to a 1.

Kingpin's Note: The White die is public, so every Red in the pool is a Blowback waiting to happen. Sometimes the smart draft isn't the die that fires your best Still; it's the one that can't be left behind to burn it down. The first seat brews richest. The last seat holds the match.

Phase 2: Operations Phase

Operations runs in Turn Order, one Play at a time.

  1. Make a Play: Spend Influence to perform 1 Action (known as a Play): either a Standard Play (Move, Recruit, Push, etc.) or a Power Play (e.g., Unload).
  2. Combat Play: If combat is triggered, resolve it fully before the Play ends.
  3. Paying for Plays: Remove the spent marker(s) from your Operations and return them to your Stash. If a Play generates Heat, place 1 spent marker onto the Heat Track. Any remaining spent markers return to your Stash.
  4. Laying Low: When you run out of Influence (or choose to step off the street early), you must Lay Low:
    1. Clear any remaining markers from your Operations to your Stash.
    2. Swap your current token for the lowest numbered available Turn Token of the opposite moon phase (e.g., claiming #1 to guarantee striking first tomorrow).
Standard Plays (Cost: 1 Influence)
Move Move any number of Mobsters (carrying max 1 barrel each) to one Connected District. Entering rival or Police turf immediately leaves your Mobsters Pinned. Rivals get a Standoff choice first; Police won't fire, but instantly smash any carried Liquor.
Push Redistribute any number of barrels between a District you Control and one Connected District. Use this to reorganize your own supply lines, or to trade with a rival (with their permission). Barrels Pushed into a rival's District immediately become their property. Thanks for the donation. You cannot Push barrels into Police Turf.
Recruit Hire Runners at your Safehouse for $300 per Runner. (Union Tax: If a rival holds the Ward Boss card, you must hand them a $100 cut for every Runner hired).
Hustle Boss must be in play. Draw 2 Contracts (3 with Texas Guinan's Favor). Discard down to your Hand Limit (default: 3, or 5 with Texas Guinan's Favor). If a Contract deck ever runs dry, immediately shuffle its face-up discard pile to form a new face-down draw deck. Limit: once per Day.
Secure ($500) Establish a foothold. Place a new Safehouse in your District, or relocate an existing one to your current District.
Smuggle At a Dock District you Control: Send a crew from the Dock to roll for Havana Rum via The Muscle Ratio (failed dice = arrests; Runners take the fall first). See Supply Lines.
Rig Build the operation, then build it better. In a District you Control, pay the bank to do one of the following:
  • Tap ($200): Flip a face-down Still face-up. It brews Moonshine.
  • Refine ($300): Place a pink cap on a Moonshine Still. It brews Bathtub Gin.
  • Perfect ($500): Add a yellow cap on top of the pink cap. It brews Whisky.
Caps stack: a Whisky Still wears both a pink cap and a yellow cap, so a knocked-down boiler always has a lower tier waiting underneath. Caps are limited (14 Gin, 6 Whisky); when the supply runs dry, no further upgrades of that tier can be made. Upgrades belong to the District, not you. Lose the block, lose the boiler.

Kingpin's Note (Fast-Tracking): To save time, you may pay the combined cash cost to jump multiple tiers in a single Rig action (e.g., pay $500 to go straight from Face-Down to Gin, or $1,000 to go from Face-Down straight to Whisky). Just add the caps so they stack.
Title Plays (Cost: 1 Influence)
Extort Borough Deed required. Shake down the locals. Once per Day, earn $200 per District you Control in that Borough. Earning $800+ (controlling 4 or more Districts) is noisy and generates Heat.
The Rounds King of Clubs required. Collect your dues from the city's nightlife. Once per Day, every rival pays you $100 straight from their bankroll for each Speakeasy they Control. If a rival can't cover their full cut, they must immediately take a Shylock's Mark to settle up: they draw the $1,500, pay you your due, and pocket the rest. Last call, and the bill's on you.

Power Plays

These Plays cost two Influence markers to perform, but count as a single Play in the turn cycle.

Power Plays (Cost: 2 Influence)
Unload Cash Out: Sell barrels at a Speakeasy you Control for their street value: Whisky $500, Rum $400, Gin $300, Moonshine $200. Every dollar is yours; the street price is the street price.

The Kickback: Speakeasies pay in favors for their favorite poison. For every barrel sold that matches the Speakeasy's House Pour, immediately move 1 spent Influence marker from your Stash back into an empty slot on your Operations Board. (Note: Your board caps at 5 slots. Any Kickbacks earned beyond your empty slots are wasted).

The Greed Tax: Unloading 4+ barrels of any type in a single Play generates Heat. If this Unload was paid for with two different players' markers, you choose which spent marker goes to the Heat Track. Max safe delivery is 3, unless you hold Stephanie St. Clair's Favor.

The Toast of the Town: The city's elite can't be bought, but they can be entertained. If this Unload delivers 4+ barrels of the House Pour to a Speakeasy in a High Society District you Control, the District's Power Broker takes notice: take their Favor Card and add 1 permanent Influence marker to your Stash (see Favors).
Rat There's no honor among thieves when the sirens wail. Drop a dime on your rivals: as long as there is at least 1 marker on the Heat Track, trigger an immediate Police Raid. The Raid targets the player with the most markers on the track; ties chase the freshest lead (see Police Raids).

The Mark of the Snitch: Ratting is the ultimate street sin. When you make this Play, you immediately take the Rat Card from the supply (or snatch it from the rival currently holding it). While you hold this card, you are a pariah; nobody will do business with you, meaning you cannot perform the Hustle Play.
Rise Boss in the morgue? Time to anoint a successor. Only available while your Boss is off the board; a mob never has more than one Boss (Sicilian Capo excepted). Appoint a new Boss ($0) in any District you Control, or any District containing no pieces at all, and immediately spawn 2 free Runners with them. You may also instantly Secure this District for free by placing or relocating a Safehouse there.

Mob Signature Plays

Every crew has tricks up its sleeve: signature moves to outfox, out-muscle, or outrun the competition. Each mob has two unique plays.

Sicilian Syndicate

Old blood, older rules. An insult to the family is a death sentence.

Capo (Cost: 2) Promote a Runner from any District to Capo (switch Runner for the spare Boss piece). Your Capo is a second Boss, providing stability and gaining +1 Threat in Combat (+2 when both Bosses attack one District). You may only have 1 Capo (2 Bosses total).
Consigliere (Cost: 1) Use your connections to smooth over legal troubles. Remove 1 Influence marker of your choice from the Heat Track and return it to its owner's Stash (move other markers down to fill gaps if required).

Hell's Kitchen Irish

They brew it strong and fight dirty. Why buy bullets when a brick works just fine?

Courage (Cost: 0) Before you roll dice for an Ambush or for an Assault that you initiated, you may discard 1 of your Liquor barrels located in that District to gain +1 Threat for that roll. You cannot use Courage to boost your defense when a rival initiates an Assault against you.
Plunder (Cost: 1) Initiate a special attack. For each successful hit, instead of eliminating a Mobster, steal 1 Liquor barrel of your choice from the target District. You can carry these away if you Advance. Defender hits still kill. You may also choose to Plunder when you Ambush (Cost: 0). Plunder does not create Heat.

East Side Vipers

They move like smoke and strike like snakes. You won't know they're there until the shots come from the shadows.

Stealth (Cost: 1) Move up to 5 Mobsters (no Liquor) into a Connected rival District without triggering a Standoff (the Defender cannot Ambush or Fold). You cannot end your Play in this state. You must immediately spend another Influence to either Assault the District, or Advance to a Connected Safe District.
Firepower Pay a flat $200 when you Assault or Ambush to roll exactly one additional attack die (up to a maximum of 5). You cannot pay extra to buy multiple dice. Cannot be used when defending an Assault.

Harlem Knights

Dressed to kill, literally. The Knights hit from the water and leave warehouses burning.

Skiff (Cost: 1) Move up to 5 Mobsters (with or without Liquor) from a Coastal District you Control to any other Coastal District. A Coastal District is any District touching water, so this reaches far beyond the Docks. If you enter a rival District, standard Ambush rules apply.
Torch (Cost: 2) Burn down a Safehouse in a rival District. To perform this Play, you must have at least one Runner Pinned in the target District. Sacrifice one of those Pinned Runners; he strikes the match and goes up with the flames. He is removed from the game. The rival's Safehouse is immediately destroyed (return it to their supply). Any rival Mobsters and Liquor in the District are unharmed and retain Control. This Play generates Heat.

Phase 3: Reckoning Phase

Once the guns go quiet, the city settles its accounts. Each Reckoning stage resolves simultaneously for all players.

1. The Sweep

The cops don't mind a hustle, but they hate an army.
The Police sweep the streets. In any District with more than 5 Mobsters, remove excess Runners to bring the count down to 5, unless you hold Mayor Jimmy Walker's Favor.

2. Stake Your Claim

Titles prove you run the streets, and the streets are re-counted every night. Re-evaluate every Title (Borough Deeds, the Ward Boss, the King of Clubs, and the Harbormaster) against the board exactly as it stands now. Yesterday's holdings count for nothing.

  • The Sole Leader Holds It: For each Title, whoever holds a strict majority of that turf (more of it than any single rival) takes or keeps the card. Nothing else matters: a boss who has pulled ahead claims the crown outright, and a former owner who has fallen behind hands it over.
  • Or Nobody Holds It: If no single boss leads (two or more rivals are tied for the most, or the turf sits empty), the Title returns to (or stays in) the Supply. A deadlock crowns no one until someone breaks it.

Police do not count as rivals; Police-controlled Districts, Speakeasies, and Ghettos count as empty for every Title check.

3. Fulfill Contracts

Remove 1 marker from each of your active Contracts and return it to your Stash. Contract objectives are checked only now, after the Sweep and Title checks resolve.

  • The Deadline: If you removed the final marker, the deadline has arrived. You cannot back out of a promised job. If your current board state meets the objective, the Contract is Completed (add it to your Respect pile and collect the Take). If you missed the objective, the Contract is Botched (discard it face-up into its deck's discard pile for nothing). In this town, nobody pays for 'almost'.
  • Commission Check: If a newly completed Contract secures you a full portfolio (1 Gig, 1 Racket, and 1 Score), immediately take a Commission Card.

The Next Day...

If any Kingpin holds a Commission Card and 20+ Respect, the game ends immediately! (see Winning the Game).

If not, the war continues. Flip the Moon Marker and begin the next Day's Shadows Phase in Turn Order.

Winning the Game: The Final Reckoning

The war for New York ends during the Reckoning Phase, the night a player holds a Commission Card (earned by completing a Gig, Racket, and Score) and 20+ Respect. Victory is checked only at Reckoning; a crown seized at noon can still slip away before nightfall.

1. A Seat on The Commission

Any thug can fire a Tommy gun, but it takes a true mastermind to earn a seat on The Commission. When a newly completed Contract secures you a full portfolio (1 Gig, 1 Racket, and 1 Score), immediately take a Commission Card. A player may only ever claim one.

2. Earning Respect

Legitimacy means nothing if the streets don't fear you. You need 20 Respect to claim the crown. To calculate your Respect, tally the spoils of your empire:

  • Borough Turf: Gain 3 Respect for each Borough Deed you hold (2 Respect for Staten Island).
  • Ward Boss: Gain 3 Respect if you hold the Ward Boss card at the end of the game.
  • King of Clubs: Gain 3 Respect if you hold the King of Clubs (the most Speakeasies) at the end of the game.
  • Harbormaster: Gain 3 Respect if you hold the Harbormaster (the most Docks) at the end of the game.
  • Criminal Legends: Gain 1, 3, or 5 Respect for each completed Contract in your ledger.
  • Blood Debts: Subtract 1 Respect for every unpaid Loan. (Shylock always gets his due).
  • The Squealer's Grave: Subtract 3 Respect if you are stuck holding the Rat Card at the end of the game. (Nobody crowns a rat).

The Final Standoff

If multiple bosses hold a Commission Card and 20+ Respect on the same night, there's no war left to fight; The Commission simply opens the books and weighs one empire against the other:

  1. Total Respect: The highest Respect takes the crown.
  2. Cold Hard Cash: If deadlocked, the Kingpin with the fattest bankroll buys the throne (include the loose change printed on your Playbook).

There are no shared victories in Moonshine Kingdom. You either rule the city alone, or bleed out in the gutter.

Supply Lines: Smuggling from Rum Row

Smuggling Runs

Havana Rum is top-shelf poison. You can't brew it in a basement; you run it through the city's waterways.

Smuggle (Cost: 1 Influence): At a Dock District you Control, run the blockade.

  1. Assign Crew: Send any number of Mobsters from the Dock; your dice pool follows The Muscle Ratio (up to 5 dice).
  2. Check Network: Count your Controlled Docks to find your Target Number.
  3. Roll & Resolve: Roll your dice pool based on Crew Size.
    • Success: For every die that meets or beats your Target Number, gain 1 Rum, placed at the Dock.
    • Intercepted: The Coast Guard is on the prowl. For every die that fails to meet the Target Number, 1 Mobster is arrested and removed from the board (Runners take the fall first).

Example: You control 3 Docks and Smuggle with a crew of 5. Roll 3 dice, succeeding on 3+. A roll of 1, 3, and 5 gains 2 Rum at the Dock; the failed die arrests 1 Mobster.

The Coast Guard can't shoot what they can't see. Favors: Friends in High Places

The Power Brokers: Loyalty on Tap

Money talks, but the right friends scream. These brokers don't carry guns; they carry the city.

The elite can't be bought; cash is vulgar, and worse, it's evidence. Their loyalty is earned in spectacle. To win a broker's Favor, Control their High Society District and Unload 4+ barrels of the local House Pour at its Speakeasy in a single Play. Throw the party of the season, and the broker is yours: take their Favor Card and add 1 permanent Influence marker to your Stash, alongside the cash and Kickbacks the Unload earns as normal. A party that big never stays quiet; the Greed Tax applies as normal, so courting High Society still generates Heat (unless you hold Stephanie St. Clair's Favor).

Honor Among Thieves: Each broker's Favor can only be won once per game by each player, and a single Unload wins one Favor at most, no matter how many barrels you pour. Once won, their loyalty (and the Influence marker) remains yours even if you lose their District, unless you break a binding deal (see Cooperation).

  • Mayor Jimmy Walker – "The Night Mayor" (Morris Park)
    A suit, a smile, and a blind eye to the law. The Mayor keeps the cops looking elsewhere.
    City Hall Pass: Your Districts may hold any number of Mobsters during The Sweep.
  • Arnold Rothstein – "The Brain" (Williamsburg)
    The man who fixed the World Series doesn't play the game; he owns the casino.
    The Inside Vig: Shylock knows who signs his checks. Repay each Shylock's Mark for $1,500 instead of $2,500.
  • Stephanie St. Clair – "Queenie" (Sugar Hill)
    The Tiger of Harlem runs the numbers game and makes her friends look untouchable.
    Untouchable: Ignore the Greed Tax. You may Unload 4+ barrels in a single Play without generating Heat.
  • Texas Guinan – "The Hostess" (Richmond Hill)
    "Hello, Sucker!" Judges, senators, and stars all drink at Texas Guinan's table.
    High Society Whispers: The right whisper opens every door. When you Hustle, draw 3 Contracts instead of 2, and your Hand Limit rises to 5.

Shylock's Mark

When the walls close in, Shylock offers a lifeline made of barbed wire. Take his cash to survive today, but the debt will bleed you tomorrow.

  • The Loan: At any time before the endgame triggers, you may take a Shylock's Mark card to gain $1,500. You may hold multiple marks.
  • Paying the Piper: To clear the debt, pay $2,500 to the bank at any time before the game ends (or $1,500 if Arnold Rothstein vouches for you), then discard the card.
  • The Toll: Shylock always gets his due. Every unpaid Mark in your ledger at the final Reckoning costs you -1 Respect.

Cooperation: Puppets in the Smoke

Trust is fleeting, but even sharp kingpins need rivals. Every favor granted might be a knife in the back.

Pulling the Strings (Puppeteering)

At any time during Operations, you may move Influence markers from your Operations to a rival's Operations (up to their 5 marker cap). This is not a Play and does not end your turn.

  • The Favor: You lend your name and network. The rival may use these markers as their own, now or later.
  • The Risk: If the rival spends your marker on a Play that generates Heat, your marker goes to the Heat Track. Otherwise, it returns to your Stash once used.

The Broker's Word: A Binding Handshake

A Mobster's word is cheap. When a deal needs teeth, have a Power Broker vouch for you.

Making the Deal

To make a binding deal, a player must have at least one Favor Card.

  1. State the Terms & Deadline: The terms must be clear, and you must agree on a specific deadline (e.g., "before the Day ends," or "until you pay me $1,000").
  2. Stake the Favor: The player stakes an available Favor Card, placing it between both players. Each Broker may only vouch for one deal at a time.

Example: "The Mayor vouches for me. If you let me pass through your Borough unharmed, I promise to give you $1,000 before Day's end."

Honoring & Breaking Deals

Deals resolve the moment their conditions are met or failed.

  • Deal Honored: The moment the terms are met, the deal is complete. The Favor Card is immediately returned to its owner.
  • Deal Broken: If the agreed deadline passes without the terms being met, or if a player violates a standing agreement for any reason (including third-party interference), the Broker's trust is shattered and your word becomes garbage. The owner must discard the Favor Card and lose its permanent Influence marker.

Deals on the Side

You may wheel and deal outside the rules, but only Cash can be physically traded off the board. Hand it over anytime, no Influence required.

Barrel Exchanges: Liquor stays on the board. To swap barrels with a rival, use a single Push Play: with their permission, you can shift any barrels either way between your District and a Connected one, clearing the whole trade in one Play with no return turn needed.

Violence isn't a backup plan; it's the cost of doing business. Smart Kingpins know a drawn gun can be stronger than a fired one. When talk fails, step inside and clear the room.

Calculating Threat

Your Base Threat is Level 1 (Kill on a 5+). This improves with bonuses, which stack up to Level 4:

  • Boss Bonus (+1): If your Boss is in the fight.
  • Safehouse Bonus (+2): Defenders holding a Secured District (Defending Assaults only).
  • Ambush Bonus (+1): Occupiers firing the first shot when enemies enter.

2. The Standoff: Crossing the Line

When an opponent Moves into a District you Control, the game pauses. As the Occupier, you must immediately choose one of three responses:

  1. Ambush: You greet the invaders with lead.
    • Free Shot: Only the Occupier rolls dice.
    • Bonus: Gain +1 Threat.
    • Casualties: Hits remove Invaders (Runners first). No Heat. Invaders are now Pinned.
  2. Hold Fire: You stand your ground but don't shoot. Maybe they are just passing through? The Invaders are now Pinned.
  3. Fold (Cost: 0): You surrender the District. You may immediately move your Mobsters to one Connected Safe District.
    • Panic: Fleeing Mobsters cannot carry Liquor; any barrels are left behind. You also cannot pack up a building; your Safehouse remains.
    • The Takeover: The Invader immediately claims Control, captures dropped Liquor, and may Commandeer or destroy your Safehouse. The Invaders are not Pinned; their Play ends.

3. Pinned: Fight or Flight

While Pinned, your Mobsters cannot make normal Plays. The standoff must be resolved before your turn moves on: spend Influence to Assault or Advance, or, if you are the Invader, Fall Back for free. Keep choosing until the Pin is resolved, or the Invader runs dry and must Fall Back. Torch, Plunder, Courage, and Firepower still work while Pinned.

The Cost of Pride: A real Kingpin knows an all-out firefight is bad business. Burning 3 or 4 Influence on one stubborn Assault may win the alley, but it leaves rivals free across the city. Talk is cheap; bullets cost time.

Assault (Cost: 1 Influence) Fight for the District. Both sides roll simultaneously.
  • Casualties: Both sides apply hits (Runners first). Defenders lose Ambush bonus but keep Safehouse/Boss bonuses.
  • Heat: If the Attacker kills Defenders, gain Heat . Defenders gain no Heat for self-defense.
  • Repeat: If both sides stand, remain Pinned. Spend another Influence to Assault again.
Advance (Cost: 1 Influence) Shoot your way out. Move Pinned Mobsters (and up to 1 barrel each) to any Safe Connected District, including across water via Docks. You cannot enter hostile turf.
Fall Back (Cost: 0 Influence)
(Invader Only)		 Cut and run. Retreat to the origin District you entered from. Carried Liquor is left behind for the Defender.
If you have 0 Influence remaining, you must Fall Back.

4. Victory & Consequences

Combat ends when one side is eliminated or the attacker retreats.

If the Defender Holds: The District remains theirs. Liquor dropped by dead Invaders becomes Defender property.

If the Invader Wins: The moment the last Defender falls or flees, you claim Control of the District.

  • The Spoils: Any Liquor in the District is now yours.
  • Safehouses: A rival Safehouse in the District is destroyed (remove it), unless you Commandeer it instead: immediately Secure the District for free by placing one of your own Safehouses there (relocating one if you have no spares). Note: You may also Commandeer a Defenseless Safehouse by Moving into its District.

Heat: Playing with Fire

Heat measures how hard the law is breathing down your neck. The Heat Track is a 5-space powder keg: when you generate Heat, place one Influence marker on it. Played right, Heat is a weapon to sic the Police on your rivals. Played wrong, it's a noose.

Drawing Heat

These noisy Plays push the track up one notch (every Play marked with the flame icon in this book generates Heat):

  • Blood on the Pavement: Violence gets noticed. If the Attacker (the player who initiated combat) inflicts 1 or more kills, they add 1 marker at the end of the Play. A multi-round Assault generates Heat only once; Defenders never generate Heat for self-defense kills.
  • Shakedowns Get Loud: If an Extort Play earns $800+, locals complain and you create Heat .
  • The Greed Tax: Unloading 4+ barrels in a single Play attracts the Police and generates Heat .

Police Raids: The Boiling Point

A Raid triggers in two ways: the 5th marker lands on the Heat Track naturally, or a rival plays the Rat Card. Either way, pause the current turn: the city is going on lockdown. If the track filled naturally (no Rat played), old grudges don't matter: return the Rat Card to the supply. If a rival played the Rat, they keep it until used.

1. Public Enemy Number One

The Police don't care who started the war, only who's making the most noise: the mob with the most markers on the Heat Track becomes the Target.

The Fresh Scent Rule (Tiebreaker): If mobs are tied, the Police chase the freshest lead: the tied mob whose marker sits furthest right on the track (most recently placed). A Raid only ever targets one player.

2. Precision Strikes

The Police mobilize from their strongholds. Resolve one strike per Precinct on the board:

  1. Find the Leads: List every District that is adjacent to the Precinct, within the same Borough, and Controlled by the Target.
  2. The Big Bust: The Police strike the listed District with the most units (Mobsters + Barrels combined).
  3. The Target's Call: If listed Districts tie, the Target chooses which operation takes the hit.
  4. Cold Trail: A Precinct with no valid lead does not strike.

3. The Ultimatum: Bribe or Bleed

Resolve each struck District separately. The Target makes a brutal choice for each:

  • Pay the Bribe: Hand $1,000 to the bank. The cops look the other way. Your Liquor and Mobsters remain safe.
  • Take the Fall: Pay nothing, and lose everything. The raid is a success. Return all Liquor in the District to the supply, then suffer arrests per The Muscle Ratio (Runners take the cuffs first).

The Aftermath: Once all strikes are resolved, the Police rebuild (see Rebuilding the Blue Wall), all Heat Track markers return to their owners' Stashes, and the paused turn resumes.

The Law: Fighting the Blue Wall

The Police are a static, automated faction. They don't take turns, collect Liquor, or Ambush; they just stand firm and guard their turf.

The Blue Wall

Any District containing a Precinct is Police Turf.

  • Confiscation & Lockdown: You may freely Move into a Police District. However, you can't sneak hooch past a station house. Any Liquor carried into Police Turf is instantly seized and returned to the supply. Your Mobsters are immediately Pinned. From here, you must spend Influence to either Advance (walk through to a Connected Safe District), Fall Back, or Storm the Precinct.

Storm the Precinct (Cost: 1 Influence)

You don't negotiate with the blue wall; you tear it down. If you want to clear a Precinct, you have to burn it to the ground. This is a high-stakes, all-or-nothing assault. Bring body bags.

  1. The Charge: Roll your Attack Dice (via The Muscle Ratio) and apply your current Threat Level.
  2. The Toll: The Police are firing from fortified cover; they do not roll defense dice. Instead, every failed die immediately kills 1 of your Mobsters in the crossfire (Runners take the bullet first).
  3. The Outcome: You need 2+ Hits to break their lines. There is no multi-round combat; you get one shot.
    • Success (2+ Hits): The Precinct is destroyed (return to supply). You claim Control of the District and may immediately Commandeer it (Secure a Safehouse for free).
    • Failure (0-1 Hits): The assault is broken. Your surviving Mobsters are repelled and must Fall Back to the District they entered from.

Win or lose, Storming a Precinct generates no Heat. The Police bury their own scandals.

Rebuilding the Blue Wall

The law never bleeds for long. Immediately after every Police Raid, the city rebuilds its shattered Precincts. Any destroyed Precincts currently in the supply are returned to the board in their home Borough. Place each returning Precinct in the first District containing no pieces at all (no Mobsters, Safehouses, or Police), reading top-to-bottom on the Town Planning Ledger (found on the Turn Summary card). If no such District exists in that Borough, the Precinct remains in the supply.

The Blood Oath: 4-Player Team Variant

When the heat boils over, enemies become brothers. The Feds are kicking down doors and lone wolves are slaughtered. The Blood Oath is the recommended 4-player mode: a cutthroat free-for-all where escalating police pressure eventually forces the city into two permanent 2v2 alliances.

Setup: The Ticking Clock

Set up as a standard 4-player match, with one addition: place the Federal Crackdown Tracker near the Heat Track with a token on space 1. Every time a Police Raid triggers, advance the token one space.

The Sit-Down

The moment the token reaches space 10 on the Crackdown Tracker, the underworld snaps. Pause the game. The four independent mobs immediately form two permanent alliances.

  1. Rank the Bosses: Tally everyone's current Respect (scoring held Titles and completed Contracts). Tiebreaker: Total Cash on hand.
  2. Forge the Families: The player in 1st Place joins forces with the player in 4th Place. The player in 2nd Place pairs with the player in 3rd Place.

New Rules of Engagement

One goal, two crews. You still run your own operations.

  • Separate Ledgers: You do not share Cash, Influence, Contracts, or Hand Limits, and you still take individual turns in Turn Order.
  • Blood is Thicker: You cannot Ambush or Assault your partner. All other rules stand: the Golden Rule of Turf means you cannot share a District with them at Day's end, and Liquor Pushed into their District becomes theirs.
  • Puppeteering: Your strongest weapon. You may freely lend Influence to your partner's Operations Board to fund their turn (standard Heat rules apply).

Claiming the Empire

The Commission only recognizes unified power. To win, an Alliance must collectively hold at least 1 Commission Card and reach a Combined Total of 35 Respect. If met during the Reckoning Phase, the game ends immediately.

The New Kings of New York

When the game ends, tally the final Respect for all four players individually (applying Title points, Contract Respect, and Shylock debt penalties). The Alliance with the highest Combined Respect wins the war.

The Capo di Tutti Capi: There are no truly shared victories... even in a team. Within the winning Alliance, the player who contributed the most Respect to the final combined total is declared the Boss of Bosses and the true Kingpin of New York. Your partner doesn't get a crown; they just get the privilege of kissing your ring.
