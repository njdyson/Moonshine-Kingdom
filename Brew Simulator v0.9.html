The Table & The Money

One focal crew (your build) drafts against players rivals for 1 Mash die (the Public Mash) + (players+1) Red dice, in random seat order. The Mash die is the headline lever. Rolled is the classic random face — the world when no rival holds the Harbormaster Title, or when you hold it yourself. Hostile Harbormaster is the worst case: a rival names the face that minimises the chance any of your Stills can be reached by a draftable Red (1–6), and breaks ties toward a face that also threatens a Blowback. This is the pressure a clumped one-number stack now lives under, and the reason a build that spreads a low and a high boiler can never be fully locked out. Naive keeps every rival's draft inattentive (random); Contested adds the last drafter's leave-behind: it takes a safe die and leaves a Still-bursting Red as the leftover Blowback — the exact threat you draft the low token to dodge. Barrel value defaults to $300 (the flat Speakeasy sale; a High Society joint pays $500). Losing the Boss changed shape in v0.8, and this default has not been re-tuned. Rise no longer promotes a Runner, so a dead Boss is body-neutral again: you place a fresh Boss in any Safe District, and if your Safehouse is off the board it goes up with him for free. The cost is pure tempo, and the tempo got dearer. Rise is now a 2-Influence Play, so a funeral eats two of your five Ledger markers, and a crew with no Boss skips The Offers that morning, so your rivals pick over the Market without you. Treat $800 as a floor and raise it to taste — at a Boss-loss rate near 8%/Day it shifts the bottom line by only tens of dollars, but it makes Twist the Valves look better than it is.

A tuning model, not the rulebook: it isolates the dawn Brew and Blowback only. Jobs, Speakeasy sales, Titles, Extort and combat all layer on top.
