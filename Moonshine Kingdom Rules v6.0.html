RULEBOOK

A Game of Bootlegging, Backstabbing, and Gang Warfare.

VERSION 6.0 — THE BOOTLEGGER'S CUT

New York City, 1926. Prohibition was supposed to dry the city out. Instead it lit a fuse. Every basement is a brewery, every back room a bank, and four mobs are clawing for the throne of the liquor trade. Bootlegging, bribery, and intimidation are your tools. Forge fragile alliances, corner the market, and turn the Police loose on your rivals. This city has a thirst that can't be quenched, and only one mob will wear its crown. Carve your name into the skyline, or vanish like cigar smoke in a hurricane.

A Day in the Life of a Kingpin

2-4 Players | 90-120 Mins

Prohibition didn't stop the drinking; it just made the price go up.

In Moonshine Kingdom, you are a rising Kingpin in 1920s New York. Prohibition has turned off the taps, creating a gold rush for anyone ruthless enough to fill the glass. Four rival mobs claw for the boroughs, and this city isn't big enough for all of you. The game is played over a series of Days. Each Day unfolds in three brutal phases: from the fog of morning to the bloody consequences of night, outmaneuver your rivals to survive.


Morning: Shadows Phase

Dawn breaks over the Hudson. Before the city wakes, the boilers burn. Fire up your Stills, stake your black-market Contracts, and turn cheap mash into liquid gold.

Day: Operations Phase

The sun is up, and so is the heat. Command your Runners to smuggle product, seize turf, and strong-arm the competition. Every move costs Influence: your political clout. Use it to expand, but remember: violence is loud, and the Police are listening.

Night: Reckoning Phase

Twilight settles like a bruise. The guns go quiet, the blood washes into the gutters, and the ledger opens. Claim your turf, cash in your Contracts, and let the Police sweep up the careless. Did you make a killing, or just a mess?

The Only Crown Worth Having

Cash buys bullets. But Respect buys immortality.

You earn Respect by conquering Boroughs, holding the most Ghettos to be named Ward Boss, and above all by pulling off Contracts. The first mob to hold a Commission Card and 20+ Respect takes the crown.

Game Components: Tools of the Trade

Your whole damn operation in miniature. Every piece earns, bleeds, or burns.

The City Map (Game Board): A soot-stained blueprint of 1920s New York, carved into 25 Districts. It includes a shared 5-space Heat Track measuring Police pressure. The city's 8 Dock Districts all connect across the water, and are where you Trade raw Moonshine for top-shelf Havana Rum. Every barrel of rum in this town came off a boat.

Operations Boards (x4): Your personal command center. A slotted board used to manage your daily action economy and cycle your Influence.

Mob Pieces (x4 Sets): Each player claims a color and commands:

  • 1 Boss, 15 Runners, and 1 Safehouse: The brains, the muscle, and the one hideout the whole operation runs out of.
  • 12 Influence Markers: The currency of the underworld. Tokens used to pay for daily actions, stake Contracts, and absorb Police Heat.
  • 1 Mob Playbook: The family secrets and your unique mob abilities.

The Ledger (Cards): The paper trail of your empire.

  • Contract Cards: High-risk, high-reward jobs that build your legend.
  • Borough Deeds (5): Proof of your dominion over the city's major territories.
  • Titles: The Ward Boss: the global crown for the boss who rules the Ghettos.
  • Shylock's Mark Cards: Debt trackers. A lifeline with teeth.
  • The Rat Card (1): The ultimate sin in cardboard form. A hot-potato mark of shame that kills your street cred and freezes you out.
  • Favor Cards: Your binding ties to the city's corrupt elite.
  • Commission Cards: Your seat on The Commission, the council that crowns kingpins.

The Stills: A boiler is printed in every District, numbered 2-12. Control the block and it starts cooking (see The Map). Hold the corner and the basement cooks.

Turn Tokens (8): Two sets of four. One set runs today's initiative (today's set); tomorrow's order is claimed from the other (tomorrow's set).

The Turn Sequence Card (1): A quick reference for the Day's phases. Its reverse holds the Town Planning Ledger, the numbered District list that sets the order Police Squads resolve Raids and breaks ties (see Police Raids).

Liquor Barrels: The lifeblood of your operation, in two flavors: Moonshine (Grey), brewed in every basement, and Havana Rum (Brown), traded for at the Docks. Cheap swill and the good stuff; nothing in between.

The Dice (10): 5 White and 5 Red. One White and a fistful of Red run the morning brew. The rest settle grudges and run blockades.

The Bankroll (Cash): Dirty, untraceable bills in denominations of $100, $500, and $1,000.

The Law (Blue Pieces): The blue wall that wants to see you swing.

  • Police Squads (4): Roaming patrols, one to a mainland Borough. An impassable wall of blue that prowls toward trouble. At setup they padlock the four High Society Speakeasies (see The Blue Wall). (Staten Island has no patrol of its own.)
  • Federal Crackdown Tracker (1): A 10-space pressure clock used in the 4-player team variant.

Key Concepts: The Underworld at a Glance

Your Mob: The Brains, the Brawn, and the Bastards

From the cold steel of the Sicilian Syndicate to the fiery grit of the Hell's Kitchen Irish, each mob brings their own brand of violence to the city's bloodstained ballet. You command:

  • A Boss: Your kingpin. The head of the snake. Bring him into a firefight to make your attacks deadlier, but if he catches a bullet, your crew is crippled until you crown a successor.
  • Runners: Your foot soldiers. Knuckle-draggers and wheelmen who haul hooch, guard your turf, and catch your bullets. You are strictly limited to 15 Runners. You cannot build a massive army to occupy the whole city. To expand, you must leave borders thin and rely on the threat of violence rather than an actual army.
  • Safehouse: Your headquarters and base of operations. You run a single Safehouse: it locks down Turf even when empty, provides a massive defensive advantage in shootouts, and anchors the block where you Recruit fresh muscle. As your empire shifts, so can your base: Secure a District you Control to pull up stakes, or take over a rival's Safehouse when you overrun their turf.

The Caps: Everything caps at 5. Brewing, Blowbacks, and Combat dice all read straight off the table. Brewing yields barrels outright; the same headcount sets your casualties and your guns.

The Map: Know Your Turf

Your empire is a broken city: 25 Districts across 5 Boroughs, each a flavor of poison and profit. Certain key Districts offer unique opportunities:

  • Speakeasies: The only spots where you can Unload Liquor for cash. Each comes in one of two grades. A Standard Speakeasy pays a flat $300 per barrel. A High Society Speakeasy, one premium joint per mainland Borough, pays a massive $500 per barrel. Sell Moonshine or Rum at either; only Rum pays a Kickback (see Unload). The booze doesn't set the price; the address does.
  • Ghettos: Gritty blocks where mob roots run deep. Hold the most at Reckoning to claim the Ward Boss card and collect Union Tax when rivals Recruit. You can also hire fresh muscle in any Ghetto you Control (see Recruit).
  • Docks: Where the rum comes ashore. At a Dock you Control you may Trade Moonshine for Havana Rum (see Operations: Trade). All Dock Districts connect across the water as if adjacent, so the waterways are the fastest road across the map.
  • Stills: Every District has a boiler printed on the board, numbered 2–12. The moment you Control the District its Still is active: it fires when its number is brewed, and risks Blowback when the leftovers match. Control is all it takes to start cooking.

The Currencies of the Underworld

To rule the city you'll need more than bullets. Two resources fuel the hustle, and a third buys power:

  • Cash: Expands your crew. Spend it to Recruit and Secure turf. Earn it by Unloading, Extorting, and completing Contracts.
  • Liquor Barrels: Your lifeblood. All hooch is born as Moonshine at your Stills; push it to a Dock and Trade it up to Havana Rum. Both sell for the same cash; only Rum buys back Influence (see Unload).

Influence: Currency of Power

Influence is your political clout. You start the game with 7 markers (gain more by buying Power Broker Favors). These markers cycle through your Operations Board:

  1. The Reserves: Uncommitted markers stack here. Use these to stake new Contracts.
  2. Fund (Shadows Phase): Move up to 5 markers from your Reserves to your Operations slots. Excess markers stay in the Reserves and cannot fund Plays today.
  3. Execute (Operations Phase): Spend markers from your Operations slots to make Plays.
  4. Resolve (After the Play): Quiet Plays return spent markers to your Reserves. If the Play generates Heat, the marker is locked onto the communal Heat Track instead.

Kingpin's Tip: Capacity vs. Time

Extra Influence does not give you more daily actions; your board is strictly capped at 5 slots. A massive Reserves simply gives you the capacity to absorb Heat, survive Police Raids, and stake multiple Contracts at once without crippling your daily hustle. You can't out-action your rivals; you have to out-think them.

Territory: Lines in the Concrete

An empire isn't built on bullets and blood alone; it's built on logistics. Moving product, and drawing borders your rivals are too terrified to cross. Because your muscle is spread thin, a cleverly placed Safehouse and a handful of guards are often enough to deter a turf war.

1. Control: Boots on the Ground

You can't tax what you don't hold. You Control a District if you have at least one Mobster (Boss or Runner) OR a Safehouse present there.

  • Instant Possession: Control shifts the moment your piece enters a District. (Moving through an unoccupied District claims it for that instant.)
  • Abandonment: A barrel of gin can't hold a street corner. If your last Mobster vacates a District and there is no Safehouse left to anchor the block, you immediately lose Control. Any Liquor left behind just waits to be stolen.
  • The Golden Rule of Turf: During the Shadows and Reckoning phases, there can be only one mob in a District. While you hold a District, rivals cannot enter without triggering combat.
  • Contested Ground: During a firefight, the original Occupier retains Control (and all defensive bonuses) until their last piece falls or retreats.

2. Safe vs. Hostile Turf

Knowing where you can move your hooch is the difference between a payday and a bloodbath.

  • Safe Districts: Any District you currently Control, plus any Defenseless District (meaning it contains no rival Mobsters and no Police Squad). Unattended Liquor and unguarded rival Safehouses do not make a District hostile; you can walk in and claim them, taking over the Safehouse for your own.
  • Hostile Districts: Any District guarded by rival Mobsters. Stepping onto Hostile turf immediately leaves your Mobsters Pinned (see Combat: Pinned).
  • The Blue Wall: A District holding a Police Squad is Impassable: no Moving, Pushing, or Attacking in, no storming the law, no bribing it. Any Speakeasy or Still the Squad sits on is padlocked (shut for everyone, held by no one for Titles) until it moves on. A padlocked Still goes completely dormant: it brews nothing and ignores Blowbacks until the cops move on. The four Squads are automated, Borough-locked patrols, one per mainland Borough, that move only during a Raid (see Police Raids). At setup they sit on the four High Society joints, padlocking the $500 trade until a Raid breaks it open.

3. Connection: The Network

Knowing the layout of the city is key to flanking enemies and bypassing blockades. Districts are Connected if:

  • Land & Bridges: They share a physical border or are linked by a Bridge.
    (e.g., Williamsburg connects to Red Hook, Brownsville, Corona, and Astoria by land, and to Five Points by Bridge).
  • Waterways (Docks): All Dock Districts connect to each other across the water as if they were adjacent.
    (e.g., You can Move directly from West Side to Throggs Neck in one Play).

Your Goal: Earning Respect

Respect is the only currency that matters in Moonshine Kingdom. Earn it two ways: claim Titles by controlling the city, and complete Contracts: jobs too loud to ignore.

1. Titles: Carving Up the City

Titles are the proof that the street belongs to you. The Borough Deeds and the Ward Boss both follow the same hard rule:

Titles aren't owned, they're held. Every Reckoning Phase the city counts heads again and asks who actually runs each kind of turf. For each Title, the boss holding a strict majority (more Districts of that type than any single rival) claims the card. Wear the crown one night and slip behind the next, and it passes straight to whoever now leads. If no single boss leads (rivals are tied at the top, or the turf has been abandoned to no one), the Title sits empty in the Supply until someone pulls ahead (see Stake Your Claim). Police do not count as rivals; Police-controlled Districts and Ghettos count as empty for Title checks.

Borough Deeds

Seizing a Borough turns muscle into empire. Hold more Districts in a Borough than any rival to claim its Deed. Boroughs are worth 3 Respect, except Staten Island at 2. Each mainland Deed also gates one Power Broker: you can only Payoff a broker while you hold the Deed that gates them (see Payoff). Staten Island gates no broker, its worth is pure, hard-won Respect.

Ward Boss

The Boroughs make headlines, but the Ghettos decide who owns the block. Hold more Ghetto Districts than any rival to claim the Ward Boss card for 3 Respect, plus the Union Tax: whenever a rival Recruits, $100 per Runner from their hiring cost goes to you instead of the bank.

2. Contracts: Forge Your Legend

A mob's reputation isn't built on promises; it's built on results. Contracts are the high-stakes jobs that turn a corner hustler into a Kingpin.

Every Contract demands a specific Objective by a merciless Deadline. They come in three escalating tiers:

  • Gigs (1 Day / 1 Respect): Quick street hustles. Keeping the lights on and the cops paid off.
  • Rackets (2 Days / 3 Respect): Mid-level capers that take turf, muscle, and a solid plan.
  • Scores (3 Days / 5 Respect): Front-page heists. Bloody legends they write ballads about.

The Ticking Clock

When you stake a Contract, you're making a promise to powerful people. Place Influence markers from your Reserves onto the card equal to the deadline (1, 2, or 3). These markers are locked up in planning. One marker returns to your Reserves each Reckoning.

During the Reckoning Phase, if you pull the final marker off the card, The Deadline has arrived. The objective is checked against the board at that exact moment; turf you held yesterday counts for nothing. Your clients don't care about excuses.

  • Completed: If you meet the objective, you deliver. Add the card to your Respect pile and collect your Take.
  • Botched: If you miss the mark, you fail. Discard the card face-up into its deck's discard pile. In this town, nobody pays for 'almost'.

The Take

Pulling off a job doesn't just buy respect; it buys bullets. When you complete a Contract, the client immediately pays out a one-time cash reward:

  • Gig: $500
  • Racket: $1,500
  • Score: $3,000

The Commission

Any street thug can knock over a Speakeasy, but building a diverse portfolio proves you run an empire. Completing a Gig, Racket, and Score earns you a Commission Card: your seat on The Commission, and a strict requirement to win the game (see Winning the Game).

Setup: Carving Out Your Corner

  1. Prepare the City:
    Unfold the Game Board and lay out the Cash and Dice. Every District's boiler number is printed right on the board, so there's nothing to seed; a Still simply runs the moment its block is Controlled.
  2. Stack the Ledger:
    Shuffle the Gig, Racket, and Score decks and place them face-down beside the board. Keep the Shylock's Mark cards close by; someone will need a loan soon. Leave all other decks in the Supply.
  3. Claim Your Crew:
    Shuffle the Playbooks and deal one to each player. Take your Operations Board and your muscle: 1 Boss, 15 Runners, and 1 Safehouse.
  4. Establish Turf:
    Nobody starts on Staten Island; return its Deed to the box. Shuffle the four remaining Borough Deeds and deal one to each player: your home turf, three Districts to garrison: 1 Safehouse, 1 Boss, and 2 Runners in your Ghetto, 3 Runners at your Dock, and 3 Runners in your Speakeasy.

    Start the Boilers: Because a Still runs the moment you Control its block, your three starting Districts are already cooking, brewing Moonshine from Day One. Every other basement waits for you to seize the corner. Hold the turf, and the hooch flows on its own.

    The Blue Wall: The law padlocks the city's richest joints. Place 1 Police Squad on each High Society Speakeasy, one per mainland Borough (4 in all). These $500 joints begin the game padlocked and impassable; the mobs must fight over the $300 scraps until a Raid scatters the cops and throws the premium spots open. Staten Island gets no Squad: the cops won't waste a wagon on a windswept rock with no speakeasy to raid.

  5. Get Your Cut:
    A rising kingpin's bankroll: take $1,500 cash and 7 Influence markers; slot 5 onto your Operations Board, drop the other 2 into your Reserves.
  6. Turn Order (Who Strikes First):
    Take one set of four Turn Tokens as today's set and randomly deal one to each player (#1 upwards) to set the morning's initiative. Keep the other set nearby as tomorrow's set; those decide the next Day's order.

    Day One, The Storm (4-Player only): In a 4-player game, a filthy sea closes the waterways on the first Day: nothing crosses open water on Day 1: no Moving or Pushing barrels between Docks, and no Skiff. Land and bridges stay open, so stake out your own streets first; the waters calm at dawn on Day 2. In 2- and 3-player games there is no storm: the water is open from Day 1. Nobody runs a blockade in a hurricane.

    In this city, the fast eat the dead. When you run out of Influence or choose to Lay Low, claim the lowest-numbered token still left in tomorrow's set. Snag #1 and you shoot first tomorrow.

Gameplay: Run the Streets

Days unfold in three phases:

Phase 1: Shadows

Dawn breaks over the Hudson. Before the city wakes, the boilers burn.

1. The Roll & The Draft

The boss holding Turn Token #1 rolls the communal pool: 1 White Die, plus Red Dice equal to Player Count + 1 (e.g., 4 players: 1 White, 5 Red). The White die is The Public Mash, the communal swill the whole city is cooking today. It stays on the table all morning.

In Turn Order, each boss drafts exactly 1 Red die. Choose carefully: the last Red die left on the table sets the Blowback, and the last boss to draft chooses a victim.

2. The Brew

Your Brew Number is simple street math: the White die plus the Red die you drafted. (e.g., White 3 + your Red 5 = a Brew Number of 8.)

Every active Still you Control matching your Brew Number fires, brewing Moonshine using The Muscle Ratio. Your brew is yours alone; rival Stills stay cold. (If the barrel supply runs dry while several of your Stills share the number, you choose which of them get the last barrels.)

3. The Blowback

Add the leftover Red (the single die no one drafted) to the White: this is the Blowback Number. Cheap pipes, high pressure, and the boilers blow. The Blowback is communal: every active, Controlled Still matching it bursts, no matter which mob owns the block.

Tally casualties in that District using The Muscle Ratio (Runners take the shrapnel first; an unprotected Boss is killed). The crew is thinned where it stands: it does not scatter, drop its barrels, or lose the block, and the Still keeps right on running for tomorrow. A Blowback weakens a crew in place, softening it for an invasion; only a Police Raid drives a crew off its turf.

4. Grease the Wheels

  • Stake Contracts: Reveal Contracts from your hand. Place Reserves markers equal to each card's Days to Deadline (1, 2, or 3). You may stake as many as you can afford. These markers are locked; they cannot fund Plays until returned at Reckoning.
  • Fund Operations: Move up to 5 markers from your Reserves into your Operations slots. Markers left in your Reserves cannot be spent on Plays today.

Example: A Morning on the Hudson

Setup: Three players. The boss holding Turn Token #1 rolls the communal pool: the White die (The Public Mash) shows 3; the four Red dice show 2, 4, 5, and 6. Every boss's Brew Number is the White 3 plus the Red they draft.

1. The Draft

  • The Irish (Token #1): They Control an active Still numbered 9, so they draft the Red 6 (White 3 + 6 = 9).
  • The Vipers (Token #2): They draft the Red 5 (White 3 + 5 = 8), matching their Still 8.
  • The Knights (Token #3): Only the 2 and the 4 remain. Their Still is numbered 7, so they draft the 4 (White 3 + 4 = 7) and leave the 2 behind.

2. The Brew

  • Irish: Still 9 fires. With 3 Mobsters in its District, they brew 2 Moonshine.
  • Vipers: Still 8 fires. With 5 Mobsters there, they brew 3 Moonshine.
  • Knights: Still 7 fires. With 2 Mobsters there, they brew 1 Moonshine.

3. The Blowback

The leftover Red 2 joins the White 3: the Blowback Number is 5. Across town, the Vipers Control an active Still numbered 5, guarded by 3 Mobsters. Cheap pipes, big bang: 2 Runners go up with the boiler (The Muscle Ratio). The survivors hold the block and the Still keeps running, but the crew is now dangerously thin.

The Vipers brewed rich at Still 8 and still lost a basement at Still 5: your Brew fires only your matching Stills, but the Blowback hunts every active Still in the city. Had the Knights drafted the 2 and left the 6, the Blowback of 9 would have gutted the Irish crew instead. The last seat picks the victim.

Kingpin's Note: The White die is public, so every Red in the pool is a Blowback waiting to happen. Sometimes the smart draft isn't the die that fires your best Still; it's the one that can't be left behind to burn it down. The first seat brews richest. The last seat holds the match.

Phase 2: Operations Phase

Operations runs in Turn Order, one Play at a time.

  1. Make a Play: Spend Influence to perform 1 Action (known as a Play): either a Standard Play (Move, Recruit, Push, etc.) or a Power Play (e.g., Unload).
  2. Combat Play: If combat is triggered, resolve it fully before the Play ends.
  3. The Cost of Doing Business: Remove the spent marker(s) from your Operations and return them to your Reserves. If a Play generates Heat, place 1 spent marker onto the Heat Track. Any remaining spent markers return to your Reserves.
  4. Laying Low: When you run out of Influence (or choose to step off the street early), you must Lay Low:
    1. Clear any remaining markers from your Operations to your Reserves.
    2. Claim the lowest-numbered token left in tomorrow's set (claim #1 to guarantee you strike first tomorrow).
Standard Plays (Cost: 1 Influence)
Move Move any number of Mobsters (carrying max 1 barrel each) to one Connected District. Entering rival turf immediately leaves your Mobsters Pinned, and the Occupier gets a Standoff choice first. You cannot Move into a District held by a Police Squad; the Blue Wall is impassable.
Push Redistribute any number of barrels back and forth between a District you Control and one Connected District, in a single Play. Use it to reorganize your own supply lines, or to swap inventory with a rival (with their permission); barrels can flow both ways at once. Any barrels you Push into a rival's District immediately become their property. Thanks for the donation. You cannot Push barrels into a Police Squad's District.
Recruit Hire Runners for $300 per Runner, in any Ghetto you Control or in the District holding your Safehouse. (Union Tax: If a rival holds the Ward Boss card, you must hand them a $100 cut for every Runner hired).
Hustle Boss must be in play. Draw 2 Contracts, then discard down to your Hand Limit of 3. If a Contract deck ever runs dry, immediately shuffle its face-up discard pile to form a new face-down draw deck. Limit: once per Day.
Secure Move your operation for $500. Relocate your Safehouse to a District you Control, pulling it off its previous block. You only run one hideout, so Securing new turf means abandoning the old.
Trade At a Dock District you Control, swap any number of Moonshine barrels sitting in that District for Havana Rum, at a 1:1 ratio. A quiet dockside exchange, and the only way Rum enters the game. Rum sells for the same cash as Moonshine but pays a Kickback when Unloaded; that is the whole reason to trade up.
Extort Shake down the locals. Once per Day, earn $100 per District you Control anywhere on the map. Earning $500+ (controlling 5 or more Districts) is noisy and generates Heat.

Texas Guinan's Favor sweetens the Hustle: draw 3 Contracts instead of 2, and your Hand Limit rises to 5 (see Favors).

Power Plays

These Plays cost two Influence markers to perform, but count as a single Play in the turn cycle.

Power Plays (Cost: 2 Influence)
Unload The Address Sets the Price: At a Speakeasy you Control, sell Moonshine or Rum for the same cash. The price is the joint, not the bottle: a flat $300 per barrel at a Standard Speakeasy, a massive $500 per barrel at a High Society joint. Cheap swill in a swanky room still drinks like champagne.

The Kickback: The difference is in the favors. Moonshine pays cash only. Havana Rum pays cash plus 1 Kickback per barrel: for every barrel of Rum sold, immediately move 1 spent Influence marker from your Reserves back into an empty slot on your Operations Board. (Your board caps at 5 slots; any Kickbacks beyond your empty slots are wasted.)

The Greed Tax: Unloading 4+ barrels of any type in a single Play generates Heat. If this Unload was paid for with two different players' markers, you choose which spent marker goes to the Heat Track. Max safe delivery is 3, unless you hold Stephanie St. Clair's Favor.
Payoff The right friends don't come cheap, and you can only shake one hand at a time. Put a Power Broker on your payroll. Every broker answers to one mainland Borough, and you must hold that Borough's Deed at the moment you Payoff to court them (each broker's Borough is named on their Favor Card and printed on its Deed). Pay the bank their fee, take their Favor Card, and add 1 permanent Influence marker to your Reserves (see Favors). Each broker can be bought only once per game by each player.

The Deed is a transient toll, not rent: hold the Borough just long enough to buy the broker, and the Favor is yours for good even if you lose that turf the very next minute. Staten Island gates no broker.
Rat There's no honor among thieves when the sirens wail. Drop a dime on your rivals: as long as there is at least 1 marker on the Heat Track, trigger an immediate Police Raid. Every Squad prowls toward the freshest Heat it can reach, so whoever fired the last loud shot bleeds worst (see Police Raids).

The Mark of the Snitch: Ratting is the ultimate street sin. When you make this Play, you immediately take the Rat Card from the supply (or snatch it from the rival currently holding it). You may make this Play even if you already hold the Rat Card, just to watch your rivals burn. While you hold this card, you are a pariah; nobody will do business with you, meaning you cannot perform the Hustle Play. Only a snitch carries the Mark: when a Raid boils over on its own, nobody takes the blame and this card stays in the supply.
Rise Boss in the morgue? Time to anoint a successor. Only available while your Boss is off the board; a mob never has more than one Boss. Appoint a new Boss ($0) in any District you Control, or any District containing no rival Mobsters or Safehouses (unattended Liquor and Stills don't count), and immediately spawn 2 free Runners with them. You may also instantly Secure this District for free by relocating your Safehouse there.

Mob Signature Plays

Every crew has tricks up its sleeve: signature moves to outfox, out-muscle, or outrun the competition. Each mob has two unique plays.

Sicilian Syndicate

Old blood, older rules. An insult to the family is a death sentence.

Hit (Cost: 2) The family doesn't fight foot soldiers; it goes for the head. While Pinned in a rival District, initiate a special Open Fire. Both sides roll simultaneously as normal. However, if you score any hits, the rival Boss takes the first hit instead of a Runner. Generates Heat if you kill.
Consigliere (Cost: 1) Use your connections to smooth over legal troubles. Remove 1 Influence marker of your choice from the Heat Track and return it to its owner's Reserves (move other markers down to fill gaps if required).

Hell's Kitchen Irish

They brew it strong and fight dirty. Why buy bullets when a brick works just fine?

Courage (Cost: 0) Before you roll dice for an Ambush or for an Open Fire that you initiated, you may discard 1 of your Liquor barrels located in that District to gain +1 Threat for that roll. You cannot use Courage to boost your defense when a rival opens fire on you.
Plunder (Cost: 1) Initiate a special attack. For each successful hit, instead of eliminating a Mobster, steal 1 Liquor barrel of your choice from the target District. You can carry these away if you Advance. Occupier hits still kill. You may also choose to Plunder when you Ambush (Cost: 0). Plunder does not create Heat.

East Side Vipers

They move like smoke and strike like snakes. You won't know they're there until the shots come from the shadows.

Stealth (Cost: 1) Move up to 5 Mobsters (no Liquor) into a Connected rival District without triggering a Standoff (the Occupier cannot Ambush or Fold). You cannot end your Play in this state. You must immediately spend another Influence to either Open Fire on the District, or Advance to a Connected Safe District.
Firepower Pay a flat $200 when you Open Fire or Ambush to roll exactly one additional attack die (up to a maximum of 5). You cannot pay extra to buy multiple dice. Cannot be used when defending Open Fire.

Harlem Knights

Dressed to kill, literally. The Knights hit from the water and leave warehouses burning.

Skiff (Cost: 1) Move up to 5 Mobsters (with or without Liquor) from a Coastal District you Control to any other Coastal District. A Coastal District is any District touching water, so this reaches far beyond the Docks. If you enter a rival District, the standard Standoff applies (the Occupier may Ambush, Hold Fire, or Fold). Skiff is a Play on your own turn, not an escape hatch: you cannot use it to flee combat or a Police Raid. Folding, Advancing, Falling Back, or running from the law all move you on foot to a Connected District like everyone else.
Torch (Cost: 2) Burn down a Safehouse in a rival District. To perform this Play, you must have at least one Runner Pinned in the target District. Sacrifice one of those Pinned Runners; he strikes the match and goes up with the flames. He is removed from the game. The rival's Safehouse is immediately destroyed (return it to their supply). Any rival Mobsters and Liquor in the District are unharmed and retain Control. This Play generates Heat.

Phase 3: Reckoning Phase

Once the guns go quiet, the city settles its accounts. Each Reckoning stage resolves simultaneously for all players.

1. The Sweep

The cops don't mind a hustle, but they hate an army.
The Police sweep the streets. In any District with more than 5 Mobsters, remove excess Runners to bring the count down to 5, unless you hold Mayor Jimmy Walker's Favor.

2. Stake Your Claim

Titles prove you run the streets, and the streets are re-counted every night. Re-evaluate every Title (the Borough Deeds and the Ward Boss) against the board exactly as it stands now. Yesterday's holdings count for nothing.

  • The Sole Leader Holds It: For each Title, whoever holds a strict majority of that turf (more of it than any single rival) takes or keeps the card. Nothing else matters: a boss who has pulled ahead claims the crown outright, and a former owner who has fallen behind hands it over.
  • Or Nobody Holds It: If no single boss leads (two or more rivals are tied for the most, or the turf sits empty), the Title returns to (or stays in) the Supply. A deadlock crowns no one until someone breaks it.

Police do not count as rivals; Police-controlled Districts and Ghettos count as empty for every Title check.

3. Settle Accounts

Remove 1 marker from each of your active Contracts and return it to your Reserves. Contract objectives are checked only now, after the Sweep and Title checks resolve.

  • The Deadline: If you removed the final marker, the deadline has arrived. You cannot back out of a promised job. If your current board state meets the objective, the Contract is Completed (add it to your Respect pile and collect the Take). If you missed the objective, the Contract is Botched (discard it face-up into its deck's discard pile for nothing). In this town, nobody pays for 'almost'.
  • Commission Check: If a newly completed Contract secures you a full portfolio (1 Gig, 1 Racket, and 1 Score), immediately take a Commission Card.

The Next Day...

If any Kingpin holds a Commission Card and 20+ Respect, the game ends immediately! (see Winning the Game).

If not, the war continues. The Turn Tokens you claimed tonight become today's set, the other set becomes tomorrow's, and the next Day's Shadows Phase begins in the new Turn Order.

Winning the Game: The Final Reckoning

The war for New York ends during the Reckoning Phase, the night a player holds a Commission Card (earned by completing a Gig, Racket, and Score) and 20+ Respect. Victory is checked only at Reckoning; a crown seized at noon can still slip away before nightfall.

1. A Seat on The Commission

Any thug can fire a Tommy gun, but it takes a true mastermind to earn a seat on The Commission. When a newly completed Contract secures you a full portfolio (1 Gig, 1 Racket, and 1 Score), immediately take a Commission Card. A player may only ever claim one.

2. Earning Respect

Legitimacy means nothing if the streets don't fear you. You need 20 Respect to claim the crown. To calculate your Respect, tally the spoils of your empire:

  • Borough Turf: Gain 3 Respect for each Borough Deed you hold (2 Respect for Staten Island).
  • Ward Boss: Gain 3 Respect if you hold the Ward Boss card at the end of the game.
  • Criminal Legends: Gain 1, 3, or 5 Respect for each completed Contract in your ledger. Your turf and your ledger of jobs are the surest roads to 20.
  • Blood Debts: Subtract 1 Respect for every unpaid Loan. (Shylock always gets his due).
  • The Squealer's Grave: Subtract 3 Respect if you are stuck holding the Rat Card at the end of the game. (Nobody crowns a rat).

The Final Standoff

If multiple bosses hold a Commission Card and 20+ Respect on the same night, there's no war left to fight; The Commission simply opens the books and weighs one empire against the other:

  1. Total Respect: The highest Respect takes the crown.
  2. Cold Hard Cash: If deadlocked, the Kingpin with the fattest bankroll buys the throne (include the loose change printed on your Playbook).

There are no shared victories in Moonshine Kingdom. You either rule the city alone, or bleed out in the gutter.

Favors: Friends in High Places

The Power Brokers: Loyalty on Tap

Money talks, but the right friends scream. These brokers don't carry guns; they carry the city.

Every broker has a price, and in this city everyone's for sale. Buying a Favor is the Payoff Power Play (see Power Plays): spend the 2 Influence, hold the Borough Deed that gates the broker (named in each entry below), and pay the bank their fee. Take their Favor Card and add 1 permanent Influence marker to your Reserves. A Favor is a pure engine piece: it earns no Respect on its own, so Respect rewards holding the map, not just passing through it. Courting a broker takes a real sit-down; you can buy one a turn, not the whole council at once.

Honor Among Thieves: Each broker can only be bought once per game by each player. Once their Favor is yours, their loyalty (and the Influence marker) stays with you for the rest of the game, unless you break a binding deal (see Cooperation).

Repay $1,000 to the bank at any time to immediately return the marker to your Reserves. No interest, no Shylock, no questions; the marker just sits in his vault until you square up.
  • Texas Guinan – "The Hostess"

    "Hello, Sucker!" Judges, senators, and stars all drink at Texas Guinan's table.

  • Mayor Jimmy Walker – "The Night Mayor"

  • Stephanie St. Clair – "Queenie"

Shylock's Mark

When the walls close in, Shylock offers a lifeline made of barbed wire. Take his cash to survive today, but the debt will bleed you tomorrow. "My bond cries out for its pound of flesh."

  • The Loan: At any time before the endgame triggers, you may take a Shylock's Mark card to gain $1,500. You may hold multiple marks.
  • Paying the Piper: To clear the debt, pay $3,000 to the bank at any time before the game ends (double what you borrowed), then discard the card. Shylock's interest is simple: he takes back twice what he gave.
  • The Toll: Shylock always gets his due. Every unpaid Mark in your ledger at the final Reckoning costs you -1 Respect.

Cooperation: Puppets in the Smoke

Trust is fleeting, but even sharp kingpins need rivals. Every favor granted might be a knife in the back.

Pulling the Strings (Puppeteering)

At any time during Operations, you may move Influence markers from your Operations to a rival's Operations (up to their 5 marker cap). This is not a Play and does not end your turn.

  • The Favor: You lend your name and network. The rival may use these markers as their own, now or later.
  • The Risk: If the rival spends your marker on a Play that generates Heat, your marker goes to the Heat Track. Otherwise, it returns to your Reserves once used.

The Broker's Word: A Binding Handshake

A Mobster's word is cheap. When a deal needs teeth, have a Power Broker vouch for you.

Making the Deal

To make a binding deal, a player must have at least one Favor Card.

  1. State the Terms & Deadline: The terms must be clear, and you must agree on a specific deadline (e.g., "before the Day ends," or "until you pay me $1,000").
  2. Stake the Favor: The player stakes an available Favor Card, placing it between both players. Each Broker may only vouch for one deal at a time.

Example: "The Mayor vouches for me. If you let me pass through your Borough unharmed, I promise to give you $1,000 before Day's end."

Honoring & Breaking Deals

Deals resolve the moment their conditions are met or failed.

  • Deal Honored: The moment the terms are met, the deal is complete. The Favor Card is immediately returned to its owner.
  • Deal Broken: If the agreed deadline passes without the terms being met, or if a player violates a standing agreement for any reason (including third-party interference), the Broker's trust is shattered and your word becomes garbage. The owner must discard the Favor Card and lose its permanent Influence marker.

Deals on the Side

You may wheel and deal outside the rules, but only Cash can be physically traded off the board. Hand it over anytime, no Influence required.

Barrel Exchanges: Liquor stays on the board. To swap barrels with a rival, use a single Push Play: with their permission, you can shift any barrels either way between your District and a Connected one, clearing the whole trade in one Play with no return turn needed.

Combat Rules: Blood on the Streets

Violence isn't a backup plan; it's the cost of doing business. Smart Kingpins know a drawn gun can be stronger than a fired one. When talk fails, step inside and clear the room.

1. The Rules of Engagement

The mechanics of violence rely on two factors: The Muscle Ratio (how many dice you roll, capped at 5 in Combat) and Threat (what number kills).

Calculating Threat

Your Base Threat is Level 1 (Kill on a 5+). It improves with bonuses, which stack up to Level 4. Each is a plain +1 or +2 Threat:

  • Boss +1 Threat — if your Boss is in the fight.
  • Safehouse +2 Threat — the Occupier defending Open Fire on their own Secured District (not on the Ambush shot).
  • Ambush +1 Threat — the Occupier's first shot, on the opening Ambush only.

2. The Standoff: Crossing the Line

Every firefight is just two questions, asked in order:

  • Step 1 — the Occupier answers: Ambush, Hold Fire, or Fold.
  • Step 2 — the Invader chooses (and keeps choosing until the Pin is resolved): Open Fire, Advance, or Fall Back.

When an opponent Moves into a District you Control, the game pauses on Step 1. As the Occupier, you must immediately choose one of three responses:

  1. Ambush: You greet the invaders with lead.
    • Free Shot: Only the Occupier rolls dice.
    • Bonus: Gain +1 Threat.
    • Casualties: Hits remove Invaders (Runners first). No Heat. Invaders are now Pinned.
  2. Hold Fire: You stand your ground but don't shoot. Maybe they are just passing through? The Invaders are now Pinned.
  3. Fold (Cost: 0): You surrender the District. You may immediately move your Mobsters to one Connected Safe District.
    • Panic: Fleeing Mobsters cannot carry Liquor; any barrels are left behind. You also cannot pack up a building; your Safehouse remains.
    • The Takeover: The Invader immediately claims Control, captures dropped Liquor, and may take over or destroy your Safehouse. The Invaders are not Pinned; their Play ends.

3. Pinned: Fight or Flight (Step 2)

While Pinned, your Mobsters cannot make normal Plays. As the Invader you must resolve the standoff before your turn moves on: spend Influence to Open Fire or Advance, or Fall Back for free. Keep choosing until the Pin is resolved, or you run dry and must Fall Back. Torch, Plunder, Courage, and Firepower still work while Pinned.

The Cost of Pride: A real Kingpin knows an all-out firefight is bad business. Burning 3 or 4 Influence to keep opening fire on one stubborn alley may win it, but it leaves rivals free across the city. Talk is cheap; bullets cost time.

Open Fire (Cost: 1) Fight for the District. Both sides roll simultaneously.
  • Casualties: Both sides apply hits (Runners first). The Occupier loses the Ambush bonus but keeps Safehouse/Boss bonuses.
  • Heat: If the Invader kills, they gain Heat . The Occupier never draws Heat in combat; the law doesn't blame a man for defending his own corner.
  • Repeat: If both sides stand, remain Pinned. Spend another Influence to Open Fire again.
Advance (Cost: 1) Shoot your way out. Move Pinned Mobsters (and up to 1 barrel each) to any Safe Connected District, including across water via Docks. You cannot enter hostile turf.
Fall Back (Cost: 0)
(Invader Only)		 Cut and run. Retreat to the origin District you entered from. Carried Liquor is left behind for the Occupier.
If you have 0 Influence remaining, you must Fall Back.

4. Victory & Consequences

Combat ends when one side is eliminated or the Invader retreats.

If the Occupier Holds: The District remains theirs. Liquor dropped by dead Invaders becomes the Occupier's property.

If the Invader Wins: The moment the last of the Occupier's pieces falls or flees, you claim Control of the District.

  • The Spoils: Any Liquor in the District is now yours.
  • Safehouses: A rival Safehouse in the District is destroyed (remove it), unless you take it over instead: immediately Secure the District for free by relocating your Safehouse there from wherever it sat before. Note: You may also take over a Defenseless Safehouse by Moving into its District.

Heat: Playing with Fire

Heat measures how hard the law is breathing down your neck. The Heat Track is a 5-space powder keg: when you generate Heat, place one Influence marker on it. Played right, it's a weapon; played wrong, it's a noose.

Drawing Heat

These noisy Plays push the track up one notch (any Play marked with the flame icon ):

  • Blood on the Pavement: If an Invader inflicts 1+ kills in combat, they add 1 marker at the end of the Play. A multi-round firefight generates Heat only once; the Occupier never draws Heat for defending their own corner.
  • Shakedowns Get Loud: An Extort Play earning $500+ (5 or more Districts) draws Heat .
  • The Greed Tax: Unloading 4+ barrels in a single Play draws Heat .

Police Raids: The Boiling Point

A Raid triggers two ways: the 5th marker lands on the track naturally, or a rival plays the Rat Card. Either way, pause the turn; the city's on lockdown.

A Raid puts every Squad on the move. Resolve them one at a time, in Town Planning Ledger order (the numbered District list on the back of the Turn Sequence card), each finishing before the next stirs. Four precincts kicking doors in, block by block.

1. The Most Wanted List

A Squad looks only at the Districts directly Connected to it within its own Borough, and chases the freshest gunshot it can hear:

  1. Follow the Smoke: Among those rivals, the Squad steps toward whoever's marker sits furthest right on the Heat Track. Quiet money always walks free.
  2. The Big Bust: If that rival holds more than one of those Districts, the Squad kicks in the one with the most Barrels (the cops follow the contraband). Tied? It hits whichever sits first on the Ledger.
  3. Cold Trail: If no reachable District carries Heat, the Squad does not move and holds its corner.

2. The Scatter

The Squad takes one step onto the chosen District and kicks the doors in:

  • Condemned: Any Safehouse there is destroyed and all Liquor seized (both to supply).
  • Run For It: The crew bolts to a single Connected Safe District: never back into the one the Squad advanced from, and no grabbing barrels on the way out.
  • Cornered: With no Connected Safe District to flee to (hemmed in by rivals, water, the Blue Wall, the city's edge, or only the road the cops came in on), the whole crew is arrested. Leave a back door, or don't leave at all.

The Squad now holds the District as an impassable wall, padlocking any Speakeasy or Still beneath it until a later Raid. A crew fleeing one Squad may land in the path of the next; the night is young.

The Aftermath: Once every Squad has moved, all Heat markers return to their owners' Reserves and the paused turn resumes.

The Blood Oath: 4-Player Team Variant

When the heat boils over, enemies become brothers. The Feds are kicking down doors and lone wolves are slaughtered. The Blood Oath is the recommended 4-player mode: a cutthroat free-for-all where escalating police pressure eventually forces the city into two permanent 2v2 alliances.

Setup: The Ticking Clock

Set up as a standard 4-player match, with one addition: place the Federal Crackdown Tracker near the Heat Track with a token on space 1. Every time a Police Raid triggers, advance the token one space.

The Sit-Down

The moment the token reaches space 10 on the Crackdown Tracker, the underworld snaps. Pause the game. The four independent mobs immediately form two permanent alliances.

  1. Rank the Bosses: Tally everyone's current Respect (scoring held Titles and completed Contracts). Tiebreaker: Total Cash on hand.
  2. Forge the Families: The player in 1st Place joins forces with the player in 4th Place. The player in 2nd Place pairs with the player in 3rd Place.

New Rules of Engagement

One goal, two crews. You still run your own operations.

  • Separate Ledgers: You do not share Cash, Influence, Contracts, or Hand Limits, and you still take individual turns in Turn Order.
  • Blood is Thicker: You cannot Ambush or Open Fire on your partner. All other rules stand: the Golden Rule of Turf means you cannot share a District with them at Day's end, and Liquor Pushed into their District becomes theirs.
  • Puppeteering: Your strongest weapon. You may freely lend Influence to your partner's Operations Board to fund their turn (standard Heat rules apply).

Claiming the Empire

The Commission only recognizes unified power. To win, an Alliance must collectively hold at least 1 Commission Card and reach a Combined Total of 35 Respect. If met during the Reckoning Phase, the game ends immediately.

The New Kings of New York

When the game ends, tally the final Respect for all four players individually (applying Title points, Contract Respect, and Shylock debt penalties). The Alliance with the highest Combined Respect wins the war.

The Capo di Tutti Capi: There are no truly shared victories... even in a team. Within the winning Alliance, the player who contributed the most Respect to the final combined total is declared the Boss of Bosses and the true Kingpin of New York. Your partner doesn't get a crown; they just get the privilege of kissing your ring.
