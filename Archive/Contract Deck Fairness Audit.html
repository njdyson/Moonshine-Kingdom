Contract Deck — Fairness Audit

Does the deck reward the board evenly, or does it hide an edge for players who’ve memorised it?

Verdict: the deck is not board-fair. Speakeasies are well covered — the real gaps are a Ward drought and a lopsided borough spread (Manhattan & Queens rich, the Bronx thin). Companion to the Contract Card Review; live deck = 52 contracts (24 Gig / 16 Racket / 12 Score), target 20 / 16 / 12.

The principle being tested

A contract deck should read as a fair map of the board. If it systematically prizes some turf over others, a player who has learned the deck gains an edge invisible to a newcomer — they grab the turf they know pays, while the table can’t see why they’re winning. Even coverage keeps the hidden information honest. Measured that way, “is the deck Speakeasy-heavy?” is the wrong question. The right one is: does every district-type and every borough get its fair share of objectives?

What the deck already gets right

✓
Speakeasies are not the problemThe stated worry — “dive bars are dead turf” — isn’t a contract problem. Speakeasies are the best-covered type on the board (King of Queens, The Cartel, Sullivan, plus 3 benched speak-count cards held in reserve). The v0.65 pass even thinned a “speak-count glut.” Adding more would re-create it.
✓
The symmetric-set instinct is already in the deckYour “King of X for every borough” idea matches an existing design language: the Every-borough ladder (Ghosts in the Shadows → The Five Families) and the Dock-rum ladder (Regatta / Quiet Drop → Cuban Prince → Dock Domination). Board-symmetric sets are a tool the deck already uses — just not yet for Wards.
!
The deck is 4 Gigs over target24 live Gigs against a target of 20. That’s swap budget — we can fix the Ward/Bronx gaps by replacing weak or duplicated Gigs, not only by adding, so the deck lands back on its clean 20 / 16 / 12 print count.

Rebalance plan — toward a board-fair deck

Fix the two real leaks (Ward drought, Bronx thinness) using the Gig overage as budget. Nothing on the Review’s “leave alone” list is touched. Net count returns to 20 / 16 / 12.

Add
The Ward HeelerGig — Control 2+ Ward Districts holding 2+ Moonshine each.
A cheap, early reason to hold Wards — the tier the drought is worst at. Distinct from Union Dues (cash) and the benched Tenement Army (raw count) by asking for product on the turf. Lifts Ward coverage 0.40 → 0.60.
Add
Boss of the BronxRacket — Control 3+ Districts in the Bronx.
Directly answers the borough outlier — gives the Bronx a home objective the way Harlem Shakedown does for Manhattan. Borough-anchored, mid-tier, no turf-type overlap.
Swap
Retire Black Hand RisingGig (Review flags it a “subset of Five Families”) to make room for The Ward Heeler.
Removes a near-duplicate, funds the Ward add, moves toward the 20-Gig target. Keep it on the bench (revivable).
Swap
Bench one of the 3 surplus Gigs flagged in the Review to hit 20 exactly — candidate: a second Heat-only or Cash-only Gig (Frame Job / Rat’s Nest / Lexington overlap).
Trims the over-count without touching turf coverage. Pick the least distinctive per the Review’s ratings.
Hold
Do NOT add more Speakeasy cards.
Already at 1.0/district and a glut was deliberately thinned. If dive bars still feel dead in play, the fix is the Night Mayor Title or a turf perk — not more contracts.
Option
The full “King of X” borough-symmetric set (Manhattan / Bronx / Brooklyn to match King of Queens)
Tempting for symmetry, but it would push Speakeasy coverage well past 1.0 and re-glut the speak-count cluster. Only pursue if you convert the archetype away from Speakeasies — e.g. “Control all Districts of one type in Borough X” — so each borough card rewards different turf. Parked as a bigger redesign, not this pass.
