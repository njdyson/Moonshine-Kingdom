A Game of Bootlegging, Backstabbing, and Gang Warfare.

New York City, 1929. Prohibition has turned the gaslit streets into a lawless gold rush, and every block is worth killing for. Only one boss will wear the crown. Will you carve your name into the annals of infamy, or vanish like cigar smoke in a hurricane?

Setting the Scene: New York, 1929

3–4 Players

The Dry Years

Since 1920 the country has been dry, and New York has never been thirstier. Liquor is illegal, so it is everywhere: cooked in tenement attics and dockside cellars, run ashore by the boats of Rum Row, and poured in a thousand back rooms behind a peephole and a password. The money is beyond counting, and the money buys everything: the beat cop, the precinct captain, the alderman, the judge. In this town the law isn't broken. It's rented.

The Man Who Kept the Peace

For most of the decade one man held it all together. Arnold Rothstein, the Brain, bankrolled the rum boats, paid off the politicians, fixed the World Series (or so they say) and settled every quarrel before it came to gunfire. Crossing him was bad for business, so nobody did. Last November somebody shot him in a hotel room over a card debt, and the peace went into the ground with him.

Four Families

Now four crews are carving up what he left behind, and every one of them is sure it's next in line:

  • The Sicilian Syndicate: Old blood and older rules. An insult to the family is a death sentence.
  • The Hell's Kitchen Irish: They brew it strong, fight dirty and always come heavy.
  • The East Side Vipers: They move like smoke, and nothing happens in this city that they don't overhear first.
  • The Harlem Knights: Dressed to kill, and every pier from Havana to Harlem is a door they can come through.

The Crown in the Gutter

Since November the crown of this town has lain in the gutter. The families at the Commission, the table where they settle their business, will accept a new man, but on their terms: he has to have made a name, and he has to claim it standing in another family's home Ward, the tenement block where that family was born. Until then it's open season, and every bar, pier and back room from Five Points to Fordham is up for grabs.

You hauled crates for another man once. Now the crates are yours, and so are the dozen men who guard them. This is Moonshine Kingdom: a city of flickering neon and flickering loyalties, where empires rise between dusk and dawn.

Game Components: Tools of the Trade

Your whole damn operation in miniature. Every piece earns, bleeds, or burns.

The City Map (Game Board): New York, 1929, drawn in gilt and lacquer: 24 Districts across 5 Boroughs, with a boiler on every block. A shared Heat Track measures how badly the city wants you gone, and the Tomorrow panel holds the Mash die and tomorrow's Turn Tokens.

The Ledger (x4): Your back-room desk. A slotted player board where the day's Influence sits, gets spent, and comes back. Every move you make starts here.

Mob Pieces (x4 Sets): One color to a mob: take the set matching the Playbook you're dealt. Each holds 1 Boss and 12 Runners (the brains and the muscle), 1 Safehouse, 10 Influence Markers, and 1 Mob Playbook of dirty tricks only your family can pull.

The Cards: The paper trail of your empire.

  • Job Cards (36): Dirty work with your name on it. Pull one off and the city remembers.
  • Bribe Cards (16): The men on your payroll, from the Precinct Captain to the Mayor.
  • Borough Cards (4): Your home turf, and the Ward your rivals must take to wear the crown.
  • Shylock's Marks (7): Debt, in writing. A lifeline with teeth.
  • Rat Cards (12): A squeal or a broken word, kept on file. Nobody deals for one; you earn it the hard way.

Turn Tokens (8): Two sets numbered 1–4. One set runs today; the other decides tomorrow.

The Town Planner (1): City Hall's own paperwork: the District roster you carve the map up from at setup. Its reverse holds a quick reference for the Day's phases.

Liquor Barrels (x30 Moonshine, x20 Havana Rum): The lifeblood of your operation. Barrels cycle back to the Supply when sold or Traded away, and the Supply is the ceiling: when Moonshine or Rum runs out, it's out. Cheap swill and the good stuff; nothing in between.

The Dice (11): 5 White, 5 Red, and 1 grey Mash die. Red fires the boilers and the Invader's guns, White the Occupier's; the lone Mash die does one job only, and the whole table watches it.

The Bankroll (Cash): Dirty, untraceable bills in denominations of $100, $500, and $1,000. The kind that never sees a bank.

Police Squads (2 Blue Pieces, numbered 1 and 2): The blue wall that wants to see you swing. Two patrols prowling the mainland, the North Squad (1) and the South Squad (2), drawn to noise.

The Blood Oath Card (1) (4-Player Team Variant only): It wears a Job's back and hides in the Jobs deck. Leave it in the box for a standard game.

Key Concepts: The Underworld at a Glance

Your Mob: The Brains, the Brawn, and the Bastards

From the Sicilian Syndicate to the Hell's Kitchen Irish, every family kills in its own accent. You command:

A Boss The head of the snake. In a firefight he makes every shot deadlier; when the Still in his District fires, a second Still runs with it (Split the Batch). Let him catch a bullet and the family is headless: no Bribes, no second Still and no crown until a successor Rises.
Runners Knuckle-draggers and hard cases who hold turf, ride shotgun on the hooch, and catch bullets. You get a dozen and no more, never enough to cover the city. So expand anyway: leave the borders thin and let the threat of violence stand guard. A man shot or arrested goes back to your supply, to be Recruited again. There's always another kid off the boat who'll stand on a corner for a dollar.
Safehouse Your one headquarters, and the only District where you Recruit. It holds no turf by itself: leave it bare and anyone can walk in. Move it by Securing a new District, or into a rival's HQ the moment you overrun it.

The Map: Know Your Turf

Your empire is a broken city: 24 Districts across 5 Boroughs, each a flavor of poison and profit. Manhattan is glitz and grit, the beating heart. The Bronx is smoke, steel and old grudges. Queens has big houses and bigger secrets, Brooklyn sweat and swinging fists, and Staten Island lies quiet, crooked and off the map. Certain key Districts offer unique opportunities:

Speakeasies Where the city drinks, and where liquor turns to cash. The 8 Speakeasies are where you Unload: Control one, haul your barrels in, and sell them across the bar. The more of them you hold, the shorter the road from Still to sale.
High Society Venues Four Speakeasies, one to each mainland Borough and each above a #7 Still, where the judges and aldermen do their drinking. They buy Havana Rum and nothing else, and every barrel of it poured there pays a Kickback in Influence on top of its price (see Unload). Rival bosses will want them too.
Wards Tenement blocks far from the glitz, where mob power really lives. Each family is born in its own home Ward, and that is where its rivals must come to Take the Crown.
Docks The ten Docks, two in every Borough, connect to one another across the water, the fastest road on the map. The boss Controlling the most Docks is the Harbormaster, and names tomorrow's Mash. At any Dock you Control, Trade Moonshine for Havana Rum, one for one: the contact boats run it straight in from Rum Row, the mother ships offshore.
Precincts East Harlem and Corona, the station houses: no Still and no business. A Police Squad starts on each, and comes home to it when it has nobody to chase (see Police Raids).
Stills Every District but the two Precincts has a boiler printed on the board, numbered 2–12, and a gauge showing its Pressure: 6 on the 7, one less for each step away, down to 1 on the 2 and the 12. The gauge lights in three colours, gold, orange and red, and the colour tells you how many die when it bursts: 1, 2 or 3. The moment you Control the District its Still is active: it pours when you brew its number, and bursts when the morning's Blowback matches it (see Shadows).

The Currencies of the Underworld

To rule the city you'll need more than bullets. Two resources fuel the hustle, and a third buys power:

  • Cash: Buys men, buildings, and judges: spend it to Recruit, to Secure a Safehouse, and to Bribe. Earn it by Unloading barrels, Extorting your turf, and cashing in unspent Influence at $100 a marker. Keep it on the table in front of you, in plain sight: anyone may ask for a count.
  • Liquor Barrels: Your lifeblood. Every drop is born as Moonshine at your Stills, and any ordinary Speakeasy you Control buys it at $200 a barrel. Traded up to Havana Rum at a Dock it fetches $500, and the four High Society Venues buy Rum alone.

Influence: Currency of Power

Influence is your political clout: the favors, phone calls and friendly cops that let a mob operate at all. Run dry and the whole racket grinds to a halt. You start with 6 markers and Bribe for more, one at a time, to a hard ceiling of 10 (see Buy the City). Day to day, those markers cycle:

  1. The Reserves: Where uncommitted markers stack, and where you stake Jobs from.
  2. Fund (Shadows): Fill your Ledger's 5 slots from the Reserves. Only Ledger markers can be spent.
  3. Execute (The Hustle): Spend Ledger markers on your Plays, and on the paid choices in a firefight (an Ambush included).
  4. Resolve: A quiet spend returns its marker to your Reserves. A noisy one locks it onto the communal Heat Track instead, until the next Police Raid sets it free.

Kingpin's Tip: Capacity, Not Actions

However deep your Reserves, the Ledger funds 5 markers a Day, so extra Influence doesn't buy extra Plays. It buys capacity: markers to stake Jobs, and enough to fill tomorrow's five after the Heat Track keeps a few. You can't out-action your rivals. You have to out-think them.

Territory: Lines in the Concrete

An empire isn't built on bullets alone. It's built on logistics: moving product, and drawing borders your rivals won't cross.

1. Control: Boots on the Ground

You can't tax what you don't hold. You Control a District if you have a Mobster (Boss or Runner) standing on it. A Safehouse alone holds nothing: a District with no Mobsters is nobody's.

  • Walk In, Walk Out: Control is settled at the end of each Play. Walk onto a District nobody Controls and it is yours; walk off it on a later Play and it isn't.
  • Barrels Hold Nothing: When a Play ends, every barrel belongs to whoever Controls the District it sits in. In a District nobody Controls they are nobody's: whoever takes it next takes them. A barrel of gin can't hold a street corner.
  • The Golden Rule of Turf: At the end of every Play, only one mob stands in a District. You may enter a rival's turf, but by the time your Play ends you must have taken the District or left it.
  • A Rival Safehouse: Take Control of a District holding one and it can't stay: take it over (your own Safehouse moves in from wherever it stood, free) or destroy it (back to its owner's supply).

2. Safe vs. Hostile Turf

The difference between a payday and a bloodbath:

  • Safe Districts: Any District with no rival Mobsters and no Police Squad on it: yours, or nobody's. Barrels and an unguarded rival Safehouse don't count.
  • Hostile Districts: Any District guarded by rival Mobsters. Step in and the Occupier answers first; unless they Fold, you are Pinned (see Combat).
  • The Blue Wall: A District holding a Police Squad is Padlocked: no Moving in, no buying it off, and its Speakeasy and Still are shut to everyone. Every other Speakeasy is open. A padlocked Still brews nothing and ignores Blowbacks. Once the Squad moves on, a Precinct is a District like any other, with no Still and no business.

3. Connection: Reading the Grid

Two words say how Districts link up:

  • Adjacent: They share a border, or a Bridge links them. (e.g., Williamsburg is Adjacent to Red Hook, Brownsville, Corona, and Astoria by border, and to Five Points by Bridge.)
  • Connected: Adjacent, or both Dock Districts: every Dock reaches every other Dock across the water, however far apart on the map. A Move goes to any Connected District. (e.g., You can Move directly from West Side to Throggs Neck in one Play.)

Respect: Earn It or Buy It

Cash runs out. Bullets run out. Respect is the only currency that outlasts them both. Respect is the name you make in this city: the reason a barman pours your drink first and a rival thinks twice before crossing your street. The first boss to 20 can Take the Crown (see Winning the Game). There are two roads to it, and most kingpins walk both. Jobs earn it. Bribes buy it.

Jobs: Forge Your Legend

Jobs are your shot at glory: high-risk capers that build a mob's reputation or bury it, and they pay Respect for what you do. Beside the board sits the Jobs Market, dealt face-up for every boss to read, and a card stays on offer until somebody takes it. Every Job names a deed and prints its Respect, from 1 for an evening's work to 3 for the kind they talk about for twenty years. The same number is its Stake, the Influence you lock up to claim it. Pay the stake, do the deed, take the Respect.

Claiming a Job

Once each morning, at The Offers, take a new Job or walk away from an old one, never both (see Grease the Wheels). To claim one, move markers from your Reserves onto the card equal to its Stake; they stay locked there until the Job resolves. There's no deadline, but the stake is rent, paid in Plays: the Offers come before you fund your Ledger, so once your Reserves can't fill all five slots, every marker on a Job is a Play you don't make, every Day you hold the card. Claim a 3 on Monday, pull it off on Thursday, and you've handed your rivals four short days on the street.

Pulling It Off

No declarations: do the deed with the table watching and the Job completes as that Play ends. Return its stake to your Reserves and add the card to your Respect pile; completed Jobs never return to the deck. Seize means take Control of a District from a rival: by gunfire or by their Fold. A Job that names who you Seize from counts their crew as it met you at the door, so the men you shoot in the fight still count. A crew Pinned in a rival District has seized nothing until the last of the Occupier's Mobsters falls or flees. Standing in a man's hallway isn't the same as owning his house.

The Play Is the Unit

A Job is a deed, not a board position, and only your own Play does it, with the fight it starts and any Raid it sets off (a Raid makes arrests, not kills). Signature Plays count, and one Play can complete several Jobs. A count is what that one Play did: 4+ barrels means four in a single Play. Your Ambush or return fire on a rival's Play completes nothing. A Job stays done even if the deed didn't survive the Play. Kill a rival Boss, then lose the firefight: nothing un-kills him. The job pays.

Bribes: Buy the City

Not every name is made with a gun. Some are bought, one envelope at a time. Above the Jobs Market sit four piles of Bribe cards, each man printed with his price and the Respect he pays: the Precinct Captain ($2,000, 1), the Alderman ($3,000, 2), the Judge ($4,000, 3) and the Mayor ($5,000, 4). The Bribe Play buys one, and he brings one more marker of Influence. Hold one of each. The families bring the guns. The new man brings the city.

Setup: Carving Out Your Corner

You're about to step into the smoky back rooms of New York. Before the bullets fly and the barrels roll, get your operation on its feet.

  1. Claim Your Crew:
    Shuffle the Playbooks and deal one to each player. You don't choose your family; it chooses you. Take your Ledger and your muscle: 1 Boss, 12 Runners, and 1 Safehouse.
  2. Turn Order (Who Strikes First):
    Take one set of four Turn Tokens as today's set and deal them out at random: #1 strikes first. Stack the other set in the board's Tomorrow panel, #1 on top: that is tomorrow's stack, taken from the top as players Lay Low. Three players? Box both #4s.
  3. Establish Turf:
    Shuffle the four Borough cards and deal one to each player, face-up in front of them: it is their home Borough. Three players? Box the fourth: that Borough is nobody's home. The Town Planner's Setup column names every starting District, so garrison the three it marks in your Borough: 1 Safehouse, 1 Boss and 2 Runners in your Ward, then 2 Runners each in the Speakeasy and the Dock it names. Your other 6 Runners wait in your supply. That Ward is your home Ward, the heart of the family, and the block every rival must take to crown.
  4. Raise the Blue Wall:
    The same column marks the law. Place the North Squad on East Harlem and the South Squad on Corona, the two Precincts. Two station houses, and a patrol in each.
  5. Stack the Job Deck:
    Set the eight 3-Respect Jobs aside. Shuffle the rest and count 15 off the top. Shuffle the 3s into what's left, put the 15 back on top, and set the deck face-down beside the board. Now deal Player Count + 1 face-down beside it: the Jobs Market, dark until Day 1's Grease the Wheels turns it face-up. The street wants a look at you first, and nobody hands a stranger the big work.

    Above the Market, lay out the Bribe cards in four face-up piles, one for each man. Everything else, the Shylock's Marks and the Rat cards, waits in the Supply until it's earned.
  6. Get Your Cut:
    A rising kingpin's bankroll, in cold, dirty bills. You're gonna need it. Take $1,500 cash, and put 6 of your 10 Influence markers in your Reserves. The other 4 wait in your supply until you Bribe for them. Day 1's Shadows funds your Ledger like any other morning.
  7. Ferment the First Mash:
    Roll the Mash die and leave it on the board: Day 1's Public Mash, fermenting since before you sat down. From tomorrow the Harbormaster sets it (see Shadows).

Gameplay: Run the Streets

The game is played in Days, and no Day in this city passes quietly. A Day runs in two phases, Shadows and The Hustle, and closes at Sunset.

Phase 1: Shadows

The city wakes to the hiss of steam and the churn of illegal boilers. Before your crew hits the streets, the barrels are brewed, a pipe or two bursts, the day's work goes up for grabs, and the docks decide what tomorrow will cook.

1. The Brew

The Mash die already sits on the board, set by yesterday's Harbormaster (on Day 1, by the setup roll). The boss holding Turn Token #1 rolls the communal pool: one Red die more than there are players (4 players: 5 Red).

  1. Read the Public Mash: The grey Mash die is the cheap molasses swill off the docks that every crew is cooking today. It stays put all morning and feeds every number below.
  2. Draft and Brew, one boss at a time: In Turn Order, each boss takes exactly 1 Red die and brews at once, before the next boss drafts. Your Brew Number is Mash + your Red. Every active Still you Control with that number pours Moonshine into its District: one barrel per Mobster you have there, up to 5. A sixth man brews nothing. Rival Stills with the same number stay cold; your brew is yours alone. Take your barrels from the Supply as you brew; if it runs dry, the rest is lost. When several of your Stills fire at once, you choose which to stock first. The bank pays the early riser. The man who sleeps in gets the dregs.
  3. Split the Batch: If the Still in your Boss's District fires, you may also fire one other active Still you Control in an Adjacent District, whatever its number, pouring one barrel per Mobster there. Half the mash goes over the wall, and a cold boiler earns its keep.

2. The Blowback

Cheap pipes, high pressure, and the boilers blow. One Red is always left undrafted: add it to the Mash for the Blowback Number. Unlike the brew, the Blowback hits everyone: every active Still with that number bursts, whoever Controls it. In each bursting District, the gauge sets the dead: remove 1 Mobster at Pressure 1–2, 2 at 3–4 and 3 at 5–6, Runners first; the Boss dies only if no Runner is left there to take the hit. The survivors patch the boiler and hold on: they keep their barrels and their turf, and the Still runs again tomorrow.

Kingpin's Tip: The Mash die has been public since yesterday, so every Red in the pool is a Blowback waiting to happen. Sometimes the smart draft isn't the die that fires your best Still; it's the one you daren't leave behind to burn it down. The last seat is left holding the match, and takes first pick of the morning's Jobs for its trouble.

3. Grease the Wheels

Whispers in the back booths over the morning coffee: this is when the work changes hands.

The Offers: In reverse Turn Order (the highest token picks first), each boss does one of three things:

  • Take a Job: Claim one from the Market and stake Influence from your Reserves equal to its Stake.
  • Walk Away: Drop a Job you hold, discard it, and take its staked markers back to your Reserves.
  • Nothing at all: Stand pat, and keep what you hold.

You may hold as many Jobs at once as your Reserves can stake.

Restock the Market: Once every boss has chosen, deal a fresh Job into each gap (deck dry? shuffle the discards into a new one). On Day 1 the Market is still face-down, so there are no Offers: turn it face-up now. Fresh word goes round the bars all day before anybody can act on it.

Fund the Ledger: Then everyone moves what's left in their Reserves into the Ledger, up to its 5 slots. Staked markers stay locked on their Jobs, and any beyond five wait in Reserves. Stake first, fund second.

4. The Harbormaster

Set tomorrow's Public Mash: The molasses comes ashore on somebody's piers. The boss who Controls the most Dock Districts is today's Harbormaster: he names any Mash face 1–6 and turns the Mash die to show it, where it stays until tomorrow's Brew. If the lead is tied, nobody holds the piers, so roll the die instead. Either way the whole table plays the Day knowing tomorrow's Mash.

Example: The Morning Brew

Setup: Day 6. Yesterday's Harbormaster set the Mash at 3. Turn Order is Sicilians #1, Irish #2, Knights #3, Vipers #4, so the Sicilians roll 5 Red for the pool: 1, 4, 4, 5, 6. Four bosses, five dice: one Red will be left holding the match.

1. Read the Public Mash

The Mash is 3. Every number this morning starts there. Each boss already knows which Red he needs; the only question is whether he gets it.

2. Draft and Brew, one boss at a time

Each boss drafts, brews and takes his barrels before the next man touches a die. Shown as a table for space; at the table it happens one seat at a time.

3. The Blowback

One Red went undrafted: the 4. Add it to the Mash: 3 + 4 = 7. Every active, Controlled Still numbered 7 bursts, whoever Controls the District, and the Cotton Club is lit.

A 7 runs at Pressure 6, so Sugar Hill takes 3 casualties, all three of them Runners: the Boss is safe while a Runner is there to take the hit. The Sicilians keep their 6 Barrels, keep the turf, and the Cotton Club runs again tomorrow.

The Moral: The Sicilians brewed richest and lost three Runners to the very same number. 7 paid them and bled them, and they could see the second 4 sitting in the pool the whole time. The first seat brews richest. The last seat holds the match.

Phase 2: The Hustle

The sun is up and the streets are yours, for as long as your clout holds out. Players act in Turn Order, one Play each, around and around the table until everyone has Laid Low for the Day.

On Your Turn: make one Play
  1. Make a Play (no passing). Spend Influence on one Play from the tables below or your Playbook, or make the one Play that costs nothing: Lay Low.
  2. Resolve any combat the Play triggers, fully, before the Play ends.
  3. Pay the cost. Spent markers return from your Ledger to your Reserves. If the Play drew Heat, 1 marker locks onto the Heat Track instead.

Plays: Working the Angles

Standard Plays (Cost: 1 Influence)
Move Send any number of your Mobsters and/or barrels from one District to one Connected District. You cannot Move into a District held by a Police Squad.

Barrels travel alone: your distribution network runs them, so a Move can send barrels with no Mobster beside them. Barrels sent alone start no Standoff, and in a District you don't Control they are no longer yours (see Barrels Hold Nothing). Sell a rival his stock, or plant the evidence the Squads come looking for.

Into a Hostile District: The Occupier answers first, and unless they Fold, your Mobsters are Pinned (see Combat). Barrels ride in with them.

A rival Safehouse with no Mobsters: no Standoff, no shots fired. Take it over or destroy it (see A Rival Safehouse).
Recruit Hire any number of Runners from your supply into the District holding your Safehouse, paying the bank $300 per Runner. No Safehouse on the board, no Recruiting.
Secure Place your Safehouse in any Safe District for $500, relocating it if it was already on the board. A rival's Safehouse there is destroyed. It holds nothing on its own: Control takes Mobsters, so Recruit there or Move men in.
Trade At a Dock you Control, swap the Moonshine barrels sitting there for Havana Rum, one for one. Swap as many as you like in one Play. The contact boats run it in from Rum Row, and no middleman takes his half. It is the only way Rum enters the game. (Dry Dock: the Rum pool is 20 barrels; when it runs out, the docks stay dry until sold Rum cycles back.)
Beg When the walls close in, go see Shylock. One deal per visit:
The Loan: Take $1,500 and one of his Marks. Hold as many as you like, but Shylock has only 7 in his book; while all 7 are out, nobody borrows until one comes back.
Square Up: Pay $2,000 and hand one Mark back. Shylock lends in fifteens and collects in twenties.
Each Mark you hold costs you 2 Respect (see Count Your Respect).
The Free Play (Cost: 0)
Lay Low End your Day. Flush, it's how you step off early; out of Influence, it's the only Play left. When you Lay Low:
  1. Clear each unspent marker from your Ledger to your Reserves, collecting $100 apiece (Reserves don't pay).
  2. Take the top token from tomorrow's stack: the lowest number left. Lay Low first, strike first tomorrow.
Broke is not Laid Low: with an empty Ledger you still take your turn until you make this Play. Once made, no more Plays until tomorrow, though you still answer anyone who walks onto your turf (see Combat).

Sunset: The streetlamps come on, and the city counts what it's owed. Sunset is the instant the final player Lays Low, uncovering SUNSET under the last token. Any promise still unmet earns its breaker a Rat card (see Cooperation), and the Turn Tokens claimed today become tomorrow's Turn Order.

Power Plays

Some moves shake the whole city. Power Plays cost double, but still count as a single Play in the turn cycle.

Power Plays (Cost: 2 Influence)
Unload Sell the Barrels: At a Speakeasy you Control, sell any number of your barrels sitting in that District. Moonshine pays $200 per barrel and Havana Rum pays $500. A High Society Venue buys Havana Rum only: no swill crosses those bars.

The High Society Kickback: At a High Society Venue you Control, every barrel of Havana Rum Unloaded also moves 1 Influence marker from your Reserves into an empty Ledger slot (no marker in Reserves, or no empty slot? That Kickback is lost). The Unload's own markers leave the Ledger first, so their slots count as empty: pour five Rum on your last two markers and the Ledger empties, then refills to five, stretching your Day past the five markers it held at dawn. Rich men tip in favors, and they drink imported.

Heat: one marker, however many barrels go.
Bribe Every man in this city has a price, and you pay it in cash. Take a Bribe card from its pile and pay the bank the price printed on it (see Buy the City). The card goes in your Respect pile, and 1 Influence marker moves from your supply into your Reserves: one more marker, for good. You may hold one of each. Your Boss makes the drop in person, so he must be on the board.
Extort Shake down the locals: earn a flat $100 for each District you Control anywhere on the map, as often as your Ledger pays for it. Heat: one marker, however many pay.
Snitch There's no honor among thieves when the sirens wail. Drop a dime on your rivals: trigger an immediate Police Raid, and the Squads come for whoever made the freshest noise (see Police Raids).

Rat: Making this Play, you take a Rat card from the Supply: −1 Respect. Snitch as often as you like, while the Supply has a Rat card. The word's out that you talk, and the street prices you accordingly.
Rise The chair never stays empty for long. With your Boss in your supply, place him in any Safe District of your home Borough: a successor takes the chair among his own people, and the family has a head again.

Mob Signature Plays

Four families, four ways to make a killing. Each has two signature tricks no rival can copy, the things that make them them. Those marked In a Firefight (Hit, Tommy Gun and Torch) are made inside a fight you are already Pinned in: they cost Influence but never a turn, and draw the fight's Heat only if they fire its first shot. The rest are ordinary Plays, and cost you your turn.

Sicilian Syndicate

Cut off the head and the body follows. The family settles its scores at the top, and one phone call later the paperwork is gone.

Hit (Cost: 2)

With no rival Boss there, it is an ordinary Open Fire.
Consigliere (Cost: 2) One phone call and the trouble disappears. Remove 1 Influence marker of your choice from the Heat Track and return it to its owner's Reserves (slide the rest down to close the gap).

Haul (Cost: 2) A sprint, not a walk: Move Mobsters (no barrels) from a District you Control two steps, each to an Adjacent District. The first step must be Safe, and they run straight through it. Otherwise it is a Move into the second: a Standoff in Hostile turf (Fall Back goes to the first step), and a rival Safehouse with no Mobsters is yours to take over or destroy.

The Safehouse is destroyed at once, and every barrel in the District burns with it, back to the Supply. Rival Mobsters are unharmed, and the rival keeps Control.

Winning the Game: Take the Crown

Every boss in New York wants the crown. Make a name, take another family's home turf, and one Play puts it on your head. Let the others rot in unmarked graves.

Take the Crown

A Power Play (Cost: 2 Influence), printed on your Playbook. Make it on your turn with both of these true:

  • The Name: 20 or more Respect (see Count Your Respect).
  • The Ward: your Boss in a rival's home Ward whose Borough card lies face-up: the Ward of a Borough another boss was dealt, never your own. At three players the boxed Borough's Ward is nobody's home, and crowns nobody. Any boss can hold his own block. The families want the man who can walk into another family's heart and stand there.

Each rival's home Ward begins the game as his headquarters, his Boss and Safehouse inside, so the crown is usually taken at gunpoint. A Ward has no bar: a crew dug in there to wait for the day earns only what its Still brews. And New York has never knelt to a dead man: a fallen Boss Rises in his own home Borough, and has to fight his way back.

Make the Play, and the war is over. The families at the Commission rise to their feet, and the game ends on the spot, in the middle of the Day. Nothing else is settled: every promise still due at Sunset dies with the war.

Count Your Respect

Respect is the pile of cards in front of you, and anyone at the table can count it:

  • Criminal Legends: Gain 1, 2 or 3 Respect for each completed Job in your Respect pile.
  • Friends in High Places: Gain 1 to 4 Respect for each Bribe card in your Respect pile, as printed.
  • On the Hook: Lose 2 Respect for each Shylock's Mark you hold, until you Square Up.
  • The Brand: Lose 1 Respect for each Rat card in your Respect pile, from a Snitch or a broken word. The street's memory is long.

One boss wears the crown, and the rest bleed out in the gutter.

Cooperation: Deals in the Smoke

Trust is as fleeting as a whisper in the wind, but no Kingpin rules alone. Every favor granted might be a favor owed, or a knife in the back.

A Binding Handshake

A Mobster's word is cheap. When a deal needs teeth, seal it the old way: look the man in the eye and shake his hand, where the whole table can see it.

Making the Deal

  1. State the Terms: The terms must be clear, and every deal runs out at Sunset. Nobody's word in this town is good for longer than that.
  2. Shake on It: Both players shake hands. That's the whole ceremony. A Handshake is not a Play, costs nothing, and can be made at any time during the Hustle. In this town, the handshake IS the contract.

Example: "Shake on it. If you let me pass through your Borough unharmed, I promise to give you $1,000 before Sunset."

Honoring & Breaking Deals

  • Deal Honored: The moment the terms are met, the deal is complete, and both reputations walk away clean.
  • Deal Broken: If Sunset comes with a promise unmet, or a player violates the agreement for any reason (including third-party interference), their word becomes garbage: the breaker takes a Rat card from the Supply and adds it to their Respect pile, alongside the Jobs they're prouder of. Rat cards stack, and each costs 1 Respect (see Winning the Game). A man who breaks his word is a rat, and the street says so.

Deals on the Side

Promise whatever you like, but only Cash crosses the table. Pass it at any time: no Play, no Influence, no questions asked. Barrels change hands on the board, by a Move into the buyer's District. A wad of bills for safe passage, a payoff for silence: it's all fair game until the double-cross.

Combat Rules: Blood on the Streets

Violence isn't a backup plan; it's the cost of doing business. Smart Kingpins know a drawn gun beats a fired one. When talk fails, step inside and clear the room.

The Rules of Engagement

Violence runs on two numbers. Each side rolls one die per Mobster in the fight, up to 5; its Threat says what number kills.

Threat Level Kill on Roll
Level 1 (Base) 5–6
Level 2 4–6
Level 3 (Max) 3–6

Calculating Threat

Everyone starts at Level 1. Bonuses stack:

  • Boss +1 Threat: If your Boss is in the fight.
  • Ambush +1 Threat: The Occupier's opening Ambush only.

These bonuses ride on the situation, not the Play: they apply to every roll you make from that position, Open Fire, Hit or Tommy Gun alike.

The Shape of a Fight

Every firefight is just two questions, asked in order:

  • Step 1, the Occupier answers: Ambush, Hold Fire, or Fold.
  • Step 2, the Invader chooses (and keeps choosing until the Pin is resolved): Open Fire, Advance, or Fall Back.

A Firefight Is One Play

The Move that walked in the door is the Play. Every choice after it, from the Ambush to the last volley and the signature tricks between, happens inside that Play, paid from the Ledger of whoever makes it; none of them costs a turn. So the crew holding the District always answers, even one that has Laid Low: the free answers are always open, the paid ones need a marker in your Ledger, and your men shoot back whenever the Invader Opens Fire.

1. The Standoff: Crossing the Line

When an opponent's Mobsters Move into a District yours hold, the game pauses on Step 1. Set each crew along the edge it crossed from, so it stays clear whose Mobsters and barrels are whose.

Ambush (Cost: 1) Greet the invaders with lead.
  • First Shot: Only the Occupier rolls dice, at +1 Threat.
  • Draws Heat : firing first, you take the fight's one Heat marker; your spent marker locks onto the Track.
  • Casualties: Hits remove Invaders (Runners first). Invaders are now Pinned.
Hold Fire (Cost: 0) Stand your ground but don't shoot. Maybe they're just passing through. The Invaders are now Pinned.
Fold (Cost: 0) Surrender the District. Your Mobsters Scram: they run to one Connected Safe District of your choice, never the one the Invader came from. With nowhere Safe to Scram, you cannot Fold.
  • Panic: A crew that Scrams carries no barrels: they are left behind.
  • The Spoils: The Invader immediately claims Control, captures the dropped barrels, and takes over or destroys your Safehouse. The Invaders are not Pinned; their Play ends.

2. Pinned: Fight or Flight

A Pin stops the city where it stands: it is fought out then and there, and no Play is made anywhere, by anyone, until it resolves. Spend Influence to Open Fire or Advance, or Fall Back for free. Hit, Tommy Gun and Torch are open to you here too, if your Ledger can pay for them.

Open Fire (Cost: 1) Fight for the District. Both sides roll simultaneously, you Red and the Occupier White, but only you pay: his return fire is free.
  • Casualties: Both sides apply hits (Runners first). The Occupier loses the Ambush bonus but keeps his Boss bonus.
  • Heat : If the Occupier Held Fire, your first volley is the fight's first shot and draws its Heat. After an Ambush, no volley does.
  • Repeat: If both sides stand, remain Pinned. Spend another Influence to Open Fire again.
Advance (Cost: 1) Push on through. Move your Pinned Mobsters to any Safe District Connected to this one, taking as many of the barrels they carried in as you like; any you leave are the Occupier's. Hostile turf is closed to you. Ambushed on the way in? Leave him a barrel or two, and the Squads will know whose door to kick.
Fall Back (Cost: 0)
(Invader Only)		 Cut and run. Retreat to the District you came from, across water if that's how you came. Carried barrels are left behind for the Occupier.
With no marker left in your Ledger, you must Fall Back.

3. Victory & Consequences

Combat ends when one side is wiped out or flees, or the Invader Advances. If both crews fall in the same volley, nobody wins and nobody Controls the District; a Safehouse there stays put.

If the Occupier Holds: The District remains theirs, along with any barrels the Invaders left behind.

If the Invader Wins: The moment the last of the Occupier's Mobsters falls or flees, you claim Control of the District.

  • The Spoils: Any barrels in the District are now yours.
  • Safehouses: Take over or destroy any rival Safehouse there (see A Rival Safehouse).

Heat: Playing with Fire

Heat measures how hard the law is breathing down your neck. The Heat Track is a 5-space fuse: draw Heat and your spent marker goes there instead of home to your Reserves. Played right it's a weapon; played wrong, a noose.

Drawing Heat

Every noisy Play (marked with the flame ) adds one marker:

  • First Shot Draws Heat: A firefight adds 1 marker , and it belongs to whoever fired first: the Occupier if they Ambush, otherwise the Invader on his first Open Fire, Hit, Tommy Gun or Torch. One marker, however many volleys.
  • Money Talks: Every Unload and every Extort adds 1 marker , however many barrels go or Districts pay. A delivery truck at the back door is a delivery truck, whether it carries one barrel or ten.

Police Raids: The Boiling Point

A Raid triggers two ways: the 5th marker lands on the track, or someone makes the Snitch Play. Either way, pause the turn. Heat lands as the Play that drew it ends, so a firefight always finishes before the sirens start.

A Raid puts both Squads on the move in number order, the North Squad (1) first, each finishing before the next stirs.

1. The Most Wanted List

Each Squad hunts within reach and chases the freshest gunshot it can hear. Its reach is only the Districts directly Adjacent to it on its own side of the city: North (Manhattan and the Bronx) or South (Queens and Brooklyn). It never crosses to the other side. Find the target in two steps:

  1. Pick the mob (Follow the Smoke): Of the mobs holding Districts in reach, target the one whose marker sits furthest right on the Heat Track: the most recent noise. If none of them has a marker there, the Squad goes home to its Precinct (East Harlem in the North, Corona in the South), and raids it, Heat or none, even if only a Safehouse or barrels wait there. Quiet money walks free, unless it's squatting in the station house.
  2. Pick the District (The Big Bust): If that mob holds one District in reach, that's the target. If it holds several, the Squad kicks in the one with the most barrels; on a tie, the hottest boiler, the one with the highest Pressure. The louder the boiler, the harder the door comes off.

2. The Scatter

The Squad takes one step onto the chosen District and kicks the doors in:

  • Condemned: Any Safehouse there is destroyed, and all barrels go to the Supply.
  • Scram: The crew runs, as from a Fold, to one Connected Safe District of its owner's choice, never the one the Squad came from.
  • Cornered: With nowhere Safe to Scram, the whole crew is arrested, back to its owner's supply. Leave a back door, or don't leave at all.

The Squad now holds the District, Padlocked, with any Speakeasy or Still beneath it, until a later Raid.

The Aftermath: Once every Squad has moved, all Heat markers return to their owners' Reserves and the turn resumes.

The Blood Oath: 4-Player Team Variant

When the heat boils over, enemies become brothers. The Feds are kicking down doors and lone wolves get slaughtered. The Blood Oath is the recommended 4-player mode: a cutthroat free-for-all until, on a morning nobody can pick, the city splits into two permanent 2v2 alliances.

Setup: The Oath in the Deck

Set up as a standard 4-player match, with one addition: when you stack the Job deck, shuffle the Blood Oath card in with the 3s, below the top 15 (see Setup).

The Truce

The moment a restock deals the Blood Oath card into the Market, the underworld snaps. Pause the game. The four independent mobs immediately form two permanent alliances; then set the card aside for good and deal a Job into its gap. The card can't be claimed, so a Viper whose Whispers finds it on top of the deck leaves it there for the next restock.

  1. Rank the Bosses: Tally everyone's current Respect (see Winning the Game: Count Your Respect). Tiebreaker: Total Cash on hand.
  2. Forge the Families: The player in 1st Place joins forces with the player in 4th Place. The player in 2nd Place pairs with the player in 3rd Place.
  3. The Target is 30: Both Alliances race for the same number: 30 combined Respect. Nothing to write down; just add your halves together and see who's closer.

New Rules of Engagement

One goal, two crews. You still run your own operations.

  • Separate Ledgers: You do not share Cash, Influence, or Jobs, and you still take individual turns in Turn Order.
  • Turf is Turf: Partner turf is Hostile ground, and his men fight like any other mob's. The Golden Rule of Turf holds between partners too, and barrels Moved into his District become his.
  • Nothing on Your Books: A Job pays only for deeds done against the opposing Alliance. Your partner's Boss, his Safehouse and his Districts are worth nothing in your Respect pile.
  • The Family Bank: Influence never crosses the table, even between partners, but Cash moves freely off the board (see Cooperation): bankroll whichever of you is closer to the deed. A Handshake broken against your own blood still earns the Rat card, and the Alliance pays the point together.

Claiming the Empire

The Commission only sits down with unified power, and it wants to see both men stand in enemy blood. Each partner must Take the Crown, with his own Boss in a home Ward of the opposing Alliance, and the Alliance must hold 30 combined Respect each time.

Their Ward Has Fallen

The first crown wins nothing yet: turn that Ward's Borough card face down. Its Ward has fallen, and nobody crowns there again. The second partner must take the other Ward, still face-up, and when he Takes the Crown there, partners win together. The Commission seats a family, not a man.

Glossary: Speak the Lingo

The words that carry a rule, in one place. Each names the section that holds the rule in full.

  • Adjacent: Sharing a border, or joined by a Bridge. (Territory)
  • Connected: Adjacent, or two Docks across the water. (Territory)
  • Control: Your Mobster on the District, settled as each Play ends. A Safehouse alone holds nothing. (Territory)
  • Destroy: Send a Safehouse back to its owner's supply. (A Rival Safehouse)
  • Firefight: Everything from the Standoff until the Pin resolves, all inside the one Play that started it. (Combat)
  • Heat: A noisy spend, its marker locked on the Heat Track until a Raid. The furthest right is the freshest noise. (Heat)
  • High Society Venue: One of the four Speakeasies on a #7 Still: Rum only, and a Kickback a barrel. (The Map)
  • Home Borough, home Ward: The Borough on the card you were dealt at setup, where a fallen Boss Rises, and its Ward, where your Boss starts and your rivals Take the Crown. At three players one Borough is nobody's home. (Setup)
  • Hostile: Guarded by rival Mobsters. (Territory)
  • In a Firefight: A Signature Play made while Pinned. It costs Influence, never a turn. (Mob Signature Plays)
  • Invader, Occupier: The mob that Moved in, and the mob that held the District. (Combat)
  • Kickback: Each Rum barrel Unloaded at a High Society Venue you Control moves a marker from your Reserves to an empty Ledger slot. (Unload)
  • Kill: A rival Mobster shot dead in your own Play's fight. An arrest is not a kill. (Jobs)
  • Ledger, Reserves: The Ledger's five slots hold what you can spend today; the Reserves hold the rest and stake Jobs. (Currencies)
  • Mark: Shylock's paper, taken with a Loan: −2 Respect while you hold it. (Beg)
  • Open: A Speakeasy that isn't Padlocked: open for business. (Territory)
  • Padlocked: Held by a Police Squad: nobody Moves or fights their way in, and its Speakeasy and Still are shut: no sales, no brew, no Blowback. (Territory)
  • Pinned: An Invader who must Open Fire, Advance or Fall Back until the fight resolves. No Play is made anywhere meanwhile. (Combat)
  • Play: Your turn's one action. Standard Plays cost 1 Influence, Power Plays 2, Lay Low nothing. (The Hustle)
  • Precinct: East Harlem or Corona: no Still and no business, where a Squad starts and comes home. (The Map)
  • Pressure: How hot a Still runs, shown on its gauge: 6 on the 7, down to 1 on the 2 and the 12. A Blowback kills 1 at Pressure 1–2, 2 at 3–4 and 3 at 5–6. (The Map, Shadows)
  • Raid: Set off by the 5th Heat marker or a Snitch. Every Police Squad moves, one at a time, after the freshest noise in its reach. (Heat)
  • Rat card: Taken for a Snitch or a broken promise: −1 Respect while you hold it. Twelve in the Supply. (Power Plays, Cooperation)
  • Respect: Your completed Jobs and Bribe cards, less 2 per Mark you hold and 1 per Rat card. (Winning the Game)
  • Safe: No rival Mobsters and no Police Squad: yours, or nobody's. (Territory)
  • Scram: A crew's run from a Fold or a Raid: to one Connected Safe District its owner picks, never the one the trouble came from, carrying no barrels. (Combat, Heat)
  • Seize: Take Control of a District from a rival, by a won fight or his Fold. Control must change hands. (Jobs)
  • Stake: Influence locked on a Job from your Reserves until it resolves: as many markers as the Job pays Respect. (Jobs)
  • Standoff: The Occupier's answer when rival Mobsters Move into his District: Ambush, Hold Fire or Fold. A Stealth gets none. (Combat)
  • Sunset: The instant the final player Lays Low. Unmet promises take their Rat card, then the Turn Tokens set tomorrow's order. (The Hustle)
  • Supply: The Supply is the shared stock of barrels and cards. Your supply is your own pieces off the board, the Influence you have yet to Bribe for among them. (Components)
  • Take over: Move your own Safehouse into a rival's place, free, when you take Control of his District. (A Rival Safehouse)
  • Threat: What a die must roll to kill: 5+ at Level 1, down to 3+ at Level 3. (Combat)
