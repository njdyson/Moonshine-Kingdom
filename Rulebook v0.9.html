RULEBOOK

A Game of Bootlegging, Backstabbing, and Gang Warfare.

New York City, 1926. Prohibition didn't stop the drinking; it just made the price go up. Every basement is a brewery, every back room a bank, and every block worth killing for. Forge fragile alliances, corner the market, and turn the Police loose on your rivals. This city has a thirst that can't be quenched, and only one mob will wear its crown. Carve your name into the skyline, or vanish like cigar smoke in a hurricane.

A Day in the Life of a Kingpin

2-4 Players | 90-120 Mins

In Moonshine Kingdom you are a rising Kingpin, one of four bosses clawing for a city that isn't big enough for all of you. The war plays out over a series of Days, each running from the fog of morning to the reckoning at night.

Morning: Shadows

Dawn breaks over the Hudson and the boilers burn. Fire your Stills and cook cheap molasses into liquid gold, but every crew is drawing off the same batch, and the pipes you leave behind have a habit of bursting. With the smoke still clearing, you take your pick of the Jobs on the Market: dirty work with your name on it.

Day: The Hustle

The sun is up, and so is the heat. Run your barrels, seize turf, and strong-arm the competition. Every Play costs Influence, your political clout, and it runs dry fast. Step off the street early and you strike first tomorrow. Remember: violence is loud, and the Police are listening.

Night: Reckoning

Twilight settles like a bruise. The cops sweep up any crew grown too big to ignore, the Deeds and crowns change hands, and the city takes its measure of you. Did you build an empire today, or just a body count?

The Only Crown Worth Having

Cash buys bullets. Respect buys immortality: the turf you bled for, the crowns you tore off rival heads, the jobs so brazen the papers set your name in ink an inch tall.

But the throne isn't seized at gunpoint; it's bought, from the judges and aldermen who quietly decide which animal gets to call himself King. Every one of them has a price, paid in cold cash, and what you buy is Influence. Build a legend the city can't ignore, buy up the men who hand out crowns, and keep your name clean: that is how New York changes hands.

Game Components: Tools of the Trade

Your whole damn operation in miniature. Every piece earns, bleeds, or burns.

The City Map (Game Board): A soot-stained blueprint of 1920s New York, carved into 25 Districts across 5 Boroughs: a boiler on every block, and a shared Heat Track measuring how badly the city wants you gone.

The Ledger (x4): Your back-room desk. A slotted player board where the day's Influence sits, gets spent, and comes back. Every move you make starts here.

Mob Pieces (x4 Sets): One color to a mob: take the set matching the Playbook you're dealt. Each holds 1 Boss and 15 Runners (the brains and the muscle), 1 Safehouse, 10 Influence Markers, and 1 Mob Playbook of dirty tricks only your family can pull.

The Cards: The paper trail of your empire.

  • Job Cards (32): Dirty work with your name on it. Pull one off and the city remembers.
  • Borough Deeds (5): The paper that says a Borough is yours.
  • Titles (3): The Ward Boss, the Harbormaster and the Night Mayor. The city's three crowns.
  • Shylock's Marks (7): Debt, in writing. A lifeline with teeth.
  • The Rat Card (1): The ultimate sin in cardboard form.

Turn Tokens (8): Two sets numbered 1–4. One set runs today; the other decides tomorrow.

The Town Planning Ledger (1): City Hall's own paperwork: the District roster you carve the map up from at setup. Its reverse holds a quick reference for the Day's phases.

Liquor Barrels (x30 Moonshine, x15 Havana Rum): The lifeblood of your operation, in two flavors: Moonshine (Grey, x30) and Havana Rum (Brown, x15). Both pools are fixed and do not scale with player count: the grey runs deep, but Rum is scarce and contested. Barrels cycle back to the bank when sold or Traded away; if a supply is empty, those barrels simply are not there to be had. Cheap swill and the good stuff; nothing in between.

The Dice (11): 5 White, 5 Red, and 1 grey Mash die. White and Red settle grudges and fire the boilers; the lone Mash die does one job only, and the whole table watches it.

The Bankroll (Cash): Dirty, untraceable bills in denominations of $100, $500, and $1,000. The kind that never sees a bank.

Police Squads (4 Blue Pieces): The blue wall that wants to see you swing. Four patrols prowling the mainland, drawn to noise.

Federal Crackdown Tracker (1) (4-Player Team Variant only): A 10-space pressure clock. Leave it in the box for a standard game.

Key Concepts: The Underworld at a Glance

Your Mob: The Brains, the Brawn, and the Bastards

From the cold steel of the Sicilian Syndicate to the fiery grit of the Hell's Kitchen Irish, every family kills in its own accent. You command:

A Boss The head of the snake. In a firefight he makes every shot deadlier; at a Still he brews an extra barrel. Let him catch a bullet and your crew is crippled until you crown a successor.
Runners Knuckle-draggers and wheelmen who haul hooch, hold turf, and catch bullets. You get 15 and no more, never enough to cover the city. So expand anyway: leave the borders thin and let the threat of violence stand guard.
Safehouse Your one and only headquarters. It holds Turf even when empty, gives a heavy edge in a shootout, and is the only block in the city where you Recruit. Move it by Securing a District you Control, or take a rival's when you overrun their turf.

The Muscle Ratio: Street Math

One rule turns headcount into results everywhere it matters: barrels brewed, bodies in a Blowback, dice in a firefight.

Count the Mobsters (Boss and Runners) in a District, then halve it, round up, and stop at 5. The same number brews your barrels, drops your dead, and fills your dice.

The Map: Know Your Turf

Your empire is a broken city: 25 Districts across 5 Boroughs, each a flavor of poison and profit. Certain key Districts offer unique opportunities:

Speakeasies The only blocks where you can Unload Liquor for cash, a flat $300 a barrel. Count for the Night Mayor Title. The booze doesn't set the price; the address does.
High Society One premium Speakeasy per mainland Borough, taking Havana Rum only and paying a massive $500 a barrel. All four start padlocked by the Police until a Raid throws them open.
Wards Gritty blocks where mob roots run deep, and your street network: every Ward you Control knocks $100 off Recruiting, wherever your Safehouse sits. Count for the Ward Boss Title.
Docks Where the rum comes ashore: at a Dock you Control, Trade Moonshine up to Havana Rum. Docks also connect to one another across the water, the fastest road on the map. Count for the Harbormaster Title.
Stills Every District has a boiler printed on the board, numbered 2–12. The moment you Control it the Still is active: it fires when its number is brewed, and risks Blowback when the leftovers match.

The Currencies of the Underworld

To rule the city you'll need more than bullets. Two resources fuel the hustle, and a third buys power:

  • Cash: Expands your crew. Spend it to Recruit and Secure turf. Earn it by Unloading, Extorting, and running Odd Jobs when you Lay Low.
  • Liquor Barrels: Your lifeblood. Every drop is born as Moonshine at your Stills; run it to a Dock and Trade it up to Havana Rum. Moonshine dumps cheap at any Standard Speakeasy; Rum sells dearer, buys Influence back, and is the only bottle the High Society rooms will pour.

Influence: Currency of Power

Influence is your political clout, and the throne itself. You start with 6 markers and Bribe for more, one at a time, to a hard ceiling of 10. Hold all ten and you've bought the whole council (see Winning the Game). A handful of Plays can cost you markers outright, but your total never falls below 5, a full Ledger: the council will take your crown, but it won't take your desk. Day to day, those markers cycle:

  1. The Reserves: Where uncommitted markers stack, and where you stake Jobs from.
  2. Fund (Shadows): Fill your Ledger's 5 slots from the Reserves. Only Ledger markers pay for Plays; the rest wait in Reserves to stake Jobs and soak up Heat.
  3. Execute (The Hustle): Spend Ledger markers to make Plays.
  4. Resolve: A quiet Play returns its marker to your Reserves. A noisy one locks it onto the communal Heat Track instead.

Kingpin's Tip: Capacity, Not Actions

Extra Influence buys no extra actions: the Ledger stops at 5 slots. What deep Reserves buy is capacity, to soak up Heat and stake Jobs without gutting your day at the table. You can't out-action your rivals. You have to out-think them.

Territory: Lines in the Concrete

An empire isn't built on bullets alone. It's built on logistics: moving product, and drawing borders your rivals won't cross. Your muscle is always spread thin, so a well-placed Safehouse and a few guards usually keep the peace.

1. Control: Boots on the Ground

You can't tax what you don't hold. You Control a District if you have a Mobster (Boss or Runner) or a Safehouse standing on it.

  • Instant Possession: Control shifts the moment your piece enters. Even passing through an empty block claims it for that instant.
  • Abandonment: A barrel of gin can't hold a street corner. Lose your last Mobster with no Safehouse to anchor the block, and Control goes with them. Any Liquor left behind waits to be stolen.
  • The Golden Rule of Turf: Come Shadows and Reckoning, only one mob stands in a District. Rivals cannot enter your turf without starting a firefight.
  • Contested Ground: During a firefight, the original Occupier retains Control (and all defensive bonuses) until their last piece falls or retreats.

2. Safe vs. Hostile Turf

The difference between a payday and a bloodbath:

  • Safe Districts: Any District you Control, plus any Defenseless block: no rival Mobsters, no Police Squad, and no rival Safehouse. Loose Liquor alone never makes a block unsafe.
  • Hostile Districts: Any District guarded by rival Mobsters. Stepping onto Hostile turf immediately leaves your Mobsters Pinned (see Combat: Pinned).
  • The Blue Wall: A District holding a Police Squad is Impassable: you cannot Move in, attack in, storm it or buy it off. Any Speakeasy or Still beneath the Squad is padlocked: shut to everyone, held by nobody for Titles, and a padlocked Still brews nothing and ignores Blowbacks. The four Squads are automated and Borough-locked, one to a mainland Borough, and move only during a Raid (see Police Raids).

3. Connection: Reading the Grid

Flanking a rival and slipping a blockade both start with the layout. Districts are Connected if:

  • Land & Bridges: They share a border, or a Bridge links them.
    (e.g., Williamsburg connects to Red Hook, Brownsville, Corona, and Astoria by land, and to Five Points by Bridge).
  • Waterways (Docks): All Dock Districts connect to each other across the water as if they were adjacent.
    (e.g., You can Move directly from West Side to Throggs Neck in one Play).

Your Goal: Earning Respect

In this city a reputation outlives any bankroll, and it's the one thing a bullet can't take back. Respect is that reputation made currency, and you build it two ways: Jobs too loud to ignore, and Titles that prove you own the streets.

1. Jobs: Forge Your Legend

Deeds and Titles pay you for what you hold; Jobs pay you for what you do. Beside the board sits the Jobs Market: the word on the street, dealt face-up for every boss to read. It doesn't churn and nothing on it expires. What the city is offering stays offered until somebody is man enough to take it.

Every Job prints two numbers and nothing else you need: its Stake, the Influence you lock up to claim it, and its Respect, what it pays when you pull it off. Pay the stake, do the deed, take the respect. They run from a 1, an evening's work, to a 5: the kind they print on the front page and talk about for twenty years.

Claiming a Job

Once each morning, at The Offers, you may take a new Job or walk away from an old one, never both (see Grease the Wheels). Claiming means staking Influence from your Reserves equal to the Job's Stake, and those markers sit locked until it resolves. The bigger the promise, the longer your capital sleeps, and the longer rivals have to wreck your setup. There's no deadline; the only clock is your money. Nobody signs contracts in this business, but breaking one takes time to smooth over.

Pulling It Off

No declarations, no paperwork: do the deed with the whole table watching and the Job completes on the spot. Take back your stake and add the card to your Respect pile; completed Jobs never return to the deck. When a card says Seize, it means take Control of a District from a rival, by gunfire or by strolling in where they left the door open.

The Play Is the Unit A Job is a deed, not a board position. When a Play ends, check it against your claimed Jobs: if the Play did the deed, the Job is done. The deed counts even if it didn't survive the Play. Kill a rival Boss, then lose the firefight: nothing un-kills him. The job pays.

2. Titles: Carving Up the City

Titles aren't owned, they're held, and only while you're the biggest name on the block. Pull ahead of every rival on the turf that counts and the crown swings your way; slip behind and it swings just as fast to whoever stands tallest. The city settles its crowns each nightfall (see Stake Your Claim), and any Title still in your grip when the books close is worth 2 Respect.

Borough Deeds

Seizing a Borough turns muscle into empire. Hold more Districts in a Borough than any single rival to claim its Deed.

Ward Boss

The Boroughs make headlines, but the Wards decide who owns the block. Hold more Ward Districts than any single rival to claim the Ward Boss Title.

Harbormaster

You can't stop a man's ferry, but you can own the pier. Hold more Dock Districts than any single rival to claim the Harbormaster Title. Every barrel of molasses lands on your docks, so you set Tomorrow's Mash: you name the Mash die's face instead of rolling it (see Ferment Tomorrow's Mash). Feed your own Stills, starve the rivals'; the whole city cooks the number you choose.

Night Mayor

The sun belongs to the law; the night belongs to you. Hold more Speakeasies than any single rival to claim the Night Mayor Title. High Society joints count too, once a Raid has thrown them open. A swanky room is still a speakeasy.

The Commission

Jobs and Titles forge your legend, but they don't buy you a chair at the table. Above the sirens and the smoke sits The Commission: the judges, the aldermen, and the money men who quietly decide who gets to call himself King. Every last one is for sale, and they all take the same coin: cold, untraceable cash. Every Bribe buys another marker of Influence, another man in your pocket, until all 10 answer to you (see Winning the Game). Respect makes you feared; a bought Commission makes you untouchable. You need both.

Setup: Carving Out Your Corner

  1. Claim Your Crew:
    Shuffle the Playbooks and deal one to each player. Take your Ledger and your muscle: 1 Boss, 15 Runners, and 1 Safehouse.
  2. Establish Turf:
    Nobody starts on Staten Island; return its Deed to the box. Shuffle the four remaining Borough Deeds and deal one to each player: your home turf. The Town Planning Ledger's Setup column names every starting District, so garrison the three it marks in your Borough: 1 Safehouse, 1 Boss and 2 Runners in your Ward, then 3 Runners each in the Speakeasy and the Dock it names. Your remaining 7 Runners wait in your supply.
  3. Raise the Blue Wall:
    The same column marks the law: 1 Police Squad on each High Society Speakeasy, one per mainland Borough (4 in all). They start padlocked and impassable. Till a Raid throws the premium joints open, the mobs fight over scraps.
  4. Stack the Job Deck:
    Set the eight 5-Respect Jobs aside. Shuffle the rest and deal Player Count + 1 face-up beside the board: the Jobs Market. Now shuffle the 5s back in and set the deck face-down alongside the row. Nobody hands a stranger the big work.

    Everything else, the Titles, Shylock's Marks and the Rat Card, waits in the Supply until it's earned. A crown, a debt, a snitch's brand: nobody starts the night wearing them.
  5. Get Your Cut:
    A rising kingpin's bankroll: take $1,500 cash and 6 Influence markers into your Reserves. Day 1's Shadows funds your Ledger like any other morning: stake first, fund second.
  6. Turn Order (Who Strikes First):
    Take one set of four Turn Tokens as today's set and deal them at random (#1 upwards) to set the morning's initiative. Keep the other set nearby as tomorrow's set, claimed one by one as players Lay Low. In this city, the fast eat the dead.
  7. Ferment the First Mash:
    Roll the Mash die and leave it on the board: Day 1's Public Mash, fermenting since before you sat down.

Gameplay: Run the Streets

Days unfold in three phases:

Phase 1: Shadows

The city wakes to the hiss of steam.

1. The Brew

The Mash die already sits on the board, rolled at the end of yesterday's Shadows (Day 1: at Setup). The boss holding Turn Token #1 rolls the communal pool: Red dice equal to Player Count + 1 (4 players: 5 Red).

  1. Read the Public Mash: The grey Mash die shows the Public Mash: the cheap molasses swill off the docks that every crew in the city is cooking today. It stays put all morning and feeds every number below.
  2. Draft a Red, in Turn Order: Each boss takes exactly 1 Red die, their own yeast in the city's sugar. Your Brew Number is Mash + your Red.
  3. Fire your Stills: Every active Still you Control matching your Brew Number brews Moonshine, per The Muscle Ratio. Your brew is yours alone; rival Stills stay cold. (Dry Brew: If the grey supply runs dry mid-brew, take only the barrels that remain, splitting the last of them across your firing Stills as you choose. Any yield past the final barrel in the bank is lost.)

Twist the Valves: If your Boss stands in a District when its Still fires, that Still brews +1 Barrel. The old man cranks the valves wide for one more run, with his hands on the boiler if it blows.

2. The Blowback

One Red die is left undrafted. Add that leftover Red to the Mash for the Blowback Number. Cheap pipes, high pressure, and the boilers blow: the Blowback is communal, so every active, Controlled Still matching it bursts, whoever owns the block.

In each bursting District, tally casualties by The Muscle Ratio. No Runner can throw himself over an explosion: if your Boss stands on a bursting Still, he is the first to fall. The survivors patch the boiler and hold on: they keep their barrels, keep their turf, and the Still runs again tomorrow.

Kingpin's Tip: The Mash die has been public since yesterday, so every Red in the pool is a Blowback waiting to happen. Sometimes the smart draft isn't the die that fires your best Still; it's the one you daren't leave behind to burn it down. The first seat brews richest. The last seat is left holding the match, and takes first pick of the morning's Jobs for its trouble.

3. Grease the Wheels

The Offers: In reverse Turn Order (last off the street last night picks first), each boss does one of three things, never two:

  • Take a Job: Claim one from the Market, stake Influence from your Reserves equal to its Stake, and deal a fresh card into the gap (deck dry? shuffle the discards into a new one). No work for a rat: a boss holding the Rat Card cannot take Jobs at all, though he may still Walk Away.
  • Walk Away: Drop a Job you hold, discard it, and take its staked markers back to your Reserves.
  • Nothing at all: Stand pat. The Market keeps until morning.

You may hold as many Jobs at once as your Reserves can stake. No Boss, no business: A crew with no Boss skips this step entirely.

Fund the Ledger: Move whatever's left in your Reserves into the Ledger, up to its 5 slots: only Ledger markers pay for Plays, so never hold any back. Staked markers stay locked on their Jobs. Stake first, fund second.

4. Ferment Tomorrow's Mash

Bootlegging takes time, and cheap mash ferments overnight. Set tomorrow's Public Mash and leave the Mash die on the board: whoever holds the Harbormaster Title owns the molasses trade and names any face they like (1–6); if nobody holds it, roll the die. Either way, you'll play the whole day knowing tomorrow's number, so seize the right Stills, park the Boss, and Lay Low early for Turn Token #1 while the intel is fresh.

The Moral: The Sicilians brewed richest and lost their Boss to the very same number. 7 paid them and 7 killed them, and they could see the second 4 sitting in the pool the whole time. Worse, tomorrow morning they have no Boss on the board, so they skip The Offers until they Rise. The first seat brews richest. The last seat holds the match.

Phase 2: The Hustle

Players act in Turn Order, taking one Play each. After your Play, the turn passes to the next boss; play keeps going around the table until everyone has Laid Low for the Day.

On Your Turn: make one Play
  1. Make a Play. Spend Influence on one Standard Play or Power Play (see the tables below).
  2. Resolve any combat the Play triggers, fully, before the Play ends.
  3. Pay the cost. Return the spent marker(s) from your Ledger to your Reserves, unless the Play drew Heat, in which case 1 marker locks onto the Heat Track instead.
Out of Influence (or stepping off early)? Lay Low
  1. Odd Jobs: Each unspent marker still in your Ledger pays you $100 (Reserve markers don't pay).
  2. Clear any remaining Ledger markers to your Reserves.
  3. Claim the lowest-numbered token left in tomorrow's set (claim #1 to strike first tomorrow).

Plays: Working the Angles

Standard Plays (Cost: 1 Influence)
Move Send any number of Mobsters and/or Liquor barrels to one Connected District. Barrels can ride with your muscle or travel alone, working your distribution network.

Into rival turf: Your Mobsters are Pinned and the Occupier gets a Standoff choice first (see Combat); barrels ride in with them, and any left in a rival's District become theirs. Thanks for the donation.

Undefended HQ: Move onto a rival Safehouse with no Mobsters guarding it and it's yours to take over or raze: no Standoff, no shots fired. Leave the door unlocked and someone walks off with the house.

The Blue Wall: You cannot Move into a District held by a Police Squad.
Recruit Hire Runners into the District holding your Safehouse. $500 per Runner, less $100 for every Ward you Control anywhere on the map, so all five Wards buy your muscle free. With no Safehouse on the board you cannot Recruit at all, at any price. Your Safehouse sets the spawn point; your Wards set the price.
Secure Place your Safehouse in any Safe District for $500, relocating it if it was already on the board. Placing a Safehouse grants immediate Control, but leave it unguarded and a rival can step in to take it over or raze it for free. You only run one hideout, so Securing new turf means abandoning the old.
Trade At a Dock District you Control, swap any number of Moonshine barrels sitting in that District for Havana Rum, at a 1:1 ratio. A quiet dockside exchange, and the only way Rum enters the game. (Dry Dock: Rum is a fixed 15-barrel pool. You can only Trade up to as many barrels as remain in the Brown supply; when the Rum bank is empty, the docks stay dry until sold Rum cycles back.) Rum is the good stuff: it pays a Kickback when Unloaded, and it is the only bottle the High Society rooms will pour. Trade up, or watch the rich crews lap you.
Beg When the walls close in, take a trip to see Shylock. He deals one Mark at a time; walk in and choose:

The Loan: Take $1,500 from the bank and one of his Marks. There's no limit on how many Marks you carry, but Shylock keeps only 7 in his book; when all 7 are out on the street, no one Begs again until one comes back. Seven marks in his book, and seven's the only number Shylock trusts.

Square Up: Pay $2,000 to the bank to clear one debt, then discard that Mark. Shylock lends in fifteens and collects in twenties, and making the drop takes the whole trip.

The Toll (always in force): While any Mark of yours is unpaid you cannot be crowned (see A Kingpin Must Have Honor).

Power Plays

Power Plays cost double, but still count as a single Play in the turn cycle.

Power Plays (Cost: 2 Influence)
Unload The Address Sets the Price: At a Speakeasy you Control, sell your liquor for cash. A Standard Speakeasy takes Moonshine or Havana Rum at a flat $300 per barrel. A High Society joint takes Havana Rum only, no swill past the velvet rope, but pays a massive $500 per barrel. The rich drink Rum, and they can tell the difference.

The Kickback: Moonshine pays cash only; Havana Rum pays cash plus 1 Kickback per barrel: for each Rum sold, move 1 spent Influence marker from your Reserves into an empty Ledger slot. Since the High Society rooms deal in Rum alone, every premium sale pays a Kickback on top of its $500.

The Greed Tax: Unloading 4+ barrels in one Play generates Heat. If two mobs' markers paid for it, you choose which goes to the Heat Track. Move more than three and the cops come sniffing.
Bribe Everyone on the Commission has a price, and you pay it in cash. Pay the bank $2,500 and take 1 permanent Influence marker for your Reserves. You may Bribe only up to a ceiling of 10 total Influence (see Winning the Game).
Extort Shake down the locals. Once per Day, earn a flat $200 for each District you Control anywhere on the map. Brutal, profitable, and loud: the Heat comes however many Districts pay.
Rat There's no honor among thieves when the sirens wail. Drop a dime on your rivals: trigger an immediate Police Raid, and whoever fired the last loud shot bleeds worst (see Police Raids).

The Mark of the Snitch: Ratting is the ultimate street sin. Making this Play, you take the Rat Card from the supply (or snatch it from the rival who holds it). Nobody brings work to a rat: While you hold the card you cannot take Jobs at The Offers, cannot Rat again, and cannot be crowned (see A Kingpin Must Have Honor). You may still Walk Away from a Job you already hold. The word's out that you talk, and the work goes to quieter men.

Buying Absolution: At any time you may return 1 Influence marker from your Reserves to the supply to discard the Rat Card (you can never fall below 5, see Influence). Otherwise it clings until a rival Rats and takes it off you. Somebody has to be paid to forget, and the price is a piece of your throne.
Rise A Boss goes where he's needed, or a new one takes the chair. Place your Boss in any Safe District, relocating him if he was already on the board and anointing a successor if he was not. The car pulls up on a different block, and the men on that corner stand a little straighter.

Mob Signature Plays

Four families, four ways to make a killing. Each runs on an always-on Trait, the thing that makes them them, plus two signature Plays no rival can copy.

Sicilian Syndicate

Old blood, older rules. An insult to the family is a death sentence, and the family is Untouchable: the precinct captains are on the payroll, so their Districts may hold any number of Mobsters at Reckoning: the Sweep never touches them. Where the Syndicate plants its flag, no one talks them down.

Hit (Cost: 2) The family doesn't fight foot soldiers; it goes for the head. While Pinned in a rival District, initiate a special attack. Both sides roll simultaneously as normal. However, if you score any hits, the rival Boss takes the first hit instead of a Runner. Generates Heat if you kill.
Consigliere (Cost: 1) One phone call and the trouble disappears. Remove 1 Influence marker of your choice from the Heat Track and return it to its owner's Reserves (slide the rest down to close the gap).

Hell's Kitchen Irish

They brew it strong and fight dirty, and they always come heavy. That's their Firepower: whenever the Irish Open Fire, they roll 1 extra attack die (up to the max of 5). Strictly an invader's tool: it never fires on defence, not even an Ambush. Why buy bullets when a brick works just fine, and why bring one gun when you can bring two?

Plunder (Cost: 1) Initiate a special attack (roll Muscle as for Open Fire): each hit steals 1 Liquor barrel of your choice from the target District instead of killing. Carry them off if you Advance (dropped if you Fall Back). Occupier hits still kill. You may also Plunder on an Ambush (Cost: 0). Never generates Heat.
Peddle (Cost: 1) Rotgut sells itself in the tenements. Unload as normal, but in any Ward you Control as well as Speakeasies. Only Moonshine may be sold this way, at the flat $300 per barrel street rate (no Kickback, no High Society price). The Greed Tax still bites at 4+ barrels.

East Side Vipers

They move like smoke and strike like snakes. Nothing happens in this city that the Vipers don't overhear first, and those Whispers are their edge: when they claim a Job, they may peek at the top card of the Jobs deck and claim it face-down instead of a Market card. Rivals see the Stake, never the job; reveal it the moment it completes. You won't know they're there until the shots come from the shadows.

Stealth (Cost: 1) Move up to 5 Mobsters (no Liquor) into a Connected rival District without triggering a Standoff (the Occupier cannot Ambush or Fold). You cannot end your Play in this state. You must immediately spend another Influence to either Open Fire on the District, or Advance to a Connected Safe District.
Tunnel (Cost: 1) The city runs on rails the cops forgot. Move up to 5 Mobsters (no Liquor) between the District holding your Safehouse and any other District you Control, in either direction. A Play on your own turn, not an escape hatch: you cannot use it to flee combat or a Police Raid.

Harlem Knights

Dressed to kill, literally. The Knights work the water, and no crew moves liquor like they do, because their Network runs every pier from Havana to Harlem. Whenever the Knights Trade at a Dock, they gain 1 extra barrel of Havana Rum for free, on top of the barrels they swap, so long as a Rum barrel is left in the supply to take. Friends on every wharf, and a fresh cask waiting at each one, until the last cask is gone.

Skiff (Cost: 1) Move up to 5 Mobsters from a Coastal District you Control to any other Coastal District. A Coastal District is any District touching water, so this reaches far beyond the Docks. Each Mobster may carry 1 Liquor barrel aboard (up to 5 barrels in all). If you enter a rival District, the standard Standoff applies (the Occupier may Ambush, Hold Fire, or Fold). Skiff is a Play on your own turn, not an escape hatch: you cannot use it to flee combat or a Police Raid.
Torch (Cost: 2) Burn down a Safehouse in a rival District. To perform this Play, you must have at least one Runner Pinned in the target District. Sacrifice one of those Pinned Runners; he strikes the match and goes up with the flames (return him to your supply). The rival's Safehouse is immediately destroyed (return it to their supply). Any rival Mobsters and Liquor in the District are unharmed and retain Control. This Play generates Heat.

Phase 3: Reckoning

Once the guns go quiet, the city settles its accounts. All players resolve this phase simultaneously, evaluating their empires exactly as they stand the moment the Hustle ends.

1. The Sweep

The cops can ignore a crew. They can't ignore an army.
In any District with more than 5 Mobsters, remove excess Runners to bring the count down to 5. (The Sicilian Syndicate is Untouchable and ignores the Sweep entirely.)

2. Stake Your Claim

This is where the crowns change hands. For each Title, whoever leads the category takes it or keeps it; if two or more rivals tie for the lead, or no one qualifies, it slips back to the Supply. (Police never count as rivals; turf they hold counts as empty for every Title check.)

  • Borough Deeds: Most Districts in that Borough.
  • Ward Boss: Most Ward Districts.
  • Harbormaster: Most Dock Districts.
  • Night Mayor: Most Speakeasies (a High Society joint counts).

The Next Day...

If any Kingpin holds all 10 Influence, 15+ Respect, and their honor, the game ends immediately! (see Winning the Game).

If not, the war continues. The Turn Tokens you claimed tonight become today's set, the other set becomes tomorrow's, and the next Day's Shadows Phase begins in the new Turn Order.

Winning the Game: The Final Reckoning

Every crew is grasping for the same brass ring, and the night one of you closes a fist around it, the war for New York is over. The crown falls to the boss who ends a Reckoning Phase holding all 10 Influence, 15+ Respect, and their honor intact. The books open only at Reckoning; a crown seized at noon can still slip away before nightfall.

1. Own the Commission (10 Influence)

Any thug can fire a Tommy gun, but it takes a true operator to buy the whole council. You start with 6; the last four come from the Bribe Play at $2,500 apiece. Where your markers sit on the night doesn't matter, in Reserves, on the Ledger, staked on Jobs or a Handshake, or cooling on the Heat Track; what counts is that you have all ten. Break your word on a staked deal, or buy a rat's absolution, and that marker is forfeit to the supply: one more bribe to pay all over again.

2. Earning Respect

Legitimacy means nothing if the streets don't fear you. Tally the spoils of your empire:

  • Borough Turf: Gain 2 Respect for each Borough Deed you hold.
  • Titles: Gain 2 Respect for each of the Ward Boss, Harbormaster and Night Mayor Titles you hold.
  • Criminal Legends: Gain 1, 3, or 5 Respect for each completed Job in your Respect pile. Turf and jobs are the surest roads to 15.

3. A Kingpin Must Have Honor

The council crowns no Rat and no debtor: while you hold the Rat Card or an unpaid Shylock's Mark, you cannot win, whatever your Respect. In the Blood Oath, neither can your Alliance. Shed the Mark, square the debt, then take the throne.

The Final Standoff

If multiple bosses qualify on the same night, there's no war left to fight; the council simply opens the books and weighs one empire against the other:

  1. Total Respect: The highest Respect takes the crown.
  2. Cold Hard Cash: If deadlocked, the Kingpin with the fattest bankroll buys the throne (include the loose change printed on your Playbook).

There are no shared victories in Moonshine Kingdom. You either rule the city alone, or bleed out in the gutter.

Cooperation: Puppets in the Smoke

Trust is fleeting, but even sharp kingpins need rivals. Every favor granted might be a knife in the back.

Pulling the Strings (Puppeteering)

At any time during the Hustle you may offer Influence from your Ledger to a rival who hasn't yet Laid Low. They may refuse. Accepted markers sit in their Ledger, never past its 5 slots, and they spend them as their own: on their next Play, or right now if they're Pinned and weighing whether to keep firing. This is not a Play and does not end your turn.

  • The Risk: If they spend your marker on a Play that draws Heat, your marker goes to the Heat Track. Otherwise it returns to your Reserves the moment it's spent. If two mobs' markers paid for one Play, the boss making it chooses where to point the finger, even between two lenders.
  • A Loan, Not a Gift: Your markers pay them Odd Jobs like any others in their Ledger, but any unspent ones come straight back to your Reserves when they Lay Low. They never become theirs.

A Binding Handshake

A Mobster's word is cheap. When a deal needs teeth, back it with the one thing you're grasping for: your Influence.

Making the Deal

  1. State the Terms: The terms must be clear, and every deal runs out at the end of the current Day. Nobody's word in this town is good for longer than that.
  2. Stake a Marker: The player making the promise takes 1 Influence marker from their Reserves and places it between both players, where it sits locked until the deal resolves. Skin in the game, and everyone can see it.

Example: "My marker's on the table. If you let me pass through your Borough unharmed, I promise to give you $1,000 before Day's end."

Honoring & Breaking Deals

  • Deal Honored: The moment the terms are met, the deal is complete. The staked marker returns to its owner's Reserves.
  • Deal Broken: If the Day ends with the terms unmet, or a player violates the agreement for any reason (including third-party interference), their word becomes garbage. The staked marker is forfeit to the supply, dropping the breaker's Influence by one (never below 5). In this town, a broken promise costs you a piece of your throne.

Deals on the Side

You may wheel and deal outside the rules, but only Cash can be physically traded off the board. Hand it over anytime, no Influence required.

Combat Rules: Blood on the Streets

Violence isn't a backup plan; it's the cost of doing business. Smart Kingpins know a drawn gun beats a fired one. When talk fails, step inside and clear the room.

1. The Rules of Engagement

Violence runs on two numbers: The Muscle Ratio (how many dice you roll) and Threat (what number kills).

Calculating Threat

Your Base Threat is Level 1 (kill on 5+). Bonuses stack, to a maximum of Level 4:

  • Boss +1 Threat: If your Boss is in the fight.
  • Safehouse +2 Threat: The Occupier defending Open Fire on their own Secured District (not on the Ambush shot).
  • Ambush +1 Threat: The Occupier's first shot, on the opening Ambush only.

These bonuses ride on the situation, not the Play, applying to every attack roll you make from that position: Open Fire, Hit or Plunder alike.

2. The Standoff: Crossing the Line

Every firefight is just two questions, asked in order:

  • Step 1, the Occupier answers: Ambush, Hold Fire, or Fold.
  • Step 2, the Invader chooses (and keeps choosing until the Pin is resolved): Open Fire, Advance, or Fall Back.

When an opponent Moves into a District you Control, the game pauses on Step 1. Set each crew along the edge it crossed from, so it stays clear whose Mobsters and barrels are whose.

  1. Ambush: You greet the invaders with lead.
    • Free Shot: Only the Occupier rolls dice.
    • Bonus: Gain +1 Threat.
    • Casualties: Hits remove Invaders (Runners first). No Heat. Invaders are now Pinned.
  2. Hold Fire: You stand your ground but don't shoot. Maybe they're just passing through. The Invaders are now Pinned.
  3. Fold (Cost: 0): You surrender the District. You may immediately move your Mobsters to one Connected Safe District.
    • Panic: Fleeing Mobsters cannot carry Liquor; any barrels are left behind, and your Safehouse stays put.
    • The Takeover: The Invader immediately claims Control, captures dropped Liquor, and may take over or destroy your Safehouse. The Invaders are not Pinned; their Play ends.

3. Pinned: Fight or Flight (Step 2)

A firefight is one Play. Moving in starts it, however many rounds of fire that takes. Each round costs its own Influence, but the whole bloody business counts as the single Play that walked in the door.

While Pinned, your Mobsters cannot make normal Plays. Spend Influence to Open Fire or Advance, or Fall Back for free. Keep choosing until the Pin is resolved, or you run dry and must Fall Back. Hit, Torch, and Plunder still work while Pinned (the Irish Firepower Trait boosts Open Fire only).

The Cost of Pride: A real Kingpin knows an all-out firefight is bad business. Burning 3 or 4 Influence to keep opening fire on one stubborn alley may win it, but it leaves rivals free across the city. Talk is cheap; bullets cost time.

Open Fire (Cost: 1) Fight for the District. Both sides roll simultaneously.
  • Casualties: Both sides apply hits (Runners first). The Occupier loses the Ambush bonus but keeps Safehouse/Boss bonuses.
  • Heat: The Occupier never draws Heat. The Invader gains Heat only if their own dice score 1+ kills.
  • Repeat: If both sides stand, remain Pinned. Spend another Influence to Open Fire again.
Advance (Cost: 1) Shoot your way out. Move Pinned Mobsters, with any barrels they hold, to any Safe Connected District, including across water via Docks. You cannot enter hostile turf.
Fall Back (Cost: 0)
(Invader Only)		 Cut and run. Retreat to the origin District you entered from. Carried Liquor is left behind for the Occupier.
If you have 0 Influence remaining, you must Fall Back.

4. Victory & Consequences

Combat ends when one side is eliminated or the Invader retreats.

If the Occupier Holds: The District remains theirs. Liquor dropped by dead Invaders becomes the Occupier's property.

If the Invader Wins: The moment the last of the Occupier's pieces falls or flees, you claim Control of the District.

  • The Spoils: Any Liquor in the District is now yours.
  • Safehouses: A rival Safehouse in the District is destroyed (remove it), unless you take it over instead: immediately Secure the District for free by relocating your Safehouse there from wherever it sat before.

Heat: Playing with Fire

Heat measures how hard the law is breathing down your neck. The Heat Track is a 5-space powder keg: generate Heat and you place one Influence marker on it. Played right it's a weapon; played wrong, a noose.

Drawing Heat

These noisy Plays push the track up one notch (any Play marked with the flame icon ):

  • Blood on the Pavement: If an Invader's dice inflict 1+ kills in combat, add 1 marker at the end of the Play. A multi-round firefight draws Heat only once.
  • Shakedowns Get Loud: Extort and Torch always draw Heat , however many Districts you squeeze or barns you light.
  • The Greed Tax: Shifting 4+ barrels in a single Play, by Unload or Peddle, draws Heat .

Police Raids: The Boiling Point

A Raid triggers two ways: the 5th marker lands on the track, or a rival makes the Rat Play. Either way, pause the turn; the city's on lockdown.

A Raid puts every Squad on the move. Resolve them one at a time, in the numbered Borough order printed on the board, each finishing before the next stirs. Four precincts kicking doors in, block by block.

1. The Most Wanted List

Each Squad hunts within reach and chases the freshest gunshot it can hear. Its reach is only the Districts directly Connected to it inside its own Borough. Find the target in two steps:

  1. Pick the mob (Follow the Smoke): Of the mobs holding those in-reach Districts, target the one whose marker sits furthest right on the Heat Track. If no District in reach carries any Heat, the Squad stays put. Quiet money always walks free.
  2. Pick the District (The Big Bust): If that mob holds one in-reach District, that's the target. If it holds several, the Squad kicks in the one with the most Barrels (cops follow the contraband); on a tie, the hottest boiler, ranked highest on its Borough's printed Pressure Strip. The louder the boiler, the harder the door comes off.

2. The Scatter

The Squad takes one step onto the chosen District and kicks the doors in:

  • Condemned: Any Safehouse there is destroyed and all Liquor seized (both to supply).
  • Run For It: The crew bolts to a single Connected Safe District, never back into the one the Squad advanced from, and no grabbing barrels on the way.
  • Cornered: If there's no Connected Safe District to flee to (every exit blocked by rivals, water, the Blue Wall, or the map's edge), the whole crew is arrested and returned to your supply. Leave a back door, or don't leave at all.

The Squad now holds the District as an impassable wall, padlocking any Speakeasy or Still beneath it until a later Raid. A crew fleeing one Squad may land in the path of the next; the night is young.

The Aftermath: Once every Squad has moved, all Heat markers return to their owners' Reserves and the turn resumes.

The Blood Oath: 4-Player Team Variant

When the heat boils over, enemies become brothers. The Feds are kicking down doors and lone wolves get slaughtered. The Blood Oath is the recommended 4-player mode: a cutthroat free-for-all where escalating police pressure eventually forces the city into two permanent 2v2 alliances.

Setup: The Ticking Clock

Set up as a standard 4-player match, with one addition: place the Federal Crackdown Tracker near the Heat Track with a token on space 1. Every time a Police Raid triggers, advance the token one space.

The Sit-Down

The moment the token reaches space 10 on the Crackdown Tracker, the underworld snaps. Pause the game. The four independent mobs immediately form two permanent alliances.

  1. Rank the Bosses: Tally everyone's current Respect (see Earning Respect). Tiebreaker: Total Cash on hand.
  2. Forge the Families: The player in 1st Place joins forces with the player in 4th Place. The player in 2nd Place pairs with the player in 3rd Place.
  3. Set the Targets: Each Alliance adds 10 to its combined Respect: that number is its Target. From here, it's a clean race to earn 10.

New Rules of Engagement

One goal, two crews. You still run your own operations.

  • Separate Ledgers: You do not share Cash, Influence, or Jobs, and you still take individual turns in Turn Order.
  • Blood is Thicker: You cannot attack your partner or their property (no Ambush, Open Fire, Hit, Plunder, or Torch). All other rules stand: partner turf is still Hostile (Pinned there, your only outs are Advance or Fall Back), the Golden Rule of Turf means you cannot share a District at Shadows or Reckoning, and Liquor Moved into their District becomes theirs.
  • Puppeteering: Your strongest weapon. Lend Influence to your partner's Ledger as normal, and they may still refuse (see Cooperation).

Claiming the Empire

The council only recognizes unified power. To win, an Alliance must reach its Target and have at least one partner holding 10 Influence (one crew buys the council on behalf of the family). If met during the Reckoning Phase, the game ends immediately: first family to the finish takes the city.

The New Kings of New York

If both Alliances hit their Targets at the same Reckoning, the crown goes to the pair furthest over its Target; still deadlocked, the fattest combined bankroll buys the throne.

The Capo di Tutti Capi: There are no truly shared victories... even in a team. Within the winning Alliance, the player who contributed the most Respect to the final combined total is declared the Boss of Bosses and the true Kingpin of New York. Your partner doesn't get a crown; they just get the privilege of kissing your ring.

The Volstead Act: 2-Player Prequel

Before the Kingdom, there was the Act. The ink on the Volstead Act is barely dry, and the judges still believe in it. There are no Jobs to pull, no Respect to earn, only two upstart outfits, a river of mash, and a bench that hangs you for a loud night's work. The Volstead Act is the 2-player mode: when the city shrinks to two, backroom politics give way to cold capital, and the game becomes a tight supply-chain duel run on a doomsday clock.

Setup: Two Upstarts

  1. Two outfits only. Set the East Side Vipers aside, they haven't formed yet. Each player takes one of the remaining three Mobs (Sicilians, Irish, or Knights); draft or choose.
  2. Carve up the city as normal. Run the standard Setup in full: deal the Borough Deeds to establish home turf, garrison the Districts the Town Planning Ledger names, raise the Blue Wall, take your cash and Influence, and deal the Turn Tokens. Two upstarts on a four-Borough map: the empty half of the city is the point.
  3. No Jobs, no Respect. Return all Job Cards to the box; skip The Offers each morning and go straight to funding the Ledger. Respect is not tracked.
  4. One title stands. The Deeds have done their work: now return every Borough Deed to the box, and every Title but the Harbormaster (whoever holds the most Docks still sets Tomorrow's Mash). In this young city, only the river carries a name worth having.
  5. Start the clock. Place the Federal Crackdown Tracker by the Heat Track with a token on space 1. Every Police Raid advances it one space, exactly as in The Blood Oath.

A Stricter Bench

The law is green and twitchy, and it watches the docks like a hawk. Three rules bend for the era:

  1. No shakedowns. The Extort Play is unavailable. Every dollar must run through the still, the docks, and the speakeasy, there are no open rackets yet.
  2. The Greed Tax bites at three. Moving 3+ barrels in a single Play (by Unload or Peddle) draws Heat (down from 4+).
  3. The city boils over fast. The Heat Track is a 3-space fuse: the 3rd marker (or a Rat) triggers a Police Raid. Ignore the 4th and 5th spaces. A loud night gets the doors kicked in before dawn.

Two Roads Out

There is no legend to forge here, only two ways to end up on top:

  • Buy the Bench. The instant you hold all 10 Influence markers and your honor, the game ends and you win. The quiet road: bribe your way to the top before the Feds ever find you.
  • The Reckoning Count. The moment the Crackdown token reaches space 10, finish the current Day, then open the books. The richest honest Kingpin takes the city, loose change and all (include the value printed on your Playbook, exactly as in the main game). No two bankrolls can tie.

A Kingpin Must Have Honor: neither road crowns a Rat or a debtor. While you hold the Rat Card or a single unpaid Shylock's Mark you cannot take the city, however fat your bankroll. Square up first, at $2,000 a Mark and a whole Play to make the drop: borrowed money spends like anyone else's, but it is not yours when the books open. Shylock lends in fifteens and collects in twenties, and this bench counts every penny. If the Count arrives with nobody clean, the Feds take the city and every outfit goes down with it.

The two roads fight each other on the same clock. The briber sells sparingly and keeps the streets quiet, stalling the count; the profiteer sells hard to bank the most cash, and every loud sale drags the Feds a step closer to ending the game on their terms. You cannot walk both roads at once, and each Raid that seizes a rival's staged booze is a payday burned before it could be banked. And watch the third clock: Shylock. A Mark is the fastest cash in the game and the only debt the bench won't overlook, so every loan you take is a Play you owe yourself later, at a worse price, on a deadline you don't control. Beg early, square up before the tenth space, or don't beg at all.
