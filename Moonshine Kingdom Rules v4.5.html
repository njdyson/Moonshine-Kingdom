RULEBOOK

A Game of Bootlegging, Backstabbing, and Gang Warfare.

In the gaslit streets of 1920s New York City, where towering skyscrapers cast long shadows over a bustling underworld, Prohibition has sparked a lawless gold rush. Four deadly mobs battle to rule the city's illicit Liquor trade. Bootlegging, political bribery, and ruthless intimidation are your tools. Muscle is expensive, and you can't be everywhere at once. You must forge fragile alliances, corner the market, and weaponize the Police against your rivals. New York is a treacherous dame with a thirst that can't be quenched. Only one mob will claim her crown. Will you carve your name into the annals of infamy, or vanish like cigar smoke in a hurricane?

Overview

2-4 Players | 90 - 120 Mins

Prohibition didn't stop the drinking; it just made the price go up.

In Moonshine Kingdom, you are a rising Kingpin in 1920s New York. Prohibition has turned off the taps, creating a gold rush for anyone ruthless enough to fill the glass. Four rival mobs claw for the boroughs, and this city isn't big enough for all of you.

A Day in the Life of a Kingpin

Each volatile round represents twenty-four hours in your empire. From the fog of morning to the bloody consequences of night, outmaneuver your rivals to survive.


Morning: The Brew

Shadows Phase

Dawn breaks over the Hudson. While the city sleeps, your operation churns. Fire up your Stills, commit to black market Contracts, and turn mash into liquid gold.

Day: The Hustle

Operations Phase

The streets are open for business. Command your Runners to smuggle product, seize territory, and strong-arm the competition. Every move costs Influence, your political clout. Use it to expand, but remember: violence is loud, and the police are listening.

Night: The Cost

Reckoning Phase

Twilight settles like a bruise. The shooting stops and the ledger opens. Claim Borough Deeds, resolve Contracts, clear out the careless, and settle your accounts. Did you make a killing, or just a mess?

The Only Crown Worth Having

Cash buys bullets. But Respect buys immortality.

You earn Respect by conquering Boroughs, claiming Syndicates, pulling off Contracts, and holding the Ghettos long enough to be named Ward Boss. The first mob to earn 20 and a seat at the High Table claims her crown.

Game Components: Tools of the Trade

Your whole damn operation in miniature. Every piece earns, bleeds, or burns.

The City Map (Game Board): A soot-stained blueprint of 1920s New York, carved into 25 Districts.

  • Heat Track: A shared 5-space powder-keg, tracking Police activity.
  • The Market: The pulse of the city's thirst, showing the street value of every hooch.

Operations Boards: Your command center, where you commit Influence and plot the day's trouble.

Mob Pieces (x4 Sets): Each player claims a color and takes command of:

  • 1 Boss, 15 Runners, and 3 Safehouses: The meat and the grinders.
  • 12 Influence Markers: The currency of the underworld. Favors, fear, and political clout.
  • 1 Mob Playbook: The family secrets.

The Ledger (Cards):

  • Contract Cards: High-risk, high-reward missions that build your legend.
  • Syndicate Cards (4): Each is worth 2 Respect and grants control over that liquor's market price.
  • Borough Deeds: The proof of your dominion over a Borough.
  • Ward Boss Card: Proof you run the Ghettos. While you hold it, Recruit costs $200 per Runner, and it's worth 3 Respect at endgame.
  • Shylock's Mark Cards: A lifeline with teeth. The debt always comes due.
  • Favor Cards: Proof of your ties to the city's elite. Gain them to earn Influence, unlock broker abilities, and cut binding deals.
  • Commission Cards: Your seat at the High table, and your key to victory.

Still Tokens (25): Basement boilers numbered 2-12, waiting for the right roll to churn out hooch.

Turn Tokens (8): New Moon 1-4 and Full Moon 1-4, plus the Moon marker to track turn order.

Liquor Barrels: Liquid gold. The lifeblood of your empire. 25 of each.

  • Bathtub Gin (Pink): The social lubricant of every speakeasy in town.
  • Whisky (Yellow): A gentleman's poison, smooth and profitable.
  • Havana Rum (Brown): The top-shelf stuff. Import only.

The Dice (10): 5 White and 5 Red.

The Bankroll (Cash): Dirty, untraceable bills in denominations of $100, $500, and $1000.

The Law (Blue Pieces): The blue wall that wants to shut you down for good.

  • Police Officers (30) & Precincts (5): The beat cops and their station houses.
  • Federal Crackdown Tracker (1): The 10-space pressure clock used in co-op and team variants.

Your Goal: Earning Respect

Respect is the only currency that matters in Moonshine Kingdom. Earn it two ways: claim Titles by controlling the city, and complete Contracts by pulling off jobs too loud to ignore.

1. Titles: Area Control

Titles are the proof that the street belongs to you. Borough Deeds, Syndicate Cards, and the Ward Boss card all follow the same hard rule:

During Turf Control, if you Control more of a Title's requirement than any single rival, claim its Card, Respect, and perks. Lose the lead, lose the Title; tied or unclaimed Titles return to the Supply. Police do not count as rivals; Police-controlled Districts, Joints, and Ghettos count as empty for Title checks.

Borough Deeds

Seizing a Borough turns muscle into empire. Control more Districts in a Borough than any single rival to claim its Deed. Boroughs are worth 3 Respect, except Staten Island at 2. Deeds also unlock that Borough's Extort action.

Syndicate Cards

The city's 12 Speakeasies form four syndicates. Control more Joints of a liquor type than any single rival to claim its Syndicate Card for 2 Respect and the right to fix that liquor's price.

The market doesn't move unless you force it. A Syndicate holder may use Fix to set that liquor's Market Value.

  • The Ceiling: Set the price from $200 to $200 + $100 per matching Joint you Control (max $500).
  • A Broken Market: If a Syndicate Card is unclaimed, that liquor's Market Value remains frozen at its current price until a new Syndicate holder emerges to change it.

Ward Boss

The Boroughs make headlines, but the Ghettos decide who owns the block. Control more Ghetto Districts than any single rival to claim the Ward Boss card. While you hold it, Recruit costs $200 per Runner, and it's worth 3 Respect at endgame.

2. Contracts: Forge Your Legend

In the cutthroat alleys of New York, a mob's reputation isn't built on good intentions; it's built on Contracts. These are the high-stakes schemes that turn a street thug into a Kingpin.

Each Contract card details an Objective and a strict Deadline. Contracts come in three escalating tiers of ambition:

  • Gigs (1 Day / 1 Respect): Quick street hustles. Keeping the lights on and the cash flowing.
  • Rackets (2 Days / 3 Respect): Mid-level capers. Operations that require turf, muscle, and a plan.
  • Scores (3 Days / 5 Respect): Headline-grabbing heists. The kind of legends they write ballads about.

The Deadline

In this business, timing is everything. When you start a Contract, place Influence markers on the card equal to the deadline number (1, 2, or 3). One marker is returned to your Stash during every Reckoning Phase.

During the Reckoning Phase, if you remove the final marker, The Deadline has arrived. The client only cares about the board state at that exact moment. If the board state matches the card's objectives, the Contract succeeds. Add the card to your Respect pile and claim your Take (see below). If the condition is not met, the Contract is botched; discard the card face-up. No excuses, no reward.

The Take:

When you successfully pull off a Contract, you earn a one-time cash reward - The Take:

  • Gig: $500
  • Racket: $1,500
  • Score: $3,000

The Commission

Completing a Gig, Racket, and Score earns you a seat on The Commission, a requirement to win the game (see Winning the Game).

Key Concepts: The Underworld at a Glance

Your Mob: The Brains, the Brawn, and the Bastards

From the cold steel of the Sicilian Syndicate to the fiery grit of the Hell's Kitchen Irish, each mob brings their own brand of violence to the city's bloodstained ballet. You command:

  • A Boss: Your kingpin. If he gets whacked, your Operation Stalls. Bring him into battle to make your attacks more deadly.
  • Runners: Your foot soldiers. Knuckle-draggers and wheelmen who haul hooch and guard your turf. You are strictly limited to 15 Runners. You cannot build a massive army, and you cannot occupy the whole city. To expand, you must take risks, leave borders thinly guarded, and rely on the threat of violence rather than actual violence.
  • Safehouses: Your fortresses in the chaos. Hidden dens where you stash hooch and Recruit new muscle. They provide cover from Attacks and Police Raids, and allow you to build an efficient supply network.

The Map: Know Your Turf

Your empire is a broken city: 25 Districts across 5 Boroughs, each a flavor of poison and profit. Certain key districts offer unique opportunities:

  • Speakeasies: Marked by a cocktail glass symbol on the board, the city's 12 Speakeasies are divided into 4 types: 3 Gin Joints, 3 Whisky Bars, 3 Moonshine Dens, and 3 Rum Clubs. Each Joint has a House Pour (its signature liquor). These are the only spots where you can Unload liquor for cash and Kickbacks.
  • High Society Districts: Affluent neighborhoods where the rich and powerful reside, and Influence is earned. Marked on the board with a crown symbol. All High Society Districts also have Speakeasies.
  • Ghettos: Gritty districts where mob roots run deep and every alley has ears. Control more Ghettos than any single rival at Reckoning and you claim the Ward Boss card. While you hold it, Recruit costs $200 per Runner. Your Hand Limit for Contract cards is normally 3, or 4 if you hold Texas Guinan's Favor. If you ever hold more than your limit, discard immediately until you're back under the cap. Marked on the board with a fist.
  • Docks: Your link to the city's waterways, crucial to Smuggle Rum and move between distant shores. Marked on the board with an anchor.
  • Stills: Hidden distilleries marked with a number from 2-12 (corresponding to a 2d6 roll). Stills produce Domestic liquor (Gin, Whisky, or Moonshine) when your number is rolled during the Shadows Phase.

The Currencies of the Underworld

To rule the city, you'll need more than just bullets. You'll need to master its three core resources.

Influence: Currency of Power

Bullets get you turf, but Influence buys you an empire. This is your political capital, the favors you're owed, and the fear you command.

Your Influence markers cycle between two zones on your player board:

  • The Stash (Your Reserves): The uncommitted markers stacked in your Stash represent your available clout. You use these to stake new Contracts and fund your daily Operations.
  • Operations (Your Daily Plays): During the Shadows Phase, you move markers from your Stash into your 5 Operations slots. These are the "action points" you spend to make Plays during the day.

You start the game with just enough clout to make some noise (7 markers). The only way to earn more is by Bribing the power brokers found in the city's High Society districts.

Kingpin's Tip: Capacity vs. Time

Earning extra Influence markers does not give you more actions in a Day. Your Operations board is strictly capped at 5 slots. Having a massive Stash of Influence simply means you have the capacity to absorb Heat, survive brutal Police Raids, and stake multiple Contracts at once without crippling your daily hustle. You can't out-action your rivals; you have to out-think them.

Liquid Assets

Cash is king for expanding your crew. You'll spend it to Recruit new Runners and Secure your turf with Safehouses. It's earned by Unloading liquor, Extorting Boroughs, and pulling off Contracts.

Liquor Barrels are the lifeblood of your operation. Produced at your Stills or smuggled from Rum Row, their value fluctuates based on who controls the Syndicates.

Territory: Lines in the Concrete

An empire isn't just bullets and blood; it's about logistics. It's about securing supply lines, moving product before it turns sour, and drawing borders your rivals are too terrified to cross. Because your forces are spread thin, a cleverly placed Safehouse and a handful of loyal guards are often enough to deter a full-scale turf war.

1. Control: Boots on the Ground

You can't tax what you don't hold. You control a District if you have at least one Mobster (Boss or Runner) OR a Safehouse present there.

  • Instant Possession: Control shifts the moment your piece enters a District. (Moving through an empty District claims it for that exact instant).
  • Abandonment: Unattended liquor does not grant control. If your last piece voluntarily leaves or is killed, you immediately lose Control.
  • The Golden Rule of Turf: During the Shadows and Reckoning phases, there can be only one mob in a District. While you hold a District, rivals cannot enter without triggering combat.
  • Contested Ground: During Combat, the original Occupier retains Control (and all defensive bonuses) until their last piece is removed.

2. Connection: The Network

Knowing the layout of the city is key to flanking enemies and moving hooch. Districts are Connected if:

  • Land & Bridges: They share a physical border or are linked by a Bridge.
    (e.g., Williamsburg connects to Red Hook, Brownsville, Corona, and Astoria by land, and to Five Points by Bridge).
  • Waterways (Docks): All Dock Districts connect to each other across the water as if they were adjacent.
    (e.g., You can Move directly from West Side to Thorggs Neck in one Play).

Setup: Carving Out Your Corner

  1. Prepare the City:
    • Place the Game Board and prepare the supply of Cash and Dice.
    • Prepare the Liquor Supply. Set starting market values: Rum $500, Whisky $400, Gin $300, Moonshine $200.
    • In every District, randomly place a face-down Still token, then flip them all over.
  2. Stack the Ledger:
    • Shuffle each Contract Card deck (Gigs, Rackets, Scores) and place them face-down next to the board.
    • Place the Shylock's Mark cards next to the Contract Ledger.
    • Place all other card decks in the Supply.
  3. Claim Your Crew:
    Shuffle the Playbooks and deal 1 to each player to assign mobs. Players take all corresponding mob pieces; 1 Boss (2 if Sicilian), 15 Runners and 3 Safehouses plus an Operations Board.
  4. Establish Turf:
    First remove Staten Island (nobody starts here), then shuffle the remaining Borough Deeds. Each player draws one card to determine their starting Borough. Place any unclaimed Deeds in the Supply, along with Staten Island. Place your starting pieces on the board:
    1. In your starting Borough's Ghetto District, place 1 Safehouse, 1 Boss, and 2 Runners.
    2. In any Speakeasy in the starting Borough, place 3 Runners.
    3. In any Dock District in the starting Borough, place 3 Runners.
    4. The Blue Wall: Finally, place 1 Police Precinct and 3 Officers in every Ghetto District that was not selected by a player.
  5. Get Your Cut:
    Players receive $1,500 cash and 7 Influence markers. Place 5 markers on your Operations slots, and the other 2 on your Stash slot.
  6. Determine Turn Order:
    Randomly deal the New Moon Turn Tokens to determine the starting order. Place the Full Moon Turn Tokens nearby to form the pool for the next round. Set the Moon marker to New Moon.

Swimming with Sharks?

If you're a fresh face at a table of veterans, have the experienced players allow you an extra Influence marker at the start of the game. It's a simple handicap that levels the playing field and keeps the blood flowing fairly.

Gameplay: Run the Streets

Rounds unfold in 3 phases: Shadows (Production), Operations (Plays), and Reckoning (Cleanup).

Turn Order: Who Strikes First

In this city, the fast eat the slow. Turn order is tracked using numbered Turn Tokens (#1 through #4). Whenever a rule says in Turn Order, player #1 acts first, down to #4.

To track the passing days, the game alternates between the New Moon and Full Moon token sets. When you run out of Influence or choose to end your turn early (Lay Low), immediately swap your current token for the lowest available Turn Token of the opposite moon phase. The first boss to step off the streets tonight strikes first tomorrow.

Phase 1: Shadows

The city wakes to a hiss of steam. In Turn Order, each boss scrambles for product and fresh deals.

1. Brew

The player holding Turn Token #1 rolls the communal dice pool to fire up the boilers:
Roll Red dice and White dice each equal to the Player Count + 1.
(e.g. In a 4-player game, roll 5 Red and 5 White dice).

The Churn: In Turn Order, each boss takes one turn to draft dice and push product onto the streets:

  • Draft: Snatch 1 Red and 1 White die from the pool. Add them together to get your Brew Number.
  • Produce: Every Still on the board matching your Brew Number fires up immediately, including your rivals' Stills Place matching Domestic Liquor barrels on the active Stills based on the crew present: 1-2 Mobsters = 1 Barrel, 3-4 Mobsters = 2 Barrels, 5+ Mobsters = 3 Barrels.
  • Dry Tap: The city's glass is only so deep. If the physical supply of a specific liquor runs out, production stops. First to draft gets the goods; the rest get excuses.

The Blowback: Greed has a price. Once every boss has drafted, there will be exactly 1 Red die and 1 White die left in the gutter. Add these two remaining dice together. This is the Blowback Number. The overworked pressure valves violently explode at every Still across the city matching this number!

  • The Blast: Scalding mash and shrapnel tear through the matching Districts. Roll call the casualties using The Muscle Ratio (1-2 mobsters = 1 dead; 3-4 = 2 dead; 5+ = 3 dead).

Note: Runners always take the hit first. If a Boss is caught in the blast and there are no Runners left to shield them, the Boss goes home in a pine box.

2. Grease the Wheels

  • Start Contracts: You may start Contracts from your hand by revealing the card face-up and placing markers from your Stash equal to the Days to Deadline (1, 2, or 3) on top.
  • Capacity: You may run as many Contracts as you have the Influence for.
  • Fund Operations: After staking Contract markers, move up to 5 markers from your Stash into your Operations slots. Any markers left in your Stash cannot be used for actions this Day.

Phase 2: Operations Phase

Operations runs in Turn Order, one Play at a time.

  1. Standard Play: Spend Influence to perform 1 Play (Move, Recruit, Shift, etc.) or 1 Power Play (e.g. Unload).
  2. Combat Play: If combat is triggered, resolve it fully before the Play ends.
  3. Paying for Plays: Remove the spent marker(s) from your Operations and return them to your Stash. If the Play generates Heat, move the marker to the Heat Track instead.
  4. Laying Low: When you run out of Influence (or choose to step off the street early), you must Lay Low:
    1. Clear any remaining markers from your Operations to your Stash.
    2. Swap your current token for the lowest available Turn Token of the opposite moon phase.
Standard Plays (Cost: 1 Influence)
Move Move any number of mobsters (carrying max 1 barrel each) to one Connected District. Entering rival or Police turf triggers Combat.
Shift ($100) Move any number of barrels from your District to any Connected District. Shifted into rival turf? It's their hooch now.
Recruit Hire Runners at your Safehouse for $300 per Runner. Cost drops to $200 per Runner if you are the Ward Boss.
Hustle Boss must be in play. Draw 2 Contracts. Discard down to your Hand Limit (default: 3). Limit: once per Day.
Secure ($500) Place a new Safehouse in your District, or relocate an existing one.
Smuggle At a Dock District you Control: Send up to 5 Mobsters from the Dock to roll for Havana Rum (failed dice = arrests; Runners take the fall first; 3 Rum = 1 Heat).
Title Plays (Cost: 1 Influence)
Extort Borough Deed required. Once per Day, earn $200 per District you Control in that Borough. Earning $500+ generates 1 Heat.
Fix Syndicate Card required. Set that liquor's Market Value between $200 and $200 + $100 per matching Joint you Control.

Power Plays

These Plays cost two Influence markers to perform, but count as a single Play in the turn cycle.

Power Plays (Cost: 2 Influence)
Unload Cash Out: Sell barrels at a Speakeasy you Control for their current Market Value.

The Kickback: Speakeasies pay in favors for their favorite poison. For every barrel sold that matches the Joint's House Pour, immediately move 1 spent Influence marker from your Stash back into an empty slot on your Operations Board. (Note: Your board caps at 5 slots. Any Kickbacks earned beyond your empty slots are wasted).

The Greed Tax: Unloading 4+ barrels of any type in a single Play generates 1 Heat. Max safe delivery is 3, unless you hold Stephanie St. Clair's Favor.
Rat Tip off the cops to trigger an immediate Police Raid and reset the Heat Track. Since you fed them the info, you may adjust the 2D6 Raid Roll by +1 or -1 to target a rival's operation (or spare your own).
Crown Boss got whacked? Appoint a new one ($0) in any safe (Controlled or Empty) District. (Because every mob is strictly limited to 15 Runners, it is incredibly rare for the 25-District board to be completely occupied, ensuring there is almost always a dark corner to rise from).

HQ: You may also Secure your new Boss's District for free.
Bribe Pay off one of the City's Power brokers in a High Society District you control for $2,500.

Take that District's Favor card and gain 1 permanent Influence marker in your Stash. Each player may buy each High Society Favor once per game. You keep it even if you later lose the District.

Mob Signature Plays

Every crew has tricks up its sleeve: signature moves to outfox, out-muscle, or outrun the competition. Each mob has two unique plays.

Sicilian Syndicate

Old blood, older rules. An insult to the family is a death sentence.

Capo (Cost: 2 Influence) Promote a Runner from any District to Capo (switch Runner for the spare Boss piece). Your Capo is a second Boss, providing stability and gaining +1 Threat in Combat (+2 when both Bosses attack one District). You may only have 1 Capo (2 Bosses total).
Consigliere (Cost: 1 Influence) Use your connections to smooth over legal troubles. Remove 1 Influence marker of your choice from the Heat Track and return it to its owner's Stash (move other markers down to fill gaps if required).

Hell's Kitchen Irish

They brew it strong and fight dirty. Why buy bullets when a brick works just fine?

Courage (Cost: 0 Influence) Before you roll in a fight, you may discard 1 barrel of your Liquor in the District where the battle is taking place to gain +1 Threat for that roll (max 4).
Plunder (Cost: 1 Influence) Initiate a special attack. For each successful hit, instead of eliminating a mobster, steal 1 Liquor barrel of your choice from the target District. You can carry these away if you Advance. Defender hits still kill. You may also choose to Plunder when you Ambush (Cost: 0 Influence). Plunder does not create Heat.

East Side Vipers

They move like smoke and strike like snakes. You won't know you're at war until your warehouse is burning.

Stealth (Cost: 1 Influence) Move up to 5 mobsters (no liquor) into a connected rival district without triggering an Ambush. You cannot end your Play in this state. You must immediately spend another Influence to either: Assault the district, or Advance to a connected safe district.
Torch (Cost: 2 Influence) Burn down a safehouse in a rival District which has 3 or less mobsters defending it. To perform this Play, you must have at least one Runner Pinned in the target District. Sacrifice one of those Pinned Runners; he is removed from the game. The rival's Safehouse is immediately destroyed (return it to their supply). Any rival mobsters and liquor in the District are unharmed and retain Control. This Play generates Heat.

Harlem Knights

Dressed to kill, literally. The Knights write letters with with Chicago Typewriters.

Skiff (Cost: 1 Influence) Move up to 5 mobsters (with or without liquor) from a Coastal District you control to any other Coastal District. This movement does not require Docks. If you enter a Rival District, standard Ambush rules apply.
Firepower Pay $200 per attack to increase your firepower. When you Assault or Ambush, you roll one additional attack die (up to a maximum of 5). Can't be used when defending an Assault.

Phase 3: Reckoning Phase

Once all players have clocked out of Operations, the city settles accounts. Each Reckoning stage is resolved simultaneously by all players, but the stages must be completed in order.

1. The Sweep

The night patrol sweeps the streets, breaking up large gang gatherings. Any District with more than 5 Mobsters faces arrests. Remove excess Runners to bring the count down to 5, unless you hold Mayor Jimmy Walker's Favor.

2. Claim Turf

Settle the night's grabs all at once.

  • Borough Deeds: If a mob controls more Districts in a Borough than any single rival, the Deed is transferred to the new owner. If tied or unclaimed, the Deed returns to the Supply.
  • Syndicate Cards: If a player controls more Joints of a type than any single rival, they claim that liquor's Syndicate Card. If tied or unclaimed, the Syndicate Card returns to the Supply.
  • Ward Boss: If a player controls more Ghetto Districts than any single rival, they claim the Ward Boss card. If tied or unclaimed, the card returns to the Supply.

3. Fulfill Contracts

Remove 1 marker from each active Contract and return it to your Stash. Contract objectives are checked only after Turf Control has been resolved.

  • Completed: If the final marker was removed and you met the objectives, add it to your Respect pile and collect the Take. Commission Check: If this newly completed Contract secures you a full portfolio (1 Gig, 1 Racket, and 1 Score), immediately take a Commission Card.
  • Failed: If the final marker was removed but you missed the objective, discard the card to a face-up discard pile.
  • Abandon: You may choose to cut your losses on an active Contract. Return the Influence markers to your Stash and discard the card to a face-up discard pile.

4. Endgame

Check if any Kingpin has a Commission Card AND 20+ Respect. If they have, the game ends immediately! (See the Winning the Game section).

If not, the war continues. Flip the Moon Phase token and begin the next round's Shadows Phase in Turn Order.

Winning the Game: The Final Reckoning

The war for New York ends the moment a player secures a Commission Card (by completing a Gig, Racket, and Score) AND amasses 20 or more Total Respect.

1. A Seat at the High Table

Any thug can fire a Tommy gun, but it takes a true mastermind to sit at the High Table. When a newly completed Contract secures you a full portfolio: 1 Gig, 1 Racket, and 1 Score, immediately take a Commission Card. A player may only ever claim one.

2. Earning Respect

Legitimacy means nothing if the streets don't fear you. You need 20 Respect to claim the crown. To calculate your current Respect, tally up the spoils of your empire:

  • Borough Turf: Gain 3 Respect for each Controlled Borough (2 Respect for Staten Island).
  • Ward Boss: Gain 3 Respect if you hold the Ward Boss card at the end of the game.
  • Criminal Legends: Gain 1, 3, or 5 Respect for each successfully completed Contract in your ledger.
  • Market Dominance: Gain 2 Respect for each Syndicate Card you currently hold.
  • Blood Debts: Subtract 1 Respect for every unpaid Loan owed to Shylock. (Shylock always gets his due).

The Final Standoff

If multiple bosses claim a Commission Card and 20+ Respect on the same night, the streets run red to settle the score:

  1. Total Respect: The highest overall Respect takes the crown.
  2. Cold Hard Cash: If deadlocked, the Kingpin with the fattest bankroll buys the throne (include the loose change printed on your Playbook).

There are no shared victories in Moonshine Kingdom. You either rule the city alone, or you bleed out in the gutter.

Heat: Playing with Fire

Heat measures how much the law's breathing down your neck. The Heat Tracker is a 5-space communal track. When you generate Heat, you must place one Influence marker on the Heat track. Played right, Heat is a weapon. Played wrong, it's a noose.

Gaining Heat

These noisy Plays Heat up the streets one notch:

  • Blood on the Pavement: Aggression draws unwanted attention. If the Attacker in a combat Play (the player who initiated Combat) inflicts 1 or more kills, they must add 1 marker to the Heat track at the conclusion of the Play.
    Note: A multi-round Assault is still a single Play and generates a maximum of 1 Heat. Defenders acting in self-defense (during an Ambush or an Assault) never generate Heat.
  • Shakedown Gets Loud: If an Extort Play earns $500+, you generate 1 Heat.
  • The Greed Tax: Triggering The Greed Tax by Unloading 4+ barrels generates 1 Heat.
  • Torch: Using the Vipers Torch Play.

Police Raids

A raid is triggered when a player uses the Rat Play, or the moment a 5th marker hits the Heat Track (reaching Boiling Point). Pause the current turn immediately and roll 2D6. The result targets every matching Still on the board.

  1. Target Mob Turf (Raids): For each hit District controlled by a Player:
    • Seizures: All Liquor in the District is confiscated and returned to the supply, unless protected by a Safehouse.
    • Fines: The occupying mob must pay a flat fine of $500. If you can't pay, you must take a Shylock's Mark card.
    • Arrests: Count the mobsters in the hit District and apply arrests using The Muscle Ratio (1-2 = 1 arrested, 3-4 = 2 arrested, 5+ = 3 arrested). Safehouses do not protect against arrests.
  2. Target Empty/Police Turf (Blockades): For each hit District that is Empty or Police-Controlled:
    • Empty District: The Police move in. Place 3 Officers in the District. If that Borough does not currently have a Precinct anywhere inside it, place a Precinct here as well.
    • Police-Controlled District: Reinforcements arrive. If the District currently contains 3 or fewer Officers, add 2 Officers.
  3. Cool Down: Once the Raid is fully resolved, all Influence markers on the Heat Track are returned to their owners' Stashes, and the paused turn resumes.

Supply Lines: Smuggling from Rum Row

Smuggling Runs

Havana Rum is top-shelf poison, and it can't be brewed in a basement. It has to be smuggled through the city's waterways.

Smuggle (Cost: 1 Influence)

At a Dock District you Control, you may run the blockade to import premium Havana Rum.

  1. Assign Crew: Send up to 5 Mobsters from the Dock.
  2. Check Network: Count your Controlled Docks to find your Target Number on the Smuggling Table.
  3. Roll & Resolve: Roll your dice pool (based on Crew Size via The Muscle Ratio).
    • Success: For every die that meets or beats your Target Number, gain 1 Rum. Place them in the Dock District.
    • Intercepted: The Coast Guard is on the prowl. For every die that fails to meet the Target Number, 1 Mobster is arrested and removed from the board (Runners always take the fall first).
    • Making Waves: If you successfully smuggle 3 Rum in a single Play, the massive shipment draws unwanted attention. You generate 1 Heat.

Example: You control 3 Docks, and decide to Smuggle some rum from one of them. You send a crew of 5, which gives you 3 dice and a target of 3+ on each die. You roll 1, 3 & 5 and gain 2 barrels of Rum, which are added to the Dock District. The failed die gets 1 Mobster arrested, and you escape with the rest of the shipment.

The Muscle Ratio: These are the same Resolution Tables shown on each Playbook card. Whenever a rule counts a group size to determine a result, the streets pay out by the numbers: 1-2 = 1, 3-4 = 2, 5-6 = 3. Use this for Brewing, Combat, Smuggling, Blowbacks, and Raid arrests.

Favors: Deals with the Devils

Bribes: The Power Brokers

Money talks, but the right friends scream. These brokers don't carry guns; they carry the city.

If you Control a broker's High Society District, you can buy their loyalty. Pay $2,500 to take their Favor Card and immediately add 1 permanent Influence marker to your Stash.

Honor Among Thieves: You may only buy each broker's Favor once per game. Once bought, their loyalty (and the Influence marker) remains yours even if you lose their District, unless you break a binding deal (see Cooperation).

Shylock's Mark

When you're backed into a corner, Shylock offers a lifeline with teeth.

  • The Loan: At any time before the endgame triggers, you may take a Shylock's Mark card to instantly gain $1,500. You may hold multiple marks.
  • Paying the Piper: To clear the debt, pay $2,500 to the bank at any time before the game ends (or $1,500 if Arnold Rothstein vouches for you), then discard the card.
  • The Toll: Shylock always gets his due. Every unpaid Mark in your ledger at the final Reckoning costs you -1 Respect.

Cooperation: Puppets in the Smoke

Trust is fleeting, but even sharp kingpins need rivals. Every favor granted might be a knife in the back.

Pulling the Strings

At any time during Operations, you may move Influence markers from your Operations to a rival's Operations (up to their 5 marker cap). This is not a Play and does not end your turn.

  • The Favor: You lend your name and network. The rival may use these markers as their own, immediately or later.
  • The Risk: If the rival spends your marker on a Play that generates Heat, your marker goes to the Heat Track. Otherwise, it returns to your Stash once used.

The Broker's Word: A Binding Handshake

A mobster's word is cheap. When a deal needs teeth, have a Power Broker vouch for you.

Making the Deal

To make a binding deal, a player must have at least one Favor Card.

  1. State the Terms & Deadline: The terms must be clear, and you must agree on a specific deadline (e.g. "before the Day ends," or "until you pay me $1000").
  2. Stake the Favor: The player stakes an available Favor Card, placing it between both players. Each Broker may only vouch for one deal at a time.

Example: "The Mayor vouches for me. If you let me pass through your Borough unharmed, I promise to give you $1,000 before the end of the Day."

Honoring & Breaking Deals

Deals are resolved the moment their conditions are met or failed.

  • Deal Honored: The moment the terms are met, the deal is complete. The Favor Card is immediately returned to its owner.
  • Deal Broken: If the agreed deadline passes without the terms being met, or if a player violates a standing agreement for any reason (including third-party interference), the Broker's trust is shattered. The owner must immediately discard the Favor Card and lose its permanent Influence marker.

Deals on the Side

You may wheel and deal outside the rules, but only Cash can be physically traded off the board. Hand it over anytime, no Influence required.

Combat Rules: Blood on the Streets

Violence is failed diplomacy. Smart Kingpins know a drawn gun can be stronger than a fired one. When talk fails, step inside and clear the room.

1. The Rules of Engagement

Calculating Threat

Your Base Threat is Level 1 (Kill on a 5+). This improves with bonuses, which stack up to Level 4:

  • Boss Bonus (+1): If your Boss is in the fight.
  • Safehouse Bonus (+2): Defenders holding a Secured District (Defending Assaults only).
  • Ambush Bonus (+1): Occupiers firing the first shot when enemies enter.

2. The Ambush: Crossing the Line

When an opponent Moves into a District you control, immediately choose: Ambush or Hold Fire.

  1. Ambush: You greet the invaders with lead.
    • Free Shot: Only Occupier rolls dice.
    • Bonus: Gain +1 Threat for the Ambush.
    • Casualties: Each hit removes one Invader mobster (Runners first). Ambush generates no Heat.
  2. Hold Fire: You let the invaders enter your territory uncontested.

    Whether you Ambush or not, the Invaders are now Pinned.

3. Pinned: Fight or Flight

The standoff stays in your turn. Keep spending Influence on Assault or Advance until Pinned is resolved, or you run dry and Fall Back.

The Cost of Pride: A real Kingpin knows an all-out firefight is bad business. Because you only have up to 5 Influence to spend each Day, burning 3 or 4 markers on a stubborn Assault will win you the alley, but cost you the war. While you bleed out your action economy in one District, your rivals have free rein over the rest of the city. Talk is cheap; bullets cost time.

Assault (Cost: 1 Influence) Fight for the District. Both sides roll simultaneously.
  • Casualties: Both sides apply hits (Runners first). Defenders lose Ambush bonus but keep Safehouse/Boss bonuses.
  • Heat: If the Attacker kills Defenders, gain 1 Heat. Defenders gain no Heat for self-defense kills.
  • Repeat: If both sides stand, remain Pinned. Spend another Influence to Assault again.
Advance (Cost: 1 Influence) Move Pinned mobsters (and up to 1 barrel each) to any Safe Connected District, including across water via Docks. You cannot enter another hostile District.
Fall Back (Cost: 0 Influence) Retreat to your origin District. Carried Liquor is left behind and becomes the Defender's property.
If you have 0 Influence remaining, you must Fall Back.

Note: Torch, Plunder, Courage and Firepower can be used while Pinned; no other Plays can.

4. Victory & Consequences

The Combat Play ends when one side is eliminated or the attacker retreats.

If the Defender Holds:

The District remains theirs. Any Liquor dropped by dead Invaders becomes property of the Defender.

If the Invader Wins:

If the last Defender falls or flees, you immediately claim Control of the District.

  • The Spoils: Any Liquor in the District is now yours.
  • Safehouses: If the District contained a Rival Safehouse, it is destroyed (remove it). However, you may choose to Commandeer it instead: immediately Secure the district for free (Cost: 0 Influence) by placing one of your own Safehouses there. If you have no spare Safehouses, you must relocate an existing one. Note: You may also Commandeer an unmanned Safehouse, by Moving into the District.

The Law: Fighting the Blue Wall

The Police are an automated faction that block expansion. They don't take turns or collect liquor; they just hold the line.

Moving into a Police District triggers a Combat Sequence exactly like Rival Combat, but with three key differences:

  • No Warning Shots: The Police never Ambush, but their blockade is absolute. Any Liquor carried into a Police District is instantly confiscated and returned to the supply. Your mobsters are immediately Pinned.
  • Blue Threat: Standard Officers defend with Base Threat (Hit on 5+). If the District contains a Precinct, they are heavily armed and defend with Level 3 Threat (Hit on 3+).
  • Killing Cops: Eliminating Officers generates +1 Heat, exactly like killing rival mobsters.

Resolve your Pinned status normally (Assault, Advance, or Fall Back). If you eliminate all Officers, you claim the District and can Commandeer or destroy Precincts exactly like rival Safehouses.

  • Torch: The East Side Vipers may use their Torch Play to burn down a Precinct exactly as if it were a rival Safehouse.

Tip: With lower player counts, the Law expand more rapidly, since there are usually more empty Districts to Blockade.

The Blood Oath: 4-Player Team Variant

When the heat boils over, enemies become brothers. The Feds are kicking down doors, and lone wolves are getting slaughtered. To survive, you must forge a new family.

The Blood Oath is the recommended full 4-player mode. It starts as a cutthroat free-for-all, but escalating police pressure eventually forces the board into two permanent 2v2 alliances.

Setup: The Ticking Clock

Set up the game exactly as a standard 4-player match, with one addition: Place the Federal Crackdown Tracker near the Heat Track, with a tracking token on the first space.

Advancing the Clock: Every time a Police Raid triggers (whether by a player using the Rat Play or the Heat Track hitting Boiling Point), advance the crackdown token one space.

The Sit-Down

The moment the token reaches space 10 on the Crackdown Tracker, the underworld buckles. Pause the game. The four independent mobs immediately form two permanent alliances.

  1. Rank the Bosses: Tally everyone's current Respect (scoring held Titles and completed Contracts). Tiebreaker: Total Cash on hand.
  2. Forge the Families: The player in 1st Place joins forces with the player in 4th Place. The player in 2nd Place pairs with the player in 3rd Place.

New Rules of Engagement

You and your partner now share a single goal: total domination. However, you still run your own distinct crews.

  • Separate Ledgers: You do not share Cash, Influence, Contracts, or Hand Limits. You still take individual turns in Turn Order.
  • Blood is Thicker: You cannot Ambush or Assault your partner's mobsters. You may safely Move through their Controlled Districts without triggering combat.
  • The Golden Rule of Turf: You still cannot share a block. You may not end your Phase with mobsters in a District occupied by your partner. If you Shift Liquor into their District, it instantly becomes their property.
  • Puppeteering: Lending Influence to your partner is no longer a fragile truce—it is your strongest weapon. You may freely move Influence to your partner's Operations board to fund their turn (standard Puppeteering Heat rules apply).

Claiming the Empire

The Commission no longer deals with lone wolves; they only recognize unified power. To win the game, an Alliance must collectively possess at least 1 Commission Card (earned by either partner) and reach a Combined Total of 35 Respect.

If an Alliance meets these conditions during the Reckoning Phase, the game ends immediately.

The New Kings of New York

When the game ends, tally the final Respect for all four players individually (applying all standard Title points, Contract payouts, and Shylock debt penalties). The Alliance with the highest Combined Respect wins the war.

The Capo di Tutti Capi: There are no truly shared victories... even in a team. Within the winning Alliance, the player who contributed the most Respect to the final combined total is declared the Boss of Bosses and the true Kingpin of New York. Your partner will just have to settle for kissing your ring.
