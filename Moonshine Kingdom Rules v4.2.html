RULEBOOK

A Game of Bootlegging, Backstabbing, and Gang Warfare.

In the gaslit streets of 1920s New York City, where towering skyscrapers cast long shadows over a bustling underworld, Prohibition has sparked a lawless gold rush. Four deadly mobs battle to rule the city's illicit Liquor trade. Bootlegging, political bribery, and ruthless intimidation are your tools. Muscle is expensive, and you can't be everywhere at once. You must forge fragile alliances, corner the market, and weaponize the Police against your rivals. New York is a treacherous dame with a thirst that can't be quenched. Only one mob will claim her crown. Will you carve your name into the annals of infamy, or vanish like cigar smoke in a hurricane?

Overview

2-4 Players | 90 - 120 Mins

Prohibition didn't stop the drinking; it just made the price go up.

In Moonshine Kingdom, you are a rising Kingpin in the chaotic, gaslit arena of 1920s New York. The law has turned off the taps, creating a gold rush for anyone ruthless enough to fill the glass. But you aren't the only shark in the water. Four rival mobs are clawing for control of the boroughs, and this city isn't big enough for all of you.

A Day in the Life of a Kingpin

The game is played over a series of volatile rounds, each representing twenty-four hours in the life of your empire. From the fog of early morning to the bloody consequences of the night, you must outmaneuver your rivals to survive.


Morning: The Brew

Shadows Phase

Dawn breaks over the Hudson. While the city sleeps, your operation is already churning. Fire up your Stills and commit to black market Contracts. This is your moment to turn mash into liquid gold and produce the Hooch that will fuel your rise.

Day: The Hustle

Operations Phase

The sun is up and the streets are open for business. Command your Runners to smuggle product, seize territory, and strong-arm the competition. Every move costs Influence, your political clout. Use it to dominate the daylight, and expand your Operation. But remember: violence is loud, and the police are listening.

Night: The Cost

Reckoning Phase

Twilight settles like a bruise. The shooting stops and the city's ledger is opened. Territory is finalized as you claim Borough Deeds, and your high-stakes Contracts either build your legend or mark your failure. The night patrol sweeps the streets, clearing out the careless. Settle your accounts and prepare for the dawn. Did you make a killing, or just a mess?

The Only Crown Worth Having

Cash buys bullets. But Respect buys immortality.

You earn Respect by conquering Boroughs, claiming Cartels by cornering the liquor market, and pulling off Contracts; crimes so brazen they become legends. Hold the Ghettos long enough to be named Ward Boss, and the streets will remember that too. The first mob to earn 20 and a seat at the High Table will claim her crown.

Your Goal: Earning Respect

Respect is the only currency that matters in Moonshine Kingdom. Earn it four ways: Boroughs, Cartels, Ward Boss, and Contracts.

1. Boroughs: Turf Wars

Seizing a Borough is how you turn muscle into a real empire. If you own more Districts in a Borough than any single rival when the sun goes down (Reckoning Phase), you snatch its Borough Deed.

All Boroughs are worth 3 Respect, except Staten Island, which bags you 2. But a king's reign is only as long as his reach. Lose control, and you lose the crown.

2. The Cartel: Cornering the Market

The city's 12 Speakeasies are split into four cartels. Dominate a liquor type's Joints, and you claim its Cartel Card for 3 Respect and the right to fix its price.

Claiming the Cartel

If a player Controls more of a specific Joint type than any single rival (e.g. 2 out of 3 Gin Joints), during the Reckoning Phase, they claim that liquor's Cartel Card and its 3 Respect. If there is a tie, or if no one controls any Joints of that type, the Cartel Card is returned to the Supply.

Price Fixing

The market doesn't move unless you force it. During Operations, a Cartel holder may use Fix to dictate that liquor's Market Value.

  • The Ceiling: The Cartel holder can set the price from a minimum of $200 to a maximum of $200 + $100 per Joint of that type they Control (max $500 if they own all 3).
  • A Broken Market: If a Cartel Card is unclaimed, that liquor's Market Value remains frozen at its current price until a new Cartel holder emerges to change it.

The Kickback

When you Unload at a Joint you Control, each barrel matching that Joint's House Pour earns an Action Kickback.

  • House Pour Match: Each barrel matching the Joint's House Pour grants 1 Kickback. Move 1 spent Influence marker directly back to an empty slot on your Operations Board. It is immediately available for your next Play.
  • Non-Matching: Barrels of other types can still be sold, but they do not refund actions.

The Greed Tax

If you Unload 4 or more barrels of any combination in one Play, you generate 1 Heat. Three barrels is the maximum safe delivery.

3. The Wards: Owning the Backstreets

The Boroughs make headlines, but the Ghettos decide who owns the block. If one mob controls more Ghetto Districts than any single rival during Reckoning, they claim the Ward Boss card. While they hold it, Enlist costs $200 per Runner. At endgame, it's worth 3 Respect. If the Ghetto count is tied, the card returns to the Supply.

4. Contracts: Forge Your Legend

In the cutthroat alleys of New York, a mob's reputation isn't built on good intentions; it's built on Contracts. These are the high-stakes schemes that turn a street thug into a Kingpin.

Each Contract card details an Objective and a strict Deadline. Contracts come in three escalating tiers of ambition:

  • Gigs (1 Day / 1 Respect): Quick street hustles. Keeping the lights on and the cash flowing.
  • Rackets (2 Days / 3 Respect): Mid-level capers. Operations that require turf, muscle, and a plan.
  • Scores (3 Days / 5 Respect): Headline-grabbing heists. The kind of legends they write ballads about.

The Deadline

In this business, timing is everything. When you start a Contract, place Influence markers on the card equal to the deadline number (1, 2, or 3). One marker is returned to your Stash during every Reckoning Phase. When the last marker is removed, The Deadline has arrived.

The client doesn't care about your hard-luck stories or how well you were doing yesterday. They only care about the result at the exact moment of the Deadline during the Reckoning Phase. If the board state matches the card's objectives at that specific moment, the Contract succeeds. Add the card to your Respect pile and claim your Take (see below). If the condition is not met, the Contract is botched; discard the card face-up. No excuses, no reward.

The Take:

When you successfully pull off a Contract, you earn a one-time cash reward - The Take:

  • Gig: $500
  • Racket: $1,500
  • Score: $3,000

The Commission

Fear buys loyalty; legitimacy crowns a Kingpin. The Commission is the underworld's ruling council. Earning a seat at their table is required to win the game. A player may only ever claim one Commission Card. Once earned, a Commission Card can never be lost, even if the conditions that earned it are later undone.

A Commission Card can be earned through criminal prowess (completing a set of all three Contract types), political domination (acquiring all four Favors), or absolute monopoly (holding all four Cartel Cards). See the 'Winning the Game' section for details.

Key Concepts: The Underworld at a Glance

Your Mob: The Brains, the Brawn, and the Bastards

From the cold steel of the Sicilian Syndicate to the fiery grit of the Hell's Kitchen Irish, each mob brings their own brand of violence to the city's bloodstained ballet. You command:

  • A Boss: Your kingpin. If he gets whacked, your Operation Stalls. Bring him into battle to make your attacks more deadly.
  • Runners: Your foot soldiers. Knuckle-draggers and wheelmen who haul hooch and guard your turf. You are limited to 15 Runners. With a small crew, Specialization is survival, and every deployment is a hard choice.
  • Safehouses: Your fortresses in the chaos. Hidden dens where you stash hooch and Enlist new muscle. They provide cover from Attacks and Police Raids, and allow you to build an efficient supply network.

The Map: Know Your Turf

Your empire is a broken city: 25 Districts across 5 Boroughs, each a flavor of poison and profit. Certain key districts offer unique opportunities:

  • Speakeasies: Marked by a cocktail glass symbol on the board, the city's 12 Speakeasies are divided into 4 types: 3 Gin Joints, 3 Whisky Bars, 3 Moonshine Dens, and 3 Rum Clubs. Each Joint has a House Pour (its signature liquor). These are the only spots where you can Unload liquor for cash and Kickbacks.
  • High Society Districts: Affluent neighborhoods where the rich and powerful reside, and Influence is earned. Marked on the board with a crown symbol. All High Society Districts also have Speakeasies.
  • Ghettos: Gritty districts where mob roots run deep and every alley has ears. Control more Ghettos than any single rival at Reckoning and you claim the Ward Boss card. While you hold it, Enlist costs $200 per Runner. Your Hand Limit for Contract cards is a fixed 3. If you ever hold more than that, discard immediately until you're back under the cap. Marked on the board with a fist.
  • Docks: Your link to the city's waterways, crucial to Smuggle Rum and move between distant shores. Marked on the board with an anchor.
  • Stills: Hidden distilleries marked with a number from 2-12 (corresponding to a 2d6 roll). Stills produce Domestic liquor (Gin, Whisky, or Moonshine) when your number is rolled during the Shadows Phase.

The Currencies of the Underworld

To rule the city, you'll need more than just bullets. You'll need to master its three core resources.

Influence: Currency of Power

Bullets get you turf, but Influence buys you an empire. This is your political capital, the favors you're owed, and the fear you command. Your Stash is your personal reserve stack of uncommitted Influence markers. This pool is the lifeblood of your operation, used to fund every move you make.

You'll spend your clout on:

  • Calling the Shots: Locking markers onto Contract cards to get a new caper started.
  • Grease the Wheels: Moving markers to your Operations (max 5) to fund your turn's actions.
  • Managing the Heat: Placing markers on the Heat Track when your moves get too loud.

You start with just enough clout to make some noise (7). The only way to earn more is by Bribing the power brokers found in the city's four High Society districts (see "Bribes" section).

Your Influence markers cycle between your personal Stash and your Operations using your Operations Board. Think of your Operations slots as the actions you've funded for the current Day, and your Stash as your reserve power (see diagram).

Liquid Assets

Cash is king for expanding your crew. You'll spend it to Enlist new Runners and Fortify your turf with Safehouses. It's earned by Unloading liquor, Extorting Boroughs and completing Contracts

Liquor Barrels are the lifeblood of your operation. Produced at your Stills or smuggled from Rum Row, their value fluctuates based on who's pulling the strings.

Territory: Lines in the Concrete

An empire isn't just bullets and blood; it's about logistics. It's about securing supply lines, moving product before it turns sour, and drawing borders your rivals are too terrified to cross. Because your forces are spread thin, a cleverly placed Safehouse and a handful of loyal guards are often enough to deter a full-scale turf war.

1. Control: Ownership

You can't tax what you don't own. Ownership is binary: you either run the block, or you don't.

  • District Control: You control a District if you have at least one Mobster (Boss or Runner) OR a Safehouse present there.
    • Instant Possession: Control is claimed the moment your piece enters a District. If you move through an empty District, it counts as yours for that instant.
    • Abandonment: Control is lost immediately if your last piece voluntarily leaves or is eliminated. Unattended liquor does not grant control.
    • Contested Ground: During combat, the Occupier retains Control, and any defensive bonuses, until their last piece is removed.
  • Borough Control: You Control a Borough if you Control the majority of its Districts. Borough Control is not instant, Deeds are exchanged during the Reckoning phase. If no single mob holds the majority, the Borough Deed is returned to the Supply.

The Golden Rule of Turf: During the Shadows and Reckoning phases, there can be only one mob in a District. While you hold a District, Rivals cannot enter without triggering combat.

2. Connection: The Network

Knowing the layout of the city is key to flanking enemies and moving hooch. Districts are Connected if:

  • Land & Bridges: They share a physical border or are linked by a Bridge.
    Example: Williamsburg connects to Red Hook, Brownsville, Corona, and Astoria by land, and to Five Points by Bridge.
  • Waterways (Docks): All Dock Districts connect to each other as if they were adjacent.
    Example: You can Move directly from West Side (Manhattan) to Thorggs Neck (The Bronx) in one action.

Setup: Carving Out Your Corner

  1. Prepare the City:
    • Place the Game Board and prepare the supply of Cash and Dice.
    • Prepare the Liquor Supply. Set starting market values: Rum $500, Whisky $400, Gin $300, Moonshine $200.
    • In every District, randomly place a face-down Still token, then flip them all over.
  2. Stack the Ledger:
    • Shuffle each Contract Card deck (Gigs, Rackets, Scores) and place them face-down next to the board.
    • Place the Shylock's Mark cards next to the Contract Ledger.
    • Place all other card decks in the Supply.
  3. Claim Your Crew:
    Shuffle the Playbooks and deal 1 to each player to assign mobs. Players take all corresponding mob pieces; 1 Boss (2 if Sicilian), 15 Runners and 3 Safehouses plus an Operations Board.
  4. Establish Turf:
    First remove Staten Island (nobody starts here), then shuffle the remaining Borough Deeds. Each player draws one card to determine their starting Borough. Place any unclaimed Deeds in the Supply, along with Staten Island. Place your starting pieces on the board:
    1. In your starting Borough's Ghetto District, place 1 Safehouse, 1 Boss, and 2 Runners.
    2. In any Speakeasy in the starting Borough, place 3 Runners.
    3. In any Dock District in the starting Borough, place 3 Runners.
    4. The Blue Wall: Finally, place 1 Police Precinct and 3 Officers in every Ghetto District that was not selected by a player.
  5. Get Your Cut:
    Players receive $1,500 cash and 7 Influence markers. Place 5 markers on your Operations slots, and the other 2 on your Stash slot.
  6. Determine Priority:
    Randomly assign the New Moon Priority Tokens to determine the starting order. Place the Full Moon Priority Tokens near the board to form the Priority Pool for the following turn. Place the Double-Sided Moon Phase token on the New Moon side to track the current phase.

The player with New Moon Priority Token #1 starts round one. Priority order is Token #1 → #4. At the end of each round, players swap to the opposite moon phase (New Moon ↔ Full Moon), then repeat.

Swimming with Sharks?

If you're a fresh face at a table of veterans, have the experienced players allow you an extra Influence marker at the start of the game. It's a simple handicap that levels the playing field and keeps the blood flowing fairly.

Gameplay: Run the Streets

Rounds unfold in 3 phases: Shadows (Production), Operations (Action), and Reckoning (Cleanup). Play follows Priority order (Token #1 → #4).

Phase 1: Shadows

The city wakes to a hiss of steam. This communal phase is where mobs scramble for product and fresh deals. All steps resolve in the current Day's Priority Order (#1 → #4).

1. Brew

The city wakes up thirsty, and your basements are already hissing. The player holding Priority Token #1 rolls the communal dice pool to fire up the boilers: Red dice and White dice each equal to the Player Count + 1.
(e.g., in a 4-player game, roll 5 Red and 5 White dice).

The Draft & The Churn: In Priority order, each boss takes their turn to claim their dice and push product onto the streets:

  • Draft: Snatch 1 Red die and 1 White die from the pool. Their sum is your Target Number.
  • Produce: Every Still matching your Target Number fires up immediately (including Rival operations). Place matching Domestic Liquor barrels on the active Stills based on the muscle present to run the machines: 1-2 Runners = 1 Barrel, 3-4 Runners = 2 Barrels, 5+ Runners = 3 Barrels.
  • Dry Tap: The city's glass is only so deep. If the physical supply of a specific liquor runs out, production stops immediately. First to draft gets the goods; the rest get excuses.

The Breakdown: Greed has a price. Once every boss has drafted and taken their cut, there will be exactly 1 Red die and 1 White die left in the gutter. Add these two remaining dice together. This is the Breakdown Number: the overworked pressure valves fail, and the boilers violently explode!

  • The Blast: Scalding mash and shrapnel tear through any District with a matching Still. Eliminate Runners by crowding: 1-2 Mobsters = 1 Killed, 3-4 Mobsters = 2 Killed, 5+ Mobsters = 3 Killed.

Note: Brick walls won't stop a bursting boiler. Safehouses do not protect against Breakdown casualties. If a Boss is caught in the blast and there are no Runners left to take the hit, the Boss goes home in a pine box.

2. Grease the Wheels

  • Start Contracts: You may start Contracts from your hand by revealing the card face-up and placing markers from your Stash equal to the Days to Deadline (1, 2, or 3) on top.
  • Capacity: You may run as many Contracts as you have the Influence for.
  • Fund Operations: After staking Contract markers, move up to 5 markers from your Stash into your Operations slots. Any markers left in your Stash cannot be used for actions this Day.

Phase 2: Operations Phase

Operations runs in a cycle. In Priority order, each player takes one Play at a time.

  1. Standard Play: Spend Influence to perform 1 Play (Move, Enlist, Shift, etc.) or 1 Power Play (e.g. Unload).
  2. Combat Play: If combat is triggered, the Play includes full resolution and doesn't end until the district is won, lost, or the attacker falls back.
  3. Paying for Plays: Remove the spent marker(s) from your Operations and return them to your Stash. If the Play generates Heat, move the marker to the Heat Track instead.
  4. Laying Low: When you run out of actions (or choose to step off the street early), you must Lay Low:
    1. Clear any remaining markers from your Operations to your Stash.
    2. Return your current Priority Token to the Priority Pool and take the lowest numbered token of the other moon phase from the Pool (turn order next round).
One-Action Moves (1 Action Each)
Move Move any number of mobsters (max 1 barrel each) to one single connected district.
Moving into rival turf initiates a Combat Sequence (see Combat Rules section).
Shift For $100, move any number of Liquor barrels from a District you control to any Connected District. If you Shift barrels into a rival District, they become the rival's property.
Enlist Hire Runners in a Safehouse you Control for $300 per Runner. If you hold the Ward Boss card, the cost drops to $200 per Runner.
Extort Shake down the locals in a Borough where you hold the Deed. Earn $200 per Controlled District in that Borough. If you make $500+ from this Extort action, generate Heat.
Limit: You may perform this action once per Day, in each Borough you Hold.
Hustle Your Boss must be in play. Draw 2 Contract cards from any combination of the 3 face-down draw piles. You must immediately discard down to your Hand Limit of 3. If a draw pile is empty, shuffle its discard pile to form a new one.
Limit: You may perform this action once per Day.
Fortify For $500, either place one of your available Safehouse pieces in a District you control, or relocate one of your existing Safehouses.
Smuggle Once per Day, at a Dock District you Control: Send up to 5 Runners to roll for Havana Rum (use Smuggling tables. 0 successes = Arrests based on Heat).
Fix If you hold a Cartel Card, set the Market Value of that liquor type to any amount between $200 and $200 + $100 per matching Joint you Control (max $500).

Power Plays

These Plays cost two Influence markers to perform, but count as a single Play in the turn cycle.

Two-Action Moves (2 Actions Each)
Unload 1. Cash out barrels in a Joint you Control for the current Market Value.

2. For each barrel that matches the Joint's House Pour, you gain an Action Kickback: move 1 spent Influence marker directly back to an empty slot on your Operations Board. It is immediately available for your next Play.

3. The Greed Tax: If you Unload 4 or more total barrels of any combination in a single Play, you generate 1 Heat. You choose which of the two spent markers is placed on the Heat Track. Three barrels is the maximum safe delivery.
Rat Rat out your rivals, to trigger a targeted Police Raid and reset Heat.
You may nudge the Raid Roll (add or subtract 1 from the total sum of the dice).
Crown Boss got whacked? Appoint a new one ($0) in any safe (Controlled or Empty) District. No safe spots? Tough luck, wait until there is.

HQ: You may also Fortify your new Bosses' District for no additional cost ($0, 0 Actions).
Bribe Pay off one of the City's Power brokers in a High Society District you control for $2,500.

Take that specific District's Favor card and gain 1 permanent Influence marker from the Supply. Add the marker to your Stash. Each player may only buy each High Society District's Favor once per game. You keep it for the rest of the game, even if you lose control of the District later.

Mob Signature Plays

Every crew in this city's got tricks up their sleeve, signature moves to outfox, out-muscle, or outrun the other syndicates vying for New York's crown. Each mob has two unique plays they can perform

Sicilian Syndicate

Old blood, older rules. An insult to the family is a death sentence.

Capo (2 Actions) Promote a Runner from any District to Capo (switch Runner for the spare Boss piece). Your Capo is a second Boss, providing stability and gaining +1 Ferocity in Combat (+2 when both Bosses attack one District). You may only have 1 Capo (2 Bosses total).
Consigliere (1 Action) Use your connections to smooth over legal troubles. Remove 1 Influence marker of your choice from the Heat Track and return it to its owner's Stash (move other markers down to fill gaps if required).

Hell's Kitchen Irish

They brew it strong and fight dirty. Why buy bullets when a brick works just fine?

Courage (0 Actions) Before you roll in a fight, you may discard 1 barrel of your Liquor in the District where the battle is taking place to gain +1 Ferocity for that roll (max 4).
Plunder (1 Action) Initiate a special attack. For each successful hit, instead of eliminating a mobster, steal 1 Liquor barrel of your choice from the target District. You can carry these away if you Advance. Defender hits still kill. You may also choose to Plunder when you Ambush (0 Actions). Plunder does not create Heat.

East Side Vipers

They move like smoke and strike like snakes. You won't know you're at war until your warehouse is burning.

Stealth (1 Action) Move up to 5 mobsters (no liquor) into a connected rival district without triggering an Ambush. You cannot end your Play in this state. You must immediately spend another action to either: Assault the district, or Advance to a connected safe district.
Torch (2 Actions) Burn down a safehouse in a rival District which has 3 or less mobsters defending it. To perform this action, you must have at least one Runner Pinned in the target District. Sacrifice one of those Pinned Runners; he is removed from the game. The rival's Safehouse is immediately destroyed (return it to their supply). Any rival mobsters and liquor in the District are unharmed and retain Control. This action generates Heat.

Harlem Knights

Dressed to kill, literally. The Knights write letters with with Chicago Typewriters.

Skiff (1 Action) Move up to 5 mobsters (with or without liquor) from a Coastal District you control to any other Coastal District. This movement does not require Docks. If you enter a Rival District, standard Ambush rules apply.
Firepower Pay $200 per attack to increase your firepower. When you Assault or Ambush, you roll one additional attack die (up to a maximum of 5). Can't be used when defending an Assault.

Phase 3: Reckoning Phase

Once all players have clocked out of Operations, the city settles accounts. Reckoning is resolved simultaneously by all players unless otherwise noted.

1. Nightwatch

The night patrol sweeps the streets for overcrowding. Any District with more than 5 Mobsters faces arrests. Remove excess Runners to bring the count down to 5.

2. Settle the Books

Balance the accounts, in the following order:

  1. Turf Check: Settle the night's grabs all at once.
    • Borough Deeds: If a mob controls more Districts in a Borough than any single rival, the Deed is transferred to the new owner. If tied, the Deed returns to the Supply.
    • Cartel Cards: If a player controls more Joints of a type than any single rival, they claim that liquor's Cartel Card. If tied or unclaimed, the Cartel Card returns to the Supply.
    • Ward Boss: If a player controls more Ghetto Districts than any single rival, they claim the Ward Boss card. If tied or unclaimed, the card returns to the Supply.
  2. Resolve Contracts: Remove 1 marker from each active Contract and return it to your Stash:
    • Completed: If the final marker was removed and you met the objectives, add it to your Respect pile and collect the Take.
    • Failed: If the final marker was removed but you missed the objective, discard the card to a face-up discard pile.
    • Abandon: You may choose to cut your losses on an active Contract. Return the Influence markers to your Stash and discard the card to a face-up discard pile.
  3. Honor Your Word: Settle your debts & deals.
    • Handshakes: Check all active deals. If honored, the staked Favor Card is returned. If broken, the owner must discard the card and lose its permanent Influence marker.
    • Repay Debts: You may now pay off any debts to Shylock ($2,500).
  4. Commission: Check if you have met the requirements for a seat at the High Table (Contract set, 4 Favors, or 4 Cartels). If you have, and do not already have one, you officially earn your Commission Card. Take one from the supply.

After all accounts are settled, the round is over. Check if a new Kingpin has been crowned (see the Winning the Game section). If not, flip the Moon Phase token and begin the next round's Shadows Phase in the new Priority Order.

Winning the Game: The Final Reckoning

The war for New York ends the moment a player secures a Commission Card AND amasses 20 or more Total Respect. Only then are you crowned the undisputed Kingpin.

1. A Seat at the High Table

Any thug can fire a Tommy gun, but it takes a true mastermind to sit at the High Table. During the Settle the Books step of the Reckoning Phase, any player who achieves one of the following legendary milestones is awarded a Commission Card. (A player may only ever claim one).

  • The Public Enemy: Prove your criminal genius by successfully completing a set of all three Contract tiers: a Gig, Racket, and Score.
  • The Puppeteer: Prove your political dominance by acquiring and holding all four unique Favor Cards.
  • The Baron: Prove your iron grip on the streets by simultaneously holding all four Cartel Cards (Gin, Whisky, Moonshine, and Rum).

2. Amassing Power

Legitimacy means nothing if the streets don't fear you. You need 20 Respect to claim the crown. To calculate your current Respect, tally up the spoils of your empire:

  • Borough Turf: Gain 3 Respect for each Controlled Borough (2 Respect for Staten Island).
  • Ward Boss: Gain 3 Respect if you hold the Ward Boss card at the end of the game.
  • Criminal Legends: Gain 1, 3, or 5 Respect for each successfully completed Contract in your ledger.
  • Market Dominance: Gain 3 Respect for each Cartel Card you currently hold.
  • Blood Debts: Subtract 1 Respect for every unpaid Loan owed to Shylock. (Shylock always gets his due).

The Final Standoff

If multiple bosses cross the 20 Respect threshold with a Commission Card on the exact same night, the streets run red to settle the score:

  1. Total Respect: The tied player with the highest overall Respect takes the crown.
  2. Cold Hard Cash: If Respect is deadlocked, the tied player with the most Total Cash (Include the loose change printed on your Playbook) buys the throne.

There are no shared victories in Moonshine Kingdom. You either rule the city alone, or you bleed out in the gutter.

Heat: Playing with Fire

Heat measures how much the law's breathing down your neck. The Heat Tracker is a 5-space communal track. When you generate Heat, you must place one Influence marker on the Heat track. Played right, Heat is a weapon. Played wrong, it's a noose.

Gaining Heat

These noisy actions Heat up the streets one notch:

  • Blood on the Pavement: Aggression draws unwanted attention. If the Attacker in a combat Play (the player who initiated Combat) inflicts 1 or more kills, they must add 1 marker to the Heat track at the conclusion of the Play.
    Note: A multi-round Assault is still a single Play and generates a maximum of 1 Heat. Defenders acting in self-defense (during an Ambush or an Assault) never generate Heat.
  • Shakedown Gets Loud: If an Extort action earns $500+, you generate 1 Heat.
  • The Greed Tax: If you Unload 4 or more total barrels of any combination in a single Play, you generate 1 Heat. You choose which of the two spent markers is placed on the Heat Track.
  • Torch: Using the Vipers Torch Play.

Police Raids

A raid is triggered when a player uses the Rat action or the Heat Track reaches Boiling Point (The 5th token is placed on the Track). Pause the current turn and roll 2D6. The result targets all matching Stills

  1. Raids: For each mob Controlled District Hit:
    • Seizures: All Liquor in the District is seized, unless protected by a Safehouse.
    • Fines: You must pay a fine of $200 per your Heat marker on the track. If you can't pay, you must take a Shylock's Mark card.
    • Arrests: Lose 1 Runner for each of your Heat markers. Safehouses do not protect against arrests. Bosses cannot be arrested.
  2. Blockades: For each hit District that is either Empty or Police Controlled:
    • Empty District: The Police move in. Place 3 Officers in the District. If the Borough does not currently contain a Precinct, place a Precinct here as well.
    • Police Controlled: Reinforcements arrive. If the District contains 3 or fewer Officers, add 2 Officers.
    3. See 'The Law' section for Police rules.
  3. Cool Down: Once resolved, all Influence markers on the Heat Track are returned to their owners' Stashes.

Order creeps in... until the next fool strikes a match.

Supply Lines: Smuggling from Rum Row

The lifeblood of your operation, Liquor can be Brewed locally in the shadows, or Smuggled from the high seas.

Smuggling Runs

Havana Rum is the premium poison. It cannot be brewed locally; it must be imported through the city's waterways.

To Smuggle (1 Action):

Once per Day, at a Dock District you Control, you may run the blockade:

  1. Assign Crew: Send up to 5 Runners from any Dock you Control.
    Note: You can't send your Boss.
  2. Check Network: Count your Controlled Docks to find the Target Number needed to succeed.
  3. Roll & Resolve: Roll your dice pool (based on Crew Size, see tables below).
    • Success: Gain 1 Rum for every die that meets or beats the Target Number. Place them in the Dock District.
    • Failure: If you roll 0 successes, the Coast Guard spots you. Gain no Rum and arrest Runners equal to current Heat level (if Heat is at level 0, there are no arrests).

Example: You control 3 Docks, and decide to Smuggle some rum from one of them. You send a crew of 5, which gives you 3 dice and a target of 3+ on each die. You roll 1, 3 & 5 and gain 2 barrels of Rum, which are added to the Dock District. You escape the Coast Guard... this time.

Favors: Deals with the Devils

Bribes: The Power Brokers

In this town, money talks, but the right friends scream. They don't carry guns, they carry the city in their pockets.

To gain their favor, you must Control the High Society District where they hold court. If you run their turf, you can pay $2,500 to buy their Favor Card. This grants you 1 Permanent Influence Marker, added directly to your Stash.

Restriction: Each player may only purchase each Broker's Favor once per game. Even if you lose control of the District later, the Favor (and the Influence) remain yours. Once bought, they stay bought, unless you betray thier trust.

  • Mayor Jimmy Walker – "The Night Mayor"
    Located in Morris Park.
    A suit, a smile, and a blind eye to the law. The Mayor is as crooked as a dog's hind leg, but he’s the best friend a bootlegger can buy. He ensures the police look the other way while you run the city.
  • Arnold Rothstein – "The Brain"
    Located in Williamsburg.
    The man who fixed the World Series. He doesn't play the game; he owns the casino. Rothstein bankrolls the underworld, and his nod opens financial doors that are welded shut to everyone else.
  • Stephanie St. Clair – "Queenie"
    Located in Sugar Hill.
    The Tiger of Harlem. She runs the numbers game and fought the mob to a standstill. You don't buy Queenie's loyalty; you pay tribute to her power. Having her on your side is a signal that you are untouchable.
  • Texas Guinan – "The Hostess"
    Located in Richmond Hill.
    "Hello, Sucker!" The Queen of the Nightclubs controls the social pulse of the city. Judges, senators, and stars drink at her table. If you want your name whispered in the right ears, you pay Texas.

Shylock's Mark

In this city, everyone owes someone. When you're cornered, a desperate deal can save your skin... but the debt always comes due.

The city's most notorious loan shark doesn't lend money, he buys pieces of your soul. This desperate deal grants you an instant infusion of $1,500 cash. You may take a loan from Shylock at any time before the endgame is triggered (you can't take a loan after the Vote has been called). To clear your name, repay $2,500 during the Settle the Books step of the Reckoning Phase. You may hold multiple marks.

Any unpaid debts cost you -1 Respect each at the final Reckoning.

Cooperation: Puppets in the Smoke

Trust is fleeting, but sometimes even the sharpest kingpins need to lean on a rival. It's a dance of power and peril, where every favor granted might just be a knife in the back.

Pulling the Strings

Sometimes, a rival needs your reputation and connections to get things done. At any time during the Operations Phase, you may move Influence markers from your Operations to a rival's Operations (up to their 5 marker cap). This is not an Action and does not end your turn.

  • The Favor: You are lending your name and your network. The rival may use these markers immediately if it is their turn, or save them for later. They use your clout exactly as if it were their own.
  • The Risk: If the rival spends your marker on a Play that generates Heat, your marker goes to the Heat Track. You stuck your neck out to vouch for them, so when the police crack down, it's your reputation that takes the hit. Otherwise, the marker is returned to your Stash once used.

The Broker's Word: A Binding Handshake

A mobster's word is cheap. When a deal needs teeth, you can have a Power Broker vouch for you.

Making the Deal

To make a binding deal, a player must have at least one Favor Card.

  1. State the Terms: The terms must be a clear outcome that will occur before the end of the day.
  2. Stake the Favor: The player stakes an available Favor Card, placing it between both players. Each Broker may only vouch for one deal at a time.

Example: "The Mayor vouches for me. If you let me pass through your Borough unharmed, I promise to give you $1,000 at Reckoning."

The Cost of Betrayal

During the Settle the Books step of the Reckoning Phase, all active deals are resolved.

  • Deal Honored: The Favor Card is returned to its owner.
  • Deal Broken: The Broker's trust is shattered. The owner must discard their Favor Card back to the supply. They immediately lose the permanent Influence marker gained from that card.

A deal is considered broken if the terms are not met for any reason, including third party interference. In this business, there are no excuses.

Deals on the Side

You're free to wheel and deal outside the rules, but only Cash can be physically traded off the board. Hand it over anytime, no action required.

Combat Rules: Blood on the Streets

Violence is a failure of diplomacy. Smart Kingpins use their muscle as a deterrent—knowing that a drawn gun is often more powerful than a fired one. But when negotiations fail, you must step inside, face the barrel of a gun, and clear the room.

1. The Rules of Engagement

The mechanics of violence rely on two factors: Dice Pool (how many guns you have) and Ferocity (how deadly you are).

Calculating Ferocity

Your Base Ferocity is Level 1 (Kill on a 5+). This improves with bonuses, which stack up to Level 4:

  • Boss Bonus (+1): If your Boss is in the fight.
  • Safehouse Bonus (+2): Defenders holding a Fortified District (Defending Assaults only).
  • Ambush Bonus (+1): Occupiers firing the first shot when enemies enter.

2. The Ambush: Crossing the Line

Think you can just waltz onto enemy territory? When an opponent Moves into a District you control, you must immediately declare your response. You have two choices: Ambush or Hold Fire.

  1. Ambush: You greet the invaders with lead.
    • Free Shot: Only Occupier rolls dice.
    • Bonus: Gain +1 Ferocity for the Ambush.
    • Casualties: For every hit, the Invader removes one mobster (Runners first). The ambushing player generates no Heat for this defensive action.
  2. Hold Fire: You let the invaders enter your territory uncontested.

    Whether you Ambush or not, the Invaders are now Pinned...

3. Pinned: Fight or Flight

Your mobsters are Pinned inside the hostile District. You must immediately resolve this stand-off. This whole sequence counts as a single Play (regardless of actions spent).

Assault (1 Action) Fight for the District. Both sides roll their dice simultaneously.
  • Casualties: Both sides apply hits and remove mobsters (Runners first). Defenders lose Ambush bonus but gain Safehouse/Boss bonuses.
  • Heat: If the Attacker kills any Defenders, the Attacker gains 1 Heat. The Defender gains no Heat for kills made in self-defense.
  • Repeat: If both sides still stand, you remain Pinned. You may spend another Action to Assault again immediately.
Advance (1 Action) Move your Pinned mobsters (and up to 1 barrel each) to any Safe Connected District (including across water via Docks). You may not Move into another hostile District.
Fall Back (0 Actions) Your mobsters retreat back to the exact District they came from. Any Liquor they were carrying is left behind and immediately becomes the property of the Defender.
(If you have 0 Actions remaining in your Operations, you must automatically Fall Back.)

Note: Some special mob abilities can be used while Pinned (Torch, Plunder, Courage and Firepower), but no other actions may be used during this Play.

4. Victory & Consequences

The Combat Play ends when one side is eliminated or the attacker retreats.

If the Defender Holds:

The District remains theirs. Any Liquor dropped by dead Invaders becomes property of the Defender.

If the Invader Wins:

If the last Defender falls or flees, you immediately claim Control of the District.

  • The Spoils: Any Liquor in the District is now yours.
  • Safehouses: If the District contained a Rival Safehouse, it is destroyed (remove it). However, you may choose to Commandeer it instead: immediately Fortify the district for free ($0/ 0 Actions) by placing one of your own Safehouses there. If you have no spare Safehouses, you must relocate an existing one. Note: You may also Commandeer an unmanned Safehouse, by Moving into the District.

The Law: Fighting the Blue Wall

The Police are an automated faction that blocks your expansion. They do not take turns, move, or collect liquor. They simply hold the line.

Crossing the Line

Moving into a Police District functions exactly like moving into a Rival District. It initiates a Combat Sequence that must be resolved immediately as part of the same Play.

1. The Checkpoint

The Police do not Ambush (they do not roll for a free attack). However, their blockade is absolute:

  • Confiscation: Upon entering, ALL Liquor carried by your entering mobsters is immediately seized and returned to the supply. You cannot smuggle product through a police line.
  • Pinned: Your mobsters are now Pinned. You must immediately resolve the standoff.

2. Breaking the Line

Resolve the Pinned status using standard rules:

  • Assault (1 Action): Attack the officers.
    • Rolls: Combat is simultaneous. You roll your dice; the Police roll theirs.
    • Police Ferocity: Standard Officers defend with Base Ferocity (Hit on 5+).
    • Precinct Bonus: If the District contains a Precinct, the Police defend with Level 3 Ferocity (Hit on 3+).
    • Heat: If you kill any Officers, you generate +1 Heat.
  • Advance (1 Action): Move your Pinned mobsters (and up to 1 barrel each) to a safe Connected District.
  • Fall Back (0 Actions): Your mobsters retreat back to the District they came from. Any Liquor is left behind. (If you have 0 Actions remaining, you must automatically Fall Back.)

Defeating the Law

If you eliminate all Officers, you claim the District.

  • Precincts: Police Precincts act exactly like Safehouses. If you clear the District, you may destroy the Precinct or Commandeer it.
  • Torch: The East Side Vipers may use their Torch play to burn down a Precinct exactly as if it were a rival Safehouse.

Tip: With lower player counts, the Law expand more rapidly, since there are usually more empty Districts to Blockade.

The Syndicate Wars: 4-Player Team Variant

When the pressure gets too high, enemies become brothers. The era of the lone wolf boss is bleeding out in the gutter. The Feds are kicking down doors, and the only way to survive the crackdown is to consolidate.

The Syndicate Wars is the recommended way to play Moonshine Kingdom at a full 4-player count. It begins as a standard cutthroat free-for-all, but mid-game, the escalating police presence forces a permanent 2v2 alliance. This completely shifts the dynamic, turning bitter rivals into indispensable partners in a race for the city's crown.

Setup: The Ticking Clock

Set up the game exactly as a standard 4-player game, with one addition: Place the Federal Crackdown Tracker near the Heat Track. Place a tracking token on the first space.

Advancing the Clock: Every time a Police Raid is triggered (either by a player using the Rat Play or the Heat Track reaching Boiling Point), advance the tracking token one space.

The Consolidation

The moment the marker reaches space 10 on the Federal Crackdown Tracker, the underworld buckles. Pause the game. The four independent mobs immediately consolidate into two warring Syndicates.

  1. Rank the Bosses: All players calculate their current Respect (scoring Cartel Cards, Boroughs, and completed Contracts). Tiebreaker: Total Cash on hand.
  2. Form the Alliances: The player in 1st Place forms a permanent Syndicate with the player in 4th Place. The player in 2nd Place pairs with the player in 3rd Place.

New Rules of Engagement

You and your partner now share a single goal: total domination. However, you are still running two distinct operations.

  • Separate Ledgers: You do not share Cash, Influence, Contracts, or Hand Limits. You still take your own individual turns in standard Moon Priority order.
  • Blood is Thicker: You cannot Ambush or Assault your partner's mobsters. You may safely Move through your partner's Controlled Districts without triggering combat.
  • The Golden Rule of Turf: You still cannot share turf. You may not end your Phase with your mobsters in a District occupied by your partner. If you Shift Liquor into your partner's District, it immediately becomes their property.
  • Puppeteering: Lending Influence to your partner is no longer a fragile truce; it is your strongest weapon. You may freely move Influence to your partner's Operations board to fund their turn (standard Puppeteering Heat rules apply).

The Syndicate Victory

The Commission doesn't deal with lone wolves anymore; they deal with Syndicates. To win the game, a Syndicate must collectively possess at least 1 Commission Card (earned by either partner) and reach a Combined Total of 35 Respect. If a Syndicate meets these conditions during the Settle the Books phase, the game ends immediately, and they are crowned the true Kings of New York.

The New Kings of New York

When the game ends, tally the final Respect for all four players individually (applying all standard points and Shylock debt penalties). The Syndicate with the highest Combined Respect wins the game.

The Capo di Tutti Capi: There are no shared victories... even in a shared victory. Within the winning Syndicate, the player who contributed the most Respect to the final combined total is declared the Senior Partner and the true Kingpin of New York. Your partner will just have to settle for kissing your ring.
