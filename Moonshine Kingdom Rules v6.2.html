RULEBOOK

A Game of Bootlegging, Backstabbing, and Gang Warfare.

New York City, 1926. Prohibition was supposed to dry the city out. Instead it lit a fuse. Every basement is a brewery, every back room a bank, and four mobs are clawing for the throne of the liquor trade. Bootlegging, bribery, and intimidation are your tools. Forge fragile alliances, corner the market, and turn the Police loose on your rivals. This city has a thirst that can't be quenched, and only one mob will wear its crown. Carve your name into the skyline, or vanish like cigar smoke in a hurricane.

A Day in the Life of a Kingpin

2-4 Players | 90-120 Mins

Prohibition didn't stop the drinking; it just made the price go up.

In Moonshine Kingdom, you are a rising Kingpin in 1920s New York. Prohibition has turned off the taps, creating a gold rush for anyone ruthless enough to fill the glass. Four rival mobs claw for the boroughs, and this city isn't big enough for all of you. The game is played over a series of Days, each unfolding in three brutal phases. From the fog of morning to the bloody consequences of night, outmaneuver your rivals to survive.


Morning: Shadows Phase

Dawn breaks over the Hudson. Before the city wakes, the boilers burn. Fire up your Stills, stake your black-market Contracts, and turn cheap mash into liquid gold.

Day: Operations Phase

The sun is up, and so is the heat. Command your Runners to smuggle product, seize turf, and strong-arm the competition. Every move costs Influence: your political clout. Use it to expand, but remember: violence is loud, and the Police are listening.

Night: Reckoning Phase

Twilight settles like a bruise. The guns go quiet, the blood washes into the gutters, and the ledger opens. Claim your turf, cash in your Contracts, and let the Police sweep up the careless. Did you make a killing, or just a mess?

The Only Crown Worth Having

Cash buys bullets. But Respect buys immortality.

You earn Respect by conquering Boroughs, claiming the Ward Boss and Harbormaster Titles, and above all by pulling off Contracts. But Respect alone won't crown you: you must also buy your way onto The Commission by collecting all four Power Broker Favors. The first mob to hold a Commission Card and 15+ Respect takes the crown.

Game Components: Tools of the Trade

Your whole damn operation in miniature. Every piece earns, bleeds, or burns.

The City Map (Game Board): A soot-stained blueprint of 1920s New York, carved into 25 Districts, with a shared 5-space Heat Track for Police pressure and a numbered Pressure Strip per Borough ranking its boilers (see Police Raids).

The Ledger (x4): Your back-room desk. A slotted player board where you spend the day's actions and cycle your Influence. Every move you make starts here.

Mob Pieces (x4 Sets): Each player claims a color and commands:

  • 1 Boss, 15 Runners, and 1 Safehouse: The brains, the muscle, and the one hideout the whole operation runs out of.
  • 11 Influence Markers: The currency of the underworld. Tokens used to pay for daily actions, stake Contracts, and absorb Police Heat.
  • 1 Mob Playbook: The family secrets: the dirty tricks and unique abilities only your mob can pull.

The Whispers (Cards): The paper trail of your empire.

  • Contract Cards: High-risk, high-reward jobs. Pull them off and they build your legend; botch them and they bury you.
  • Notoriety Cards: The prize for a full set of Contracts (Gig, Racket, and Score). Each is worth 2 Respect.
  • Borough Deeds (5): The paper that says a Borough is yours. Hold the most turf in one and the Deed comes to you.
  • Titles (2): The Ward Boss and the Harbormaster. Crowns claimed by ruling the Wards or the Docks.
  • Shylock's Mark Cards: Debt trackers. A lifeline with teeth.
  • The Rat Card (1): The ultimate sin in cardboard form. A mark of shame; no Rat is ever crowned.
  • Favor Cards: The strings you pull with the city's corrupt elite: judges, aldermen, and the men who look the other way.
  • Commission Cards: Your seat at the table of The Commission, the council that crowns kingpins.

Turn Tokens (8): Two sets of numbered tokens (1–4). One set dictates today's initiative; the other is claimed to determine tomorrow's order.

The Turn Sequence Card (1): A quick reference for the Day's phases. Its reverse holds the Town Planning Ledger, the District roster for setup.

Liquor Barrels: The lifeblood of your operation, in two flavors: Moonshine (Grey) and Havana Rum (Brown). Cheap swill and the good stuff; nothing in between.

The Dice (10): 5 White and 5 Red. Used to fire up the morning mash and settle grudges on the street.

The Bankroll (Cash): Dirty, untraceable bills in denominations of $100, $500, and $1,000. The kind that never sees a bank.

Police Squads (4 Blue Pieces): The blue wall that wants to see you swing. Impassable roaming patrols that prowl toward trouble, one to a mainland Borough. At setup they padlock the four High Society Speakeasies (see The Blue Wall).

Federal Crackdown Tracker (1): A 10-space pressure clock used in the 4-player team variant.

Key Concepts: The Underworld at a Glance

Your Mob: The Brains, the Brawn, and the Bastards

From the cold steel of the Sicilian Syndicate to the fiery grit of the Hell's Kitchen Irish, each mob brings their own brand of violence to the city's bloodstained ballet. You command:

  • A Boss: Your kingpin. The head of the snake. Bring him into a firefight to make your attacks deadlier, park him at a Still to brew an extra barrel — but if he catches a bullet, your crew is crippled until you crown a successor.
  • Runners: Your foot soldiers. Knuckle-draggers and wheelmen who haul hooch, guard your turf, and catch your bullets. You are strictly limited to 15 Runners — never enough to occupy the whole city. To expand, you must leave borders thin and rely on the threat of violence rather than an actual army.
  • Safehouse: Your headquarters and base of operations. You run a single Safehouse: it locks down Turf even when empty, provides a massive defensive advantage in shootouts, and anchors the block where you Recruit fresh muscle. As your empire shifts, so can your base: Secure a District you Control to pull up stakes, or take over a rival's Safehouse when you overrun their turf.

The Map: Know Your Turf

Your empire is a broken city: 25 Districts across 5 Boroughs, each a flavor of poison and profit. Certain key Districts offer unique opportunities:

  • Speakeasies: The only spots where you can Unload Liquor for cash. Most pay a flat $300 per barrel, but one premium joint per mainland Borough is a High Society Speakeasy, paying a massive $500 per barrel. Sell Moonshine or Rum at either; only Rum pays a Kickback (see Unload). The booze doesn't set the price; the address does.
  • Wards: Gritty blocks where mob roots run deep. Hold the most at Reckoning to claim the Ward Boss card and collect Union Tax when rivals Recruit. You can also hire fresh muscle in any Ward you Control (see Recruit).
  • Docks: Where the rum comes ashore. At a Dock you Control you may Trade Moonshine for Havana Rum (see Operations: Trade). All Dock Districts connect across the water as if adjacent, so the waterways are the fastest road across the map.
  • Stills: Every District has a boiler printed on the board, numbered 2–12. The moment you Control the District its Still is active: it fires when its number is brewed, and risks Blowback when the leftovers match.

The Currencies of the Underworld

To rule the city you'll need more than bullets. Two resources fuel the hustle, and a third buys power:

  • Cash: Expands your crew. Spend it to Recruit and Secure turf. Earn it by Unloading, Extorting, and completing Contracts.
  • Liquor Barrels: Your lifeblood. All hooch is born as Moonshine at your Stills; push it to a Dock and Trade it up to Havana Rum. Both sell for the same cash; only Rum buys back Influence (see Unload).

Influence: Currency of Power

Influence is your political clout. You start the game with 7 markers (gain more by buying Power Broker Favors). These markers cycle through your Ledger:

  1. The Reserves: Uncommitted Influence markers stack here. Use these to stake new Contracts.
  2. Fund (Shadows Phase): Move up to 5 markers from your Reserves onto your Ledger slots. Excess markers stay in the Reserves and cannot fund Plays today.
  3. Execute (Operations Phase): Spend markers from your Ledger slots to make Plays.
  4. Resolve (After the Play): Quiet Plays return spent markers to your Reserves. If the Play generates Heat, the marker is locked onto the communal Heat Track instead.

Kingpin's Tip: Capacity, Not Actions

Extra Influence does not buy more daily actions; your Ledger is strictly capped at 5 slots. Deep Reserves simply give you the capacity to absorb Heat, survive Police Raids, and stake multiple Contracts without crippling your daily hustle. You can't out-action your rivals; you have to out-think them.

Territory: Lines in the Concrete

An empire isn't built on bullets alone; it's built on logistics, on moving product and drawing borders your rivals fear to cross. Your muscle is spread thin, so a well-placed Safehouse and a few guards are often enough to keep the peace.

1. Control: Boots on the Ground

You can't tax what you don't hold. You Control a District if you have at least one Mobster (Boss or Runner) OR a Safehouse present there.

  • Instant Possession: Control shifts the moment your piece enters a District. (Moving through an unoccupied District claims it for that instant.)
  • Abandonment: A barrel of gin can't hold a street corner. If your last Mobster leaves and no Safehouse remains to anchor the block, you lose Control at once. Any Liquor left behind waits to be stolen.
  • The Golden Rule of Turf: During the Shadows and Reckoning phases, there can be only one mob in a District. While you hold a District, rivals cannot enter without triggering combat.
  • Contested Ground: During a firefight, the original Occupier retains Control (and all defensive bonuses) until their last piece falls or retreats.

2. Safe vs. Hostile Turf

Knowing where you can move your hooch is the difference between a payday and a bloodbath.

  • Safe Districts: Any District you Control, plus any Defenseless one (no rival Mobsters, no Police Squad). Unattended Liquor and unguarded rival Safehouses don't make turf hostile; walk in, claim them, and take the Safehouse for your own.
  • Hostile Districts: Any District guarded by rival Mobsters. Stepping onto Hostile turf immediately leaves your Mobsters Pinned (see Combat: Pinned).
  • The Blue Wall: A District holding a Police Squad is Impassable: no Moving, Pushing, or Attacking in, no storming or bribing the law. Any Speakeasy or Still the Squad sits on is padlocked (shut for everyone, held by no one for Titles) until it moves on. A padlocked Still goes dormant: it brews nothing and ignores Blowbacks. The four Squads are automated, Borough-locked patrols, one per mainland Borough, that move only during a Raid (see Police Raids).

3. Connection: The Network

Knowing the layout is key to flanking enemies and slipping blockades. Districts are Connected if:

  • Land & Bridges: They share a physical border or are linked by a Bridge.
    (e.g., Williamsburg connects to Red Hook, Brownsville, Corona, and Astoria by land, and to Five Points by Bridge).
  • Waterways (Docks): All Dock Districts connect to each other across the water as if they were adjacent.
    (e.g., You can Move directly from West Side to Throggs Neck in one Play).

Your Goal: Earning Respect

Respect is the only currency that matters in Moonshine Kingdom. Earn it two ways: claim Titles by controlling the city, and complete Contracts: jobs too loud to ignore.

1. Titles: Carving Up the City

Titles are the proof that the street belongs to you. The Borough Deeds, the Ward Boss, and the Harbormaster all follow the same hard rule:

Titles aren't owned, they're held. Every Reckoning Phase the city counts heads again: for each Title, the boss with a strict majority (more Districts of that type than any single rival) claims the card, and it changes hands the night a rival pulls ahead. Tie at the top or abandon the turf, and the Title sits empty until a sole leader emerges. Police never count as rivals; their turf counts as empty (see Stake Your Claim).

Borough Deeds

Seizing a Borough turns muscle into empire. Hold more Districts in a Borough than any rival to claim its Deed. Every Borough Deed is worth 2 Respect.

The Squeeze: A Deed also fattens the shakedown. While you hold a Borough's Deed, any Districts you Control in that specific Borough pay out $200 when you use the Extort Power Play — double the usual $100. Respect and a richer shakedown; that's all a Deed buys.

Ward Boss

The Boroughs make headlines, but the Wards decide who owns the block. Hold more Ward Districts than any rival to claim the Ward Boss card for 2 Respect, plus the Union Tax: whenever a rival Recruits, $100 per Runner from their hiring cost goes to you instead of the bank.

Harbormaster

You can't stop a man from buying a ferry ticket, but you can damn sure stop his cargo. Hold more Dock Districts than any rival to claim the Harbormaster card for 2 Respect, and with it the shipping lanes. While you hold this Title, no rival may carry Liquor barrels across the water, by Move, Advance, or Push, without your say-so. Empty Mobsters still cross freely. Wave a rival through for free, or name your price in Cash.

The Harlem Knights' Skiff Play runs hidden jetties and rowboats; it bypasses the blockade entirely.

2. Contracts: Forge Your Legend

A mob's reputation isn't built on promises; it's built on results. Contracts are the high-stakes jobs that turn a corner hustler into a Kingpin.

Every Contract demands a specific Objective by a merciless Deadline. They come in three escalating tiers:

  • Gigs (1 Day / 1 Respect): Quick street hustles. Keeping the lights on and the cops paid off.
  • Rackets (2 Days / 3 Respect): Mid-level capers that take turf, muscle, and a solid plan.
  • Scores (3 Days / 5 Respect): Front-page heists. Bloody legends they write ballads about.

The Ticking Clock

Staking a Contract is a promise to powerful people. Place Influence markers from your Reserves onto the card equal to its deadline (1, 2, or 3); they're locked in planning, and one returns each Reckoning. When you pull the last marker, The Deadline hits and the objective is checked against the board at that exact moment; yesterday's turf counts for nothing. Your clients don't care about excuses.

  • Completed: Meet the objective and you deliver — add the card to your Respect pile and collect your Take.
  • Botched: Miss the mark and you fail — discard it face-up into its deck's discard pile. In this town, nobody pays for 'almost'.

The Take

Pulling off a job doesn't just buy Respect; it buys bullets. When you complete a Contract, the client immediately pays out a one-time cash reward:

  • Gig: $500
  • Racket: $1,500
  • Score: $3,000

The Full Set: Notoriety

The streets remember a crew that can pull off any job. Complete a full set — 1 Gig, 1 Racket, and 1 Score — and you claim a Notoriety Card worth +2 Respect. Set that trio aside; a second set earns a second card. A specialist gets by. A legend does it all.

The Commission

Contracts forge your legend, but they don't buy you a chair at the table. That seat belongs to The Commission, and it's paid for in bribes, not bullets. Court all four Power Broker Favors (Walker, Rothstein, St. Clair, and Guinan) and a Commission Card is yours — the standing the crown demands (see Favors and Winning the Game). Respect makes you feared; the Commission makes you untouchable. You need both.

Kingpin's Tip: The Leadership Vacuum

Foot soldiers don't negotiate with high rollers; you need a living Boss to green-light a job. Because Contracts are staked at dawn, but a new Boss can only be anointed during the Day, a rival who puts your Don in the morgue will freeze your ability to start new jobs the next morning. If you know a rival is gearing up for a massive Score, a targeted hit is the best way to stall their operation. Protect your Boss, or your empire stops moving.

Setup: Carving Out Your Corner

  1. Prepare the City:
    Unfold the Game Board and lay out the Cash and Dice.
  2. Stack the Whispers:
    Shuffle the Gig, Racket, and Score decks and place them face-down beside the board. Keep the Shylock's Mark and Notoriety cards close by; someone will need a loan, and someone will complete a set. Leave all other decks in the Supply.
  3. Claim Your Crew:
    Shuffle the Playbooks and deal one to each player. Take your Ledger and your muscle: 1 Boss, 15 Runners, and 1 Safehouse.
  4. Establish Turf:
    Nobody starts on Staten Island; return its Deed to the box. Shuffle the four remaining Borough Deeds and deal one to each player — your home turf. Garrison its three Districts: 1 Safehouse, 1 Boss, and 2 Runners in your Ward, 3 Runners at your Dock, and 3 Runners in your Speakeasy.

    Start the Boilers: A Still runs the moment you Control its block, so your three starting Districts are already cooking from Day One. Every other basement waits for you to seize the corner.

    The Blue Wall: Place 1 Police Squad on each High Society Speakeasy, one per mainland Borough (4 in all). These $500 joints start padlocked and impassable, so the mobs fight over the $300 scraps until a Raid throws the premium spots open.

  5. Get Your Cut:
    A rising kingpin's bankroll: take $1,500 cash and 7 Influence markers; slot 5 onto your Ledger, drop the other 2 into your Reserves.
  6. Turn Order (Who Strikes First):
    Take one set of four Turn Tokens as today's set and randomly deal one to each player (#1 upwards) to set the morning's initiative. Keep the other set nearby as tomorrow's set; those decide the next Day's order.

    In this city, the fast eat the dead. When you run out of Influence or choose to Lay Low, claim the lowest-numbered token still left in tomorrow's set. Snag #1 and you shoot first tomorrow.

    Day One, The Storm (4-Player only): A filthy sea closes the waterways on Day 1: nothing crosses open water (no Moving or Pushing barrels between Docks, no Skiff). Land and bridges stay open; the waters calm at dawn on Day 2. 2- and 3-player games have no storm. Nobody runs a blockade in a hurricane.

Gameplay: Run the Streets

Days unfold in three phases:

Phase 1: Shadows

The city wakes to the hiss of steam.

1. The Brew

The boss holding Turn Token #1 rolls the communal pool: 1 White Die, plus Red Dice equal to Player Count + 1 (e.g., 4 players: 1 White, 5 Red). The White die is The Public Mash, the communal swill the whole city is cooking today, and stays on the table all morning.

In Turn Order, each boss drafts exactly 1 Red die. Your Brew Number is the White die plus your drafted Red die. Every active Still you Control matching this number fires, brewing Moonshine using The Muscle Ratio. Your brew is yours alone; rival Stills stay cold. (If the barrel supply runs dry while several of your Stills share the number, you choose which get the last barrels.)

Crack the Whip: If your Boss is in the District when its Still fires, it brews +1 Barrel. The old man watching makes the mash move faster — but he's standing beside the boiler if it blows.

2. The Blowback

Add the leftover Red (the single die no one drafted) to the White: this is the Blowback Number, and the last boss to draft chooses which die gets left behind. Cheap pipes, high pressure, and the boilers blow. The Blowback is communal: every active, Controlled Still matching it bursts, no matter which mob owns the block.

Tally casualties in that District using The Muscle Ratio (Runners take the shrapnel first; an unprotected Boss is killed). The crew takes its losses, patches up the boiler, and holds the block: it keeps its barrels, keeps its turf, and the Still runs again tomorrow.

Kingpin's Tip: The White die is public, so every Red in the pool is a Blowback waiting to happen. Sometimes the smart draft isn't the die that fires your best Still; it's the one that can't be left behind to burn it down. The first seat brews richest. The last seat holds the match.

3. Grease the Wheels

  • Stake Contracts: No job moves without the Boss's say-so, and a Boss six feet under gives no orders. Reveal Contracts from your hand. Place Influence markers from your Reserves equal to each card's Days to Deadline (1, 2, or 3). You may stake as many as you can afford. These markers are locked; they cannot fund Plays until returned at Reckoning.
  • Fund the Ledger: Move up to 5 markers from your Reserves onto your Ledger slots. Markers left in your Reserves cannot be spent on Plays today.

Phase 2: Operations

Operations runs in Turn Order, one Play at a time.

  1. Make a Play: Spend Influence to perform 1 Action (a Play): either a Standard Play (Move, Recruit, Push, etc.) or a Power Play (e.g., Unload).
  2. Resolve Combat: If your Play triggers combat, resolve it fully before the Play ends.
  3. The Cost of Doing Business: Remove the spent marker(s) from your Ledger and return them to your Reserves — unless the Play generated Heat, in which case 1 spent marker locks onto the Heat Track instead.
  4. Laying Low: When you run out of Influence (or choose to step off the street early), you must Lay Low:
    1. Clear any remaining markers from your Ledger to your Reserves.
    2. Claim the lowest-numbered token left in tomorrow's set (claim #1 to guarantee you strike first tomorrow).
Standard Plays (Cost: 1 Influence)
Move Move any number of Mobsters (carrying max 1 barrel each) to one Connected District. Entering rival turf immediately leaves your Mobsters Pinned, and the Occupier gets a Standoff choice first. You cannot Move into a District held by a Police Squad; the Blue Wall is impassable. (Moving in with no Influence left is legal — it may pressure a Fold — but if the Occupier stands firm you have nothing to spend and must Fall Back at once.)
Push Redistribute any number of barrels back and forth between a District you Control and one Connected District, in a single Play. Use it to reorganize your own supply lines, or to swap inventory with a rival (with their permission); barrels can flow both ways at once. Any barrels you Push into a rival's District immediately become their property. Thanks for the donation. You cannot Push barrels into a Police Squad's District.
Recruit Hire Runners for $500 per Runner, in any Ward you Control or in the District holding your Safehouse. (Union Tax: If a rival holds the Ward Boss card, you must hand them a $100 cut for every Runner hired).
Hustle Draw 2 Contracts, then discard down to your Hand Limit of 3. If a deck runs dry, shuffle its discards into a new draw deck. Limit: once per Day.
Secure Move your operation for $500. Relocate your Safehouse to a District you Control, pulling it off its previous block. You only run one hideout, so Securing new turf means abandoning the old.
Trade At a Dock District you Control, swap any number of Moonshine barrels sitting in that District for Havana Rum, at a 1:1 ratio. A quiet dockside exchange, and the only way Rum enters the game. Rum sells for the same cash as Moonshine, but pays a Kickback when Unloaded, recycling spent Influence back onto your board. Trade up, or watch the rich crews lap you.
Borrow Sit down with Shylock: take $1,500 from the bank and his Mark. You may hold only 1 Mark at a time, and no debtor is ever crowned; repay $3,000 at any time to clear it (see Shylock's Mark).

Power Plays

Power Plays cost 2 Influence, but still count as a single Play in the turn cycle.

Power Plays (Cost: 2 Influence)
Unload The Address Sets the Price: At a Speakeasy you Control, sell Moonshine or Rum for the same cash. The price is the joint, not the bottle: a flat $300 per barrel at most Speakeasies, a massive $500 at a High Society joint. Cheap swill in a swanky room still drinks like champagne.

The Kickback: Moonshine pays cash only; Havana Rum pays cash plus 1 Kickback per barrel: for each Rum sold, move 1 spent Influence marker from your Reserves into an empty Ledger slot. (Your board caps at 5; Kickbacks beyond that are wasted.)

The Greed Tax: Unloading 4+ barrels in one Play generates Heat. If two players' markers paid for it, you choose which goes to the Heat Track. Max safe delivery is 3, unless you hold Stephanie St. Clair's Favor.
Payoff The right friends don't come cheap, and you shake one hand at a time. Put a Power Broker on your payroll: pay the bank their fee and take their Favor Card plus 1 permanent Influence marker for your Reserves. Any broker, in any order, the moment you can afford them. Collect all four and you claim a Commission Card. Once per broker, per player (see Favors).
Extort Shake down the locals. Once per Day, earn $100 for each District you Control anywhere on the map. Shaking down the streets is a brutal, noisy business; this Play always generates Heat , however many Districts pay. (A Borough's Deed sweetens the take: Districts you Control in that Borough pay $200 each instead. See Borough Deeds.)
Rat There's no honor among thieves when the sirens wail. Drop a dime on your rivals: with at least 1 marker on the Heat Track, trigger an immediate Police Raid. Every Squad prowls toward the freshest Heat it can reach, so whoever fired the last loud shot bleeds worst (see Police Raids).

The Mark of the Snitch: Ratting is the ultimate street sin. Making this Play, you take the Rat Card from the supply (or snatch it from the rival who holds it) — even if you already hold it, just to watch your rivals burn. A pariah does no business: while you hold the card you cannot Hustle and cannot be crowned (see A Kingpin Must Have Honor). When a Raid boils over on its own, nobody takes the blame; the card stays put.

The Mark Dies with the Don: If your Boss is killed or arrested, return the Rat Card to the supply. He takes the shame to his grave.
Rise Boss in the morgue? Time to anoint a successor. Available only while your Boss is off the board. Appoint a new Boss ($0) in any District you Control, or any with no rival Mobsters or Safehouses (unattended Liquor and Stills don't count), and spawn 2 free Runners with them. You may also Secure that District for free, relocating your Safehouse there.

Mob Signature Plays

Every crew has tricks up its sleeve: signature moves to outfox, out-muscle, or outrun the competition. Each mob has two unique plays.

Sicilian Syndicate

Old blood, older rules. An insult to the family is a death sentence.

Hit (Cost: 2) The family doesn't fight foot soldiers; it goes for the head. While Pinned in a rival District, initiate a special Open Fire. Both sides roll simultaneously as normal. However, if you score any hits, the rival Boss takes the first hit instead of a Runner. Generates Heat if you kill.
Consigliere (Cost: 1) One phone call and the trouble disappears. Remove 1 Influence marker of your choice from the Heat Track and return it to its owner's Reserves (move other markers down to fill gaps if required).

Hell's Kitchen Irish

They brew it strong and fight dirty. Why buy bullets when a brick works just fine?

Courage (Cost: 0) Before you roll dice for an Ambush or for an Open Fire that you initiated, you may discard 1 of your Liquor barrels located in that District to gain +1 Threat for that roll. You cannot use Courage to boost your defense when a rival opens fire on you.
Plunder (Cost: 1) Initiate a special attack: each of your hits steals 1 Liquor barrel of your choice from the target District instead of killing. Carry the barrels off if you Advance. Occupier hits still kill. You may also Plunder when you Ambush (Cost: 0). Plunder never generates Heat.

East Side Vipers

They move like smoke and strike like snakes. You won't know they're there until the shots come from the shadows.

Stealth (Cost: 1) Move up to 5 Mobsters (no Liquor) into a Connected rival District without triggering a Standoff (the Occupier cannot Ambush or Fold). You cannot end your Play in this state. You must immediately spend another Influence to either Open Fire on the District, or Advance to a Connected Safe District.
Firepower Pay a flat $200 when you Open Fire or Ambush to roll exactly one additional attack die (up to a maximum of 5). You cannot pay extra to buy multiple dice. Cannot be used when defending Open Fire.

Harlem Knights

Dressed to kill, literally. The Knights hit from the water and leave warehouses burning.

Skiff (Cost: 1) Move up to 5 Mobsters (with or without Liquor) from a Coastal District you Control to any other Coastal District. A Coastal District is any District touching water, so this reaches far beyond the Docks. If you enter a rival District, the standard Standoff applies (the Occupier may Ambush, Hold Fire, or Fold). Skiff is a Play on your own turn, not an escape hatch: you cannot use it to flee combat or a Police Raid.
Torch (Cost: 2) Burn down a Safehouse in a rival District. To perform this Play, you must have at least one Runner Pinned in the target District. Sacrifice one of those Pinned Runners; he strikes the match and goes up with the flames (return him to your supply). The rival's Safehouse is immediately destroyed (return it to their supply). Any rival Mobsters and Liquor in the District are unharmed and retain Control. This Play generates Heat. (A Defenseless Safehouse needs no Torch: just Move in and destroy or take it over.)

Phase 3: Reckoning

Once the guns go quiet, the city settles its accounts. Each Reckoning stage resolves simultaneously for all players.

1. The Sweep

The cops don't mind a hustle, but they hate an army.
The Police sweep the streets. In any District with more than 5 Mobsters, remove excess Runners to bring the count down to 5, unless you hold Mayor Jimmy Walker's Favor.

2. Stake Your Claim

Titles prove you run the streets, and the streets are re-counted every night. Re-evaluate every Title (the Borough Deeds, the Ward Boss, and the Harbormaster) against the board exactly as it stands now. Yesterday's holdings count for nothing.

  • The Sole Leader Holds It: For each Title, whoever holds a strict majority of that turf (more of it than any single rival) takes or keeps the card.
  • Or Nobody Holds It: If no single boss leads (two or more rivals are tied for the most, or the turf sits empty), the Title returns to (or stays in) the Supply. Police never count as rivals; turf they hold counts as empty for every Title check.

3. Settle Accounts

Remove 1 marker from each of your active Contracts and return it to your Reserves. Contract objectives are checked only now, after the Sweep and Title checks resolve.

  • The Deadline: If you removed the final marker, the deadline has arrived. You cannot back out of a promised job. If your board state meets the objective right now, the Contract is Completed (add it to your Respect pile and collect the Take). If you missed the objective, the Contract is Botched (discard it face-up into its deck's discard pile for nothing).
  • Notoriety Check: The moment a completed Contract gives you a full set (1 Gig, 1 Racket, 1 Score), claim a Notoriety Card (+2 Respect) and set that set aside (see Contracts).
  • Commission Check: The Commission Card is earned during Operations, the moment you Payoff your fourth Power Broker Favor (see Favors), not at Reckoning.

The Next Day...

If any Kingpin holds a Commission Card, 15+ Respect, and their honor, the game ends immediately! (see Winning the Game).

If not, the war continues. The Turn Tokens you claimed tonight become today's set, the other set becomes tomorrow's, and the next Day's Shadows Phase begins in the new Turn Order.

Winning the Game: The Final Reckoning

The war for New York ends during the Reckoning Phase, the night a player holds a Commission Card (earned by collecting all four Power Broker Favors) and 15+ Respect, with their honor intact. Victory is checked only at Reckoning; a crown seized at noon can still slip away before nightfall.

1. A Seat on The Commission

Any thug can fire a Tommy gun, but it takes a true operator to buy the whole council. When you Payoff your fourth Power Broker (Walker, Rothstein, St. Clair, and Guinan), you immediately take a Commission Card. That's $10,000 in fees, so the crown is out of reach until your liquor economy is humming. A player may only ever claim one.

2. Earning Respect

Legitimacy means nothing if the streets don't fear you. You need 15 Respect to claim the crown. To calculate your Respect, tally the spoils of your empire:

  • Borough Turf: Gain 2 Respect for each Borough Deed you hold.
  • Ward Boss: Gain 2 Respect if you hold the Ward Boss card at the end of the game.
  • Harbormaster: Gain 2 Respect if you hold the Harbormaster card at the end of the game.
  • Criminal Legends: Gain 1, 3, or 5 Respect for each completed Contract in your Respect pile. Turf and jobs are the surest roads to 15.
  • Notoriety: Gain 2 Respect for each Notoriety Card you hold (one per completed set of Gig, Racket, and Score).

3. A Kingpin Must Have Honor

The Commission crowns no Rat and no debtor: while you hold the Rat Card or an unpaid Shylock's Mark, you cannot win, whatever your Respect. In the Blood Oath, neither can your Alliance. Shed the Mark, square the debt, then take the throne.

The Final Standoff

If multiple bosses hold a Commission Card and 15+ Respect on the same night, there's no war left to fight; The Commission simply opens the books and weighs one empire against the other:

  1. Total Respect: The highest Respect takes the crown.
  2. Cold Hard Cash: If deadlocked, the Kingpin with the fattest bankroll buys the throne (include the loose change printed on your Playbook).

There are no shared victories in Moonshine Kingdom. You either rule the city alone, or bleed out in the gutter.

Favors: Friends in High Places

The Power Brokers: Loyalty on Tap

Money talks, but the right friends scream. These brokers don't carry guns; they carry the city.

Every broker has a price, and in this city everyone's for sale. Buying a Favor is the Payoff Power Play (see Power Plays): pay the bank their fee, take their Favor Card, and add 1 permanent Influence marker to your Reserves. A Favor earns no Respect on its own. Courting a broker takes a real sit-down, so it's one handshake per Play, not the whole council.

The Whole Council: The four Favors run $10,000 in total (and hand you 4 permanent Influence along the way). Collect all four and you immediately take a Commission Card, your seat at the table and the gateway to victory (see Winning the Game). This is the only way to earn one.

Honor Among Thieves: A Favor and its Influence marker stay with you for good — unless you break a binding deal (see Cooperation).

Shylock's Mark

When the walls close in, Shylock offers a lifeline made of barbed wire. Take his cash to survive today, but the debt will bleed you tomorrow. "My bond cries out for its pound of flesh."

  • The Loan: Make the Borrow Standard Play (1 Influence) to take a Shylock's Mark card and $1,500. You may only hold 1 active Mark at a time.
  • Paying the Piper: Clear the debt by paying $3,000 to the bank at any time, then discard the card. Shylock takes back twice what he gave.
  • The Toll: While your Mark is unpaid you cannot be crowned (see A Kingpin Must Have Honor). Square up before the books close.

Cooperation: Puppets in the Smoke

Trust is fleeting, but even sharp kingpins need rivals. Every favor granted might be a knife in the back.

Pulling the Strings (Puppeteering)

At any time during the Operations Phase, you may move Influence markers from your Ledger to a rival's Ledger (up to their 5 marker cap). This is not a Play and does not end your turn.

  • The Favor: You lend your name and network. The rival may use these markers as their own, now or later.
  • The Risk: If the rival spends your marker on a Play that generates Heat, your marker goes to the Heat Track. Otherwise, it returns to your Reserves once used.

The Broker's Word: A Binding Handshake

A Mobster's word is cheap. When a deal needs teeth, have a Power Broker vouch for you.

Making the Deal

To make a binding deal, a player must have at least one Favor Card.

  1. State the Terms & Deadline: The terms must be clear, and you must agree on a specific deadline (e.g., "before the Day ends," or "until you pay me $1,000").
  2. Stake the Favor: The player stakes an available Favor Card, placing it between both players. Each Broker may only vouch for one deal at a time.

Example: "The Mayor vouches for me. If you let me pass through your Borough unharmed, I promise to give you $1,000 before Day's end."

Honoring & Breaking Deals

Deals resolve the moment their conditions are met or failed.

  • Deal Honored: The moment the terms are met, the deal is complete. The Favor Card is immediately returned to its owner.
  • Deal Broken: If the agreed deadline passes without the terms being met, or if a player violates a standing agreement for any reason (including third-party interference), the Broker's trust is shattered and your word becomes garbage. The owner must discard the Favor Card and lose its permanent Influence marker.

Deals on the Side

You may wheel and deal outside the rules, but only Cash can be physically traded off the board. Hand it over anytime, no Influence required.

Barrel Exchanges: Liquor stays on the board. To swap barrels with a rival, use a single Push Play: with their permission, you can shift any barrels either way between your District and a Connected one, clearing the whole trade in one Play with no return turn needed.

Combat Rules: Blood on the Streets

Violence isn't a backup plan; it's the cost of doing business. Smart Kingpins know a drawn gun can be stronger than a fired one. When talk fails, step inside and clear the room. (Nothing is ever destroyed for good: every casualty — killed, sacrificed, or arrested — returns to its owner's supply to be Recruited again, and a burned Safehouse can be re-established with a later Secure.)

1. The Rules of Engagement

Violence runs on two numbers: The Muscle Ratio (how many dice you roll) and Threat (what number kills).

Calculating Threat

Your Base Threat is Level 1 (kill on 5+). Bonuses stack, to a maximum of Level 4:

  • Boss +1 Threat — if your Boss is in the fight.
  • Safehouse +2 Threat — the Occupier defending Open Fire on their own Secured District (not on the Ambush shot).
  • Ambush +1 Threat — the Occupier's first shot, on the opening Ambush only.

2. The Standoff: Crossing the Line

Every firefight is just two questions, asked in order:

  • Step 1 — the Occupier answers: Ambush, Hold Fire, or Fold.
  • Step 2 — the Invader chooses (and keeps choosing until the Pin is resolved): Open Fire, Advance, or Fall Back.

When an opponent Moves into a District you Control, the game pauses on Step 1. As the Occupier, you must immediately choose one of three responses:

  1. Ambush: You greet the invaders with lead.
    • Free Shot: Only the Occupier rolls dice.
    • Bonus: Gain +1 Threat.
    • Casualties: Hits remove Invaders (Runners first). No Heat. Invaders are now Pinned.
  2. Hold Fire: You stand your ground but don't shoot. Maybe they're just passing through. The Invaders are now Pinned.
  3. Fold (Cost: 0): You surrender the District. You may immediately move your Mobsters to one Connected Safe District.
    • Panic: Fleeing Mobsters cannot carry Liquor; any barrels are left behind. You also cannot pack up a building; your Safehouse remains.
    • The Takeover: The Invader immediately claims Control, captures dropped Liquor, and may take over or destroy your Safehouse. The Invaders are not Pinned; their Play ends.

3. Pinned: Fight or Flight (Step 2)

While Pinned, your Mobsters cannot make normal Plays. As the Invader you must resolve the standoff before your turn moves on: spend Influence to Open Fire or Advance, or Fall Back for free. Keep choosing until the Pin is resolved, or you run dry and must Fall Back. Torch, Plunder, Courage, and Firepower still work while Pinned.

The Cost of Pride: A real Kingpin knows an all-out firefight is bad business. Burning 3 or 4 Influence to keep opening fire on one stubborn alley may win it, but it leaves rivals free across the city. Talk is cheap; bullets cost time.

Open Fire (Cost: 1) Fight for the District. Both sides roll simultaneously.
  • Casualties: Both sides apply hits (Runners first). The Occupier loses the Ambush bonus but keeps Safehouse/Boss bonuses.
  • Heat: The law doesn't blame a man for defending his own corner, so the Occupier never draws Heat. The Invader gains Heat only if their dice score 1+ kills. (If the Invader misses entirely but takes casualties from the Occupier, no Heat is generated.)
  • Repeat: If both sides stand, remain Pinned. Spend another Influence to Open Fire again.
Advance (Cost: 1) Shoot your way out. Move Pinned Mobsters (and up to 1 barrel each) to any Safe Connected District, including across water via Docks. You cannot enter hostile turf.
Fall Back (Cost: 0)
(Invader Only)		 Cut and run. Retreat to the origin District you entered from. Carried Liquor is left behind for the Occupier.
If you have 0 Influence remaining, you must Fall Back.

4. Victory & Consequences

Combat ends when one side is eliminated or the Invader retreats.

If the Occupier Holds: The District remains theirs. Liquor dropped by dead Invaders becomes the Occupier's property.

If the Invader Wins: The moment the last of the Occupier's pieces falls or flees, you claim Control of the District.

  • The Spoils: Any Liquor in the District is now yours.
  • Safehouses: A rival Safehouse in the District is destroyed (remove it), unless you take it over instead: immediately Secure the District for free by relocating your Safehouse there from wherever it sat before.

Heat: Playing with Fire

Heat measures how hard the law is breathing down your neck. The Heat Track is a 5-space powder keg: when you generate Heat, place one Influence marker on it. Played right, it's a weapon; played wrong, it's a noose.

Drawing Heat

These noisy Plays push the track up one notch (any Play marked with the flame icon ):

  • Blood on the Pavement: If an Invader's dice inflict 1+ kills in combat, they add 1 marker at the end of the Play. A multi-round firefight generates Heat only once.
  • Shakedowns Get Loud: The Extort Power Play always draws Heat , however many Districts you squeeze.
  • The Greed Tax: Unloading 4+ barrels in a single Play draws Heat .

Police Raids: The Boiling Point

A Raid triggers two ways: the 5th marker lands on the track naturally, or a rival makes the Rat Play. Either way, pause the turn; the city's on lockdown.

A Raid puts every Squad on the move. Resolve them one at a time, in the numbered Borough order printed on the board, each finishing before the next stirs. Four precincts kicking doors in, block by block.

1. The Most Wanted List

A Squad looks only at the Districts directly Connected to it within its own Borough, and chases the freshest gunshot it can hear:

  1. Follow the Smoke: Among the mobs in those Districts, the Squad steps toward whoever's marker sits furthest right on the Heat Track. Quiet money always walks free.
  2. The Big Bust: If that rival holds more than one of those Districts, the Squad kicks in the one with the most Barrels (the cops follow the contraband). Tied? They hit the hottest boiler: the District ranked highest on its Pressure Strip. The louder the boiler, the harder the door comes off.
  3. Cold Trail: If no reachable District carries Heat, the Squad does not move and holds its corner.

2. The Scatter

The Squad takes one step onto the chosen District and kicks the doors in:

  • Condemned: Any Safehouse there is destroyed and all Liquor seized (both to supply).
  • Run For It: The crew bolts to a single Connected Safe District — never back into the one the Squad advanced from, and no grabbing barrels on the way out.
  • Cornered: With no Connected Safe District to flee to (hemmed in by rivals, water, the Blue Wall, the city's edge, or only the road the cops came in on), the whole crew is arrested (return every arrested piece to your supply). Leave a back door, or don't leave at all.

The Squad now holds the District as an impassable wall, padlocking any Speakeasy or Still beneath it until a later Raid. A crew fleeing one Squad may land in the path of the next; the night is young.

The Aftermath: Once every Squad has moved, all Heat markers return to their owners' Reserves and the paused turn resumes.

The Blood Oath: 4-Player Team Variant

When the heat boils over, enemies become brothers. The Feds are kicking down doors and lone wolves are slaughtered. The Blood Oath is the recommended 4-player mode: a cutthroat free-for-all where escalating police pressure eventually forces the city into two permanent 2v2 alliances.

Setup: The Ticking Clock

Set up as a standard 4-player match, with one addition: place the Federal Crackdown Tracker near the Heat Track with a token on space 1. Every time a Police Raid triggers, advance the token one space.

The Sit-Down

The moment the token reaches space 10 on the Crackdown Tracker, the underworld snaps. Pause the game. The four independent mobs immediately form two permanent alliances.

  1. Rank the Bosses: Tally everyone's current Respect (scoring held Titles and completed Contracts). Tiebreaker: Total Cash on hand.
  2. Forge the Families: The player in 1st Place joins forces with the player in 4th Place. The player in 2nd Place pairs with the player in 3rd Place.
  3. Set the Targets: Each Alliance adds 10 to its combined Respect: that number is its Target. From here, it's a clean race to earn 10.

New Rules of Engagement

One goal, two crews. You still run your own operations.

  • Separate Ledgers: You do not share Cash, Influence, Contracts, or Hand Limits, and you still take individual turns in Turn Order.
  • Blood is Thicker: You cannot attack your partner or their property (no Ambush, Open Fire, Hit, Plunder, or Torch). All other rules stand: partner turf is still Hostile (Pinned there, your only outs are Advance or Fall Back), the Golden Rule of Turf means you cannot share a District at Day's end, and Liquor Pushed into their District becomes theirs.
  • Puppeteering: Your strongest weapon. You may freely lend Influence to your partner's Ledger to fund their turn (standard Heat rules apply).

Claiming the Empire

The Commission only recognizes unified power. To win, an Alliance must collectively hold at least 1 Commission Card and reach its Target. If met during the Reckoning Phase, the game ends immediately: first family to the finish takes the city.

The New Kings of New York

If both Alliances hit their Targets at the same Reckoning, the crown goes to the pair furthest over its Target; still deadlocked, the fattest combined bankroll buys the throne.

The Capo di Tutti Capi: There are no truly shared victories... even in a team. Within the winning Alliance, the player who contributed the most Respect to the final combined total is declared the Boss of Bosses and the true Kingpin of New York. Your partner doesn't get a crown; they just get the privilege of kissing your ring.
