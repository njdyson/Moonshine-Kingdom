RULEBOOK

A Game of Bootlegging, Backstabbing, and Gang Warfare.

New York City, 1926. Prohibition was supposed to dry the city out. Instead it lit a fuse. Every basement is a brewery, every back room a bank, and four mobs are clawing for the throne of the liquor trade. Bootlegging, bribery, and intimidation are your tools. Forge fragile alliances, corner the market, and turn the Police loose on your rivals. This city has a thirst that can't be quenched, and only one mob will wear its crown. Carve your name into the skyline, or vanish like cigar smoke in a hurricane.

A Day in the Life of a Kingpin

2-4 Players | 90-120 Mins

Prohibition didn't stop the drinking; it just made the price go up.

In Moonshine Kingdom, you are a rising Kingpin in 1920s New York. Prohibition turned off the taps, sparking a gold rush for anyone ruthless enough to fill the glass. Four rival mobs claw for the boroughs, and this city isn't big enough for all of you. The game plays out over a series of Days, each unfolding in three brutal phases: from the fog of morning to the bloody consequences of night.

Morning: Shadows Phase

Dawn breaks over the Hudson. Before the city wakes, the boilers burn. Fire up your Stills, eye the Jobs on the morning Market, and turn cheap mash into liquid gold.

Day: Operations Phase

The sun is up, and so is the heat. Command your Runners to smuggle product, seize turf, and strong-arm the competition. Every move costs Influence: your political clout. Use it to expand, but remember: violence is loud, and the Police are listening.

Night: Reckoning Phase

Twilight settles like a bruise. The guns go quiet, the blood washes into the gutters, and the ledger opens. Claim your turf, count the take, and let the Police sweep up the careless. Did you make a killing, or just a mess?

The Only Crown Worth Having

Cash buys bullets. But Respect buys immortality.

Most bosses end the same way: face down in an alley, name forgotten by the morning edition. Not you. You're chasing the kind of legend they whisper about fifty years on: the turf you bled for, the crowns you tore off rival heads, the jobs so brazen the papers set your name in ink an inch tall. That is Respect, the one fortune the grave can't pick from your pockets.

But the gutter only carries you so far. The real throne isn't seized at gunpoint; it's bought, from the judges, aldermen, and money men who quietly decide which animal gets to call himself King. Every one of them has a price, paid in cold cash, and each you buy off earns you a marker of Influence. Stack it high while your legend towers over the competition, and the city is yours.

Game Components: Tools of the Trade

Your whole damn operation in miniature. Every piece earns, bleeds, or burns.

The City Map (Game Board): A soot-stained blueprint of 1920s New York, carved into 25 Districts, with a shared 5-space Heat Track for Police pressure and a numbered Pressure Strip per Borough ranking its boilers (see Police Raids).

The Ledger (x4): Your back-room desk. A slotted player board where you spend the day's actions and cycle your Influence. Every move you make starts here.

Mob Pieces (x4 Sets): Each player claims a color and commands:

  • 1 Boss, 15 Runners, and 1 Safehouse: The brains, the muscle, and the one hideout the whole operation runs out of.
  • 10 Influence Markers: The currency of power. Tokens to pay for daily actions, stake Jobs, absorb Police Heat, and buy your way onto the council. Ten is the ceiling, and it's the crown.
  • 1 Mob Playbook: The family secrets: the dirty tricks and unique abilities only your mob can pull.

The Whispers (Cards): The paper trail of your empire.

  • Job Cards: Dirty work with your name on it, drafted from a face-up Market. Pull one off and it builds your legend; pass, and a rival won't.
  • Borough Deeds (5): The paper that says a Borough is yours. Hold the most turf in one and the Deed comes to you.
  • Titles (4): The Ward Boss, the Harbormaster, the Night Mayor, and Public Enemy No. 1. Crowns claimed by ruling the streets, the docks, the nightlife, or the headlines.
  • Shylock's Mark Cards: Debt trackers. A lifeline with teeth.
  • The Rat Card (1): The ultimate sin in cardboard form. A mark of shame; no Rat is ever crowned.

Turn Tokens (8): Two sets of numbered tokens (1–4). One set dictates today's initiative; the other is claimed to determine tomorrow's order.

The Turn Sequence Card (1): A quick reference for the Day's phases. Its reverse holds the Town Planning Ledger, the District roster for setup.

Liquor Barrels: The lifeblood of your operation, in two flavors: Moonshine (Grey) and Havana Rum (Brown). Cheap swill and the good stuff; nothing in between.

The Dice (11): 5 White, 5 Red, and 1 grey Mash die. White and Red settle grudges on the street and draft the morning mash; the lone Mash die does one job only: The Public Mash.

The Bankroll (Cash): Dirty, untraceable bills in denominations of $100, $500, and $1,000. The kind that never sees a bank.

Police Squads (4 Blue Pieces): The blue wall that wants to see you swing. Impassable roaming patrols that prowl toward trouble, one to a mainland Borough. At setup they padlock the four High Society Speakeasies (see The Blue Wall).

Federal Crackdown Tracker (1): A 10-space pressure clock used in the 4-player team variant.

Key Concepts: The Underworld at a Glance

Your Mob: The Brains, the Brawn, and the Bastards

From the cold steel of the Sicilian Syndicate to the fiery grit of the Hell's Kitchen Irish, each mob brings their own brand of violence to the city's bloodstained ballet. You command:

A Boss Your kingpin, the head of the snake. Bring him into a firefight to make your attacks deadlier, park him at a Still to brew an extra barrel; but if he catches a bullet, your crew is crippled until you crown a successor.
Runners Your foot soldiers: knuckle-draggers and wheelmen who haul hooch, guard your turf, and catch your bullets. You are strictly limited to 15 Runners, never enough to occupy the whole city. To expand, leave borders thin and rely on the threat of violence rather than an actual army.
Safehouse Your headquarters. You run a single Safehouse: it locks down Turf even when empty, gives a massive defensive edge in shootouts, and anchors the block where you Recruit fresh muscle. As your empire shifts, so can your base: Secure a District you Control to pull up stakes, or take over a rival's Safehouse when you overrun their turf.

The Map: Know Your Turf

Your empire is a broken city: 25 Districts across 5 Boroughs, each a flavor of poison and profit. Certain key Districts offer unique opportunities:

Speakeasies The only spots where you can Unload Liquor for cash. Most pay a flat $300 per barrel (see Unload). Hold the most drinking venues (Speakeasies and High Society joints) at Reckoning to claim the Night Mayor Title. The booze doesn't set the price; the address does.
High Society One premium Speakeasy per mainland Borough, paying a massive $500 per barrel. The four start padlocked by the Police until a Raid throws them open (see The Blue Wall).
Wards Gritty blocks where mob roots run deep. Hold the most at Reckoning to claim the Ward Boss Title. You can also hire muscle on the cheap in any Ward you Control (see Recruit).
Docks Where the rum comes ashore. At a Dock you Control you may Trade Moonshine for Havana Rum (see Trade). Hold the most at Reckoning to claim the Harbormaster Title. All Docks connect across the water as if adjacent, so the waterways are the fastest road across the map.
Stills Every District has a boiler printed on the board, numbered 2–12. The moment you Control it the Still is active: it fires when its number is brewed, and risks Blowback when the leftovers match. Each Borough's Pressure Strip ranks its boilers for Police Raids (see Police Raids).

The Currencies of the Underworld

To rule the city you'll need more than bullets. Two resources fuel the hustle, and a third buys power:

  • Cash: Expands your crew. Spend it to Recruit and Secure turf. Earn it by Unloading, Extorting, and running Odd Jobs when you Lay Low.
  • Liquor Barrels: Your lifeblood. All hooch is born as Moonshine at your Stills; run it to a Dock and Trade it up to Havana Rum. Both sell for the same cash; only Rum buys back Influence (see Unload).

Influence: Currency of Power

Influence is your political clout, and the throne itself. You start the game with 6 markers and buy more with the Bribe Play, one at a time, up to a hard ceiling of 10. The last four are the crown: hold all ten and you've bought the whole council (see Winning the Game). These markers cycle through your Ledger:

  1. The Reserves: Uncommitted Influence markers stack here. Use these to stake claimed Jobs.
  2. Fund (Shadows Phase): Fill your Ledger slots (up to 5) from your Reserves. Only Ledger markers fund Plays; any beyond 5 stay in Reserves as capacity for staking and Heat.
  3. Execute (Operations Phase): Spend markers from your Ledger slots to make Plays.
  4. Resolve (After the Play): Quiet Plays return spent markers to your Reserves. If the Play generates Heat, the marker is locked onto the communal Heat Track instead.

Kingpin's Tip: Capacity, Not Actions

Extra Influence does not buy more daily actions; your Ledger is capped at 5 slots. Deep Reserves give you the capacity to absorb Heat, survive Police Raids, and stake Jobs without crippling your daily hustle. You can't out-action your rivals; you have to out-think them.

Territory: Lines in the Concrete

An empire isn't built on bullets alone; it's built on logistics, on moving product and drawing borders your rivals fear to cross. Your muscle is spread thin, so a well-placed Safehouse and a few guards are often enough to keep the peace.

1. Control: Boots on the Ground

You can't tax what you don't hold. You Control a District if you have at least one Mobster (Boss or Runner) OR a Safehouse present there.

  • Instant Possession: Control shifts the moment your piece enters a District. (Moving through an unoccupied District claims it for that instant.)
  • Abandonment: A barrel of gin can't hold a street corner. If your last Mobster leaves and no Safehouse remains to anchor the block, you lose Control at once. Any Liquor left behind waits to be stolen.
  • The Golden Rule of Turf: During the Shadows and Reckoning phases, there can be only one mob in a District. While you hold a District, rivals cannot enter without triggering combat.
  • Contested Ground: During a firefight, the original Occupier retains Control (and all defensive bonuses) until their last piece falls or retreats.

2. Safe vs. Hostile Turf

Knowing where you can move your hooch is the difference between a payday and a bloodbath.

  • Safe Districts: Any District you Control, plus any Defenseless one (no rival Mobsters, no Police Squad). Unattended Liquor and unguarded rival Safehouses don't make turf hostile; walk in, claim them, and take the Safehouse for your own.
  • Hostile Districts: Any District guarded by rival Mobsters. Stepping onto Hostile turf immediately leaves your Mobsters Pinned (see Combat: Pinned).
  • The Blue Wall: A District holding a Police Squad is Impassable: no Moving or Attacking in, no storming or bribing the law. Any Speakeasy or Still the Squad sits on is padlocked (shut for everyone, held by no one for Titles) until it moves on. A padlocked Still goes dormant: it brews nothing and ignores Blowbacks. The four Squads are automated, Borough-locked patrols, one per mainland Borough, that move only during a Raid (see Police Raids).

3. Connection: Reading the Grid

Knowing the layout is key to flanking enemies and slipping blockades. Districts are Connected if:

  • Land & Bridges: They share a physical border or are linked by a Bridge.
    (e.g., Williamsburg connects to Red Hook, Brownsville, Corona, and Astoria by land, and to Five Points by Bridge).
  • Waterways (Docks): All Dock Districts connect to each other across the water as if they were adjacent.
    (e.g., You can Move directly from West Side to Throggs Neck in one Play).

Your Goal: Earning Respect

In this city, a reputation is worth more than any bankroll, and it's the one thing a bullet can't take back. Respect is that reputation made currency, and you build it two ways: by pulling off Jobs too loud to ignore, and by claiming Titles that prove you own the streets and the headlines alike.

1. Jobs: Forge Your Legend

Deeds and Titles pay you for what you hold; Jobs pay you for what you do. Beside the board sits the Jobs Market: the word on the street, dealt face-up in a row for every boss to read. New Jobs land beside the deck, at the fresh end, and age toward the stale end; whenever a Job leaves the row, close the gap and deal a fresh card at once. Jobs come in three escalating tiers:

  • Gigs (Stake 1 / 1 Respect): Quick street hustles. One sharp Play, little setup.
  • Rackets (Stake 2 / 3 Respect): Mid-level capers. One Play, with real turf and muscle behind it.
  • Scores (Stake 3 / 5 Respect): Front-page heists. One thunderous Play at the end of a campaign.

Claiming a Job

You may Hustle once each morning (see Grease the Wheels): claim one Job from the Market and stake Influence from your Reserves onto it equal to its Stake. Those markers sit locked until the job resolves; the bigger the promise, the longer your capital sleeps, and the longer rivals get to wreck your setup. There's no deadline: the only clock is your money. Cold feet? Walk away any time on your turn (it's not a Play): discard the card and your markers come home. Nobody signs contracts in this business.

Pulling It Off

No declarations, no paperwork: do the deed with the whole table watching and the Job completes on the spot. Take back your stake and add the card to your Respect pile; completed Jobs never return to the deck. Job verbs are your Plays; to Seize a District is to take Control of it from a rival, by gunfire or by strolling in where they left the door open.

The Play Is the Unit A Job is a deed, not a board position. When a Play ends, check it against your claimed Jobs: if the Play did the deed, the Job is done. The deed counts even if it didn't survive the Play. Kill a rival Boss, then lose the firefight: nothing un-kills him. The job pays.

2. Titles: Carving Up the City

Titles aren't owned, they're held, and only for as long as you're the biggest name on the block. Power here follows muscle: pull ahead of every rival on the turf that counts and the crown swings your way; slip behind and it swings just as fast to whoever now stands tallest. The city settles its crowns each nightfall (see Stake Your Claim), and any Title still in your grip when the books close is worth 2 Respect.

Borough Deeds

Seizing a Borough turns muscle into empire. Hold more Districts in a Borough than any single rival to claim its Deed.

Ward Boss

The Boroughs make headlines, but the Wards decide who owns the block. Hold more Ward Districts than any single rival to claim the Ward Boss Title.

Harbormaster

You can't stop a man's ferry, but you can own the pier. Hold more Dock Districts than any single rival to claim the Harbormaster Title. Every barrel of molasses lands on your docks, so you set Tomorrow's Mash: at the end of each Shadows, instead of rolling the Mash die, name any face you like (see Ferment Tomorrow's Mash). Feed your own Stills, starve the rivals'; the whole city cooks the number you choose.

Night Mayor

The sun belongs to the law; the night belongs to you. Hold more drinking venues than any single rival to claim the Night Mayor Title. Every Speakeasy counts, and so does every High Society joint: a swanky room is still a speakeasy, and the grandest jewel in the nightlife crown. (The four High Society venues start padlocked by the Police, so they only join the count once a Raid throws them open.)

Public Enemy No. 1

The streets remember a crew that can pull off any job. The moment you hold more completed sets of Jobs (1 Gig, 1 Racket, and 1 Score) than any single rival, you claim the Public Enemy No. 1 Title. A specialist gets by. A legend does it all.

The Commission

Jobs and Titles forge your legend, but they don't buy you a chair at the table. Above the sirens and the smoke sits The Commission: the judges, the aldermen, and the money men who quietly decide who gets to call himself King. Every last one of them is for sale, and they all take the same coin: cold, untraceable cash. Pay it a bribe at a time with the Bribe Play, and each envelope buys you another marker of Influence, another man in your pocket, until all 10 are yours and the whole council answers to you (see Winning the Game). Respect makes you feared; a bought Commission makes you untouchable. You need both.

Setup: Carving Out Your Corner

  1. Prepare the City:
    Unfold the Game Board and lay out the Cash and Dice. Roll the Mash die and leave it on the board: Day 1's Public Mash, fermenting since before you sat down (see The Brew).
  2. Stack the Whispers:
    Pull the Gigs from the Jobs deck, shuffle them, and deal Player Count + 1 face-up in a row: the Jobs Market. Shuffle the remaining Gigs back into the deck and set it face-down at one end of the row; that end is fresh, the far end stale. Small jobs first: nobody hands a stranger a Score. Everything else, the Titles, the Shylock's Marks, the Rat Card, waits in the Supply until it's earned; keep it within reach but off the table. A crown, a debt, a snitch's brand: nobody starts the night wearing them.
  3. Claim Your Crew:
    Shuffle the Playbooks and deal one to each player. Take your Ledger and your muscle: 1 Boss, 15 Runners, and 1 Safehouse.
  4. Establish Turf:
    Nobody starts on Staten Island; return its Deed to the box. Shuffle the four remaining Borough Deeds and deal one to each player: your home turf. Garrison its three Districts: 1 Safehouse, 1 Boss, and 2 Runners in your Ward, 3 Runners at your Dock, and 3 Runners in your Speakeasy.
  5. Raise the Blue Wall:
    Place 1 Police Squad on each High Society Speakeasy, one per mainland Borough (4 in all). These $500 joints start padlocked and impassable, so the mobs fight over the $300 scraps until a Raid throws the premium spots open.
  6. Get Your Cut:
    A rising kingpin's bankroll: take $1,500 cash and 6 Influence markers; slot 5 onto your Ledger, drop the last 1 into your Reserves.
  7. Turn Order (Who Strikes First):
    Take one set of four Turn Tokens as today's set and randomly deal one to each player (#1 upwards) to set the morning's initiative. Keep the other set nearby as tomorrow's set; those decide the next Day's order, claimed one by one as players Lay Low. In this city, the fast eat the dead.

Gameplay: Run the Streets

Days unfold in three phases:

Phase 1: Shadows

The city wakes to the hiss of steam.

1. The Brew

The Mash die already sits on the board, rolled at the end of yesterday's Shadows (Day 1: at Setup). The boss holding Turn Token #1 rolls the communal pool: Red dice equal to Player Count + 1 (4 players: 5 Red). So there's always one more Red than there are players; after everyone drafts, exactly one Red is left over.

  1. Read the Public Mash: The Mash die is The Public Mash, the swill the whole city cooks today. It stays put all morning and feeds every number below; no surprises, the table has known it since yesterday.
  2. Draft a Red, in Turn Order: Each boss takes exactly 1 Red die. Your Brew Number is Mash + your Red. Choose carefully: the Red you leave on the table may be the one that burns you (see The Blowback).
  3. Fire your Stills: Every active Still you Control matching your Brew Number brews Moonshine, per The Muscle Ratio. Your brew is yours alone; rival Stills stay cold. (If barrels run short across several of your Stills, you choose which get the last ones.)

Twist the Valves: If your Boss stands in a District when its Still fires, that Still brews +1 Barrel. The old man cranks the valves wide for one more run, but his hands are on the boiler if it blows (see The Blowback).

2. The Blowback

One Red die is left undrafted. Add that leftover Red to the Black for the Blowback Number. Cheap pipes, high pressure, and the boilers blow: the Blowback is communal, so every active, Controlled Still matching it bursts, whoever owns the block.

In each bursting District, tally casualties by The Muscle Ratio. A boiler blast plays no favourites: no Runner can throw himself over an explosion, so if your Boss stands on a bursting Still, he is the first to fall (a firefight is the only place his crew can take the hit for him). The survivors patch the boiler and hold on: they keep their barrels, keep their turf, and the Still runs again tomorrow.

Kingpin's Tip: The Mash die has been public since yesterday, so every Red in the pool is a Blowback waiting to happen. Sometimes the smart draft isn't the die that fires your best Still; it's the one you daren't leave behind to burn it down. The first seat brews richest. The last seat is stuck with whatever's left, holding the match.

3. Grease the Wheels

  • Yesterday's News: The Market moves on: discard the Job at the stale end, close the gap, and deal a fresh card (deck dry? shuffle the discards into a new one). A job the whole city passed on is this morning's fish wrap.
  • Hustle: In Turn Order, each boss may claim one Job: take the card, stake Influence from your Reserves equal to its Stake, and refill the row. One handshake a day; make it count.
  • Fund the Ledger: Load your Ledger from what's left in your Reserves, up to all 5 slots (only Ledger markers pay for Plays today). Anything above 5 stays behind as capacity: fuel to soak up Police Heat. Stake first, fund second.

4. Ferment Tomorrow's Mash

Bootlegging takes time, and cheap mash ferments overnight. Set tomorrow's Public Mash and leave the Mash die on the board: if a rival holds the Harbormaster Title, they own the molasses trade and name any face they like (1–6); otherwise, roll the Mash die. Either way, you'll play today's whole Operations Phase knowing tomorrow's number, so seize the right Stills, park the Boss, and Lay Low early for Turn Token #1 while the intel is fresh.

Phase 2: Operations

Players act in Turn Order, taking one Play each. After your Play, the turn passes to the next boss; play keeps going around the table until everyone has Laid Low for the Day.

On Your Turn: make one Play
  1. Make a Play. Spend Influence to perform 1 Action: a Standard Play or a Power Play (see the tables below).
  2. Resolve any combat the Play triggers, fully, before the Play ends. (A firefight can eat several Influence, but it's still this one turn.)
  3. Pay the cost. Return the spent marker(s) from your Ledger to your Reserves, unless the Play drew Heat, in which case 1 marker locks onto the Heat Track instead.

Then the turn passes. When it comes round again you take another Play, and so on until you run dry or choose to step off the street.

Out of Influence (or stepping off early)? Lay Low
  1. Odd Jobs: each unspent marker still in your Ledger pays you $100 (Reserve markers don't pay).
  2. Clear any remaining Ledger markers to your Reserves.
  3. Claim the lowest-numbered token left in tomorrow's set (claim #1 to strike first tomorrow).

Plays: Working the Angles

Standard Plays (Cost: 1 Influence)
Move Send any number of Mobsters and/or Liquor barrels to one Connected District. Barrels can ride with your muscle or travel alone, working your distribution network.

Into rival turf: your Mobsters are Pinned and the Occupier gets a Standoff choice first (see Combat); barrels ride in with them, and any left in a rival's District become theirs. Thanks for the donation.

The Blue Wall: you cannot Move into a District held by a Police Squad.
Recruit Hire Runners for $300 per Runner in any Ward you Control, or $400 in the District holding your Safehouse. The address sets the price; a Safehouse on the front line hires straight into the fight.
Secure Move your operation for $500. Relocate your Safehouse to a District you Control, pulling it off its previous block. You only run one hideout, so Securing new turf means abandoning the old.
Trade At a Dock District you Control, swap any number of Moonshine barrels sitting in that District for Havana Rum, at a 1:1 ratio. A quiet dockside exchange, and the only way Rum enters the game. Rum sells for the same cash as Moonshine, but pays a Kickback when Unloaded, recycling spent Influence back onto your board. Trade up, or watch the rich crews lap you.
Beg When the walls close in, Shylock offers a lifeline made of barbed wire. Take $1,500 from the bank and one of his Marks.

The Loan: There's no limit on how many Marks you carry, but Shylock keeps only 8 in his book. When all 8 are out on the street, no one Begs again until one comes back.

Paying the Piper: Clear a debt by paying $3,000 to the bank any time, then discard that Mark; each is squared one at a time. Shylock always takes back twice what he gave.

The Toll: While any Mark of yours is unpaid you cannot be crowned (see A Kingpin Must Have Honor). One Mark or five, the crown waits until every debt is square.

Power Plays

Power Plays cost 2 Influence, but still count as a single Play in the turn cycle.

Power Plays (Cost: 2 Influence)
Unload The Address Sets the Price: At a Speakeasy you Control, sell Moonshine or Rum for the same cash. The price is the joint, not the bottle: a flat $300 per barrel at most Speakeasies, a massive $500 at a High Society joint. Cheap swill in a swanky room still drinks like champagne.

The Kickback: Moonshine pays cash only; Havana Rum pays cash plus 1 Kickback per barrel: for each Rum sold, move 1 spent Influence marker from your Reserves into an empty Ledger slot. (Your board caps at 5; Kickbacks beyond that are wasted.)

The Greed Tax: Unloading 4+ barrels in one Play generates Heat. If two players' markers paid for it, you choose which goes to the Heat Track. Max safe delivery is 3; move more and the cops come sniffing.
Bribe Everyone on the Commission has a price, and you pay it in cash. Pay the bank $2,500 and take 1 permanent Influence marker for your Reserves. You may Bribe only up to a ceiling of 10 total Influence; buy the last four and the council is yours (see Winning the Game). The marker is yours for good, unless you stake it on a deal and break your word (see Cooperation).
Extort Shake down the locals. Once per Day, earn a flat $200 for each District you Control anywhere on the map. It's brutal, highly profitable, and noisy; this Play always generates Heat , however many Districts pay.
Rat There's no honor among thieves when the sirens wail. Drop a dime on your rivals: with at least 1 marker on the Heat Track, trigger an immediate Police Raid. Every Squad prowls toward the freshest Heat it can reach, so whoever fired the last loud shot bleeds worst (see Police Raids).

The Mark of the Snitch: Ratting is the ultimate street sin. Making this Play, you take the Rat Card from the supply (or snatch it from the rival who holds it). A pariah does no business: while you hold the card you cannot Hustle and cannot be crowned (see A Kingpin Must Have Honor). When a Raid boils over on its own, nobody takes the blame; the card stays put.

The Mark Dies with the Don: If your Boss is killed or arrested, return the Rat Card to the supply. He takes the shame to his grave.
Rise Boss in the morgue? Time to anoint a successor. Available only while your Boss is off the board. Appoint a new Boss ($0) in any District you Control, or any with no rival Mobsters or Safehouses (unattended Liquor and Stills don't count), and spawn 2 free Runners with them. You may also Secure that District for free.

Mob Signature Plays

Four families, four ways to make a killing. Each runs on an always-on Trait, the thing that makes them them, plus two signature Plays no rival can copy.

Sicilian Syndicate

Old blood, older rules. An insult to the family is a death sentence, and the family is Untouchable: the precinct captains are on the payroll, so the Sweep never thins their ranks. Their Districts may hold any number of Mobsters at Reckoning. Fifteen Runners only stretch so far, so massing a crew in one block leaves your borders thin, but where the Syndicate plants its flag, no one talks them down.

Hit (Cost: 2) The family doesn't fight foot soldiers; it goes for the head. While Pinned in a rival District, initiate a special attack. Both sides roll simultaneously as normal. However, if you score any hits, the rival Boss takes the first hit instead of a Runner. Generates Heat if you kill.
Consigliere (Cost: 1) One phone call and the trouble disappears. Remove 1 Influence marker of your choice from the Heat Track and return it to its owner's Reserves (slide the rest down to close the gap).

Hell's Kitchen Irish

They brew it strong and fight dirty, and they always come heavy. That's their Firepower: whenever the Irish Open Fire, they roll 1 extra attack die (up to the max of 5). Strictly an invader's tool: it never fires on defence, not even an Ambush. Why buy bullets when a brick works just fine, and why bring one gun when you can bring two?

Plunder (Cost: 1) Initiate a special attack: each hit steals 1 Liquor barrel of your choice from the target District instead of killing. Carry them off if you Advance (dropped if you Fall Back). Occupier hits still kill. You may also Plunder on an Ambush (Cost: 0). Never generates Heat.
Peddle (Cost: 1) Rotgut sells itself in the tenements. Unload as normal, but in any Ward you Control as well as Speakeasies. Only Moonshine may be sold this way, at the flat $300 per barrel street rate (no Kickback, no High Society price). The Greed Tax still bites at 4+ barrels.

East Side Vipers

They move like smoke and strike like snakes. Nothing happens in this city that the Vipers don't overhear first, and those Whispers are their edge: when they Hustle, they may peek at the top card of the Jobs deck and claim it face-down instead of a Market card. Rivals see the Stake, never the job; reveal it the moment it completes. You won't know they're there until the shots come from the shadows.

Stealth (Cost: 1) Move up to 5 Mobsters (no Liquor) into a Connected rival District without triggering a Standoff (the Occupier cannot Ambush or Fold). You cannot end your Play in this state. You must immediately spend another Influence to either Open Fire on the District, or Advance to a Connected Safe District.
Tunnel (Cost: 1) The city runs on rails the cops forgot. Move up to 5 Mobsters (no Liquor) between the District holding your Safehouse and any other District you Control, in either direction. Like Skiff, this is a Play on your own turn, not an escape hatch: you cannot use it to flee combat or a Police Raid.

Harlem Knights

Dressed to kill, literally. The Knights work the water, and no crew moves liquor like they do, because their Network runs every pier from Havana to Harlem. Whenever the Knights Trade at a Dock, they gain 1 extra barrel of Havana Rum for free, on top of the barrels they swap. Friends on every wharf, and a fresh cask waiting at each one.

Skiff (Cost: 1) Move up to 5 Mobsters from a Coastal District you Control to any other Coastal District. A Coastal District is any District touching water, so this reaches far beyond the Docks. Each Mobster may carry 1 Liquor barrel aboard (up to 5 barrels in all). If you enter a rival District, the standard Standoff applies (the Occupier may Ambush, Hold Fire, or Fold). Skiff is a Play on your own turn, not an escape hatch: you cannot use it to flee combat or a Police Raid.
Torch (Cost: 2) Burn down a Safehouse in a rival District. To perform this Play, you must have at least one Runner Pinned in the target District. Sacrifice one of those Pinned Runners; he strikes the match and goes up with the flames (return him to your supply). The rival's Safehouse is immediately destroyed (return it to their supply). Any rival Mobsters and Liquor in the District are unharmed and retain Control. This Play generates Heat.

Phase 3: Reckoning

Once the guns go quiet, the city settles its accounts. All players resolve this phase simultaneously, evaluating their empires exactly as they stand the moment the Operations Phase ends. Yesterday's holdings count for nothing.

1. The Sweep

The cops don't mind a hustle, but they hate an army.
In any District with more than 5 Mobsters, remove excess Runners to bring the count down to 5. (The Sicilian Syndicate is Untouchable and ignores the Sweep entirely.)

2. Stake Your Claim

This is where the crowns change hands. For each Title, whoever leads the category takes it or keeps it; if two or more rivals tie for the lead, or no one qualifies, it slips back to the Supply. (Police never count as rivals; turf they hold counts as empty for every Title check.)

  • Borough Deeds: Held by the player Controlling the most Districts in that Borough.
  • Ward Boss: Held by the player Controlling the most Ward Districts.
  • Harbormaster: Held by the player Controlling the most Dock Districts.
  • Night Mayor: Held by the player Controlling the most drinking venues (Speakeasy and High Society Districts combined).
  • Public Enemy No. 1: Held by the player with the most completed Sets of Jobs (1 Gig, 1 Racket, 1 Score).

The Next Day...

If any Kingpin holds all 10 Influence, 15+ Respect, and their honor, the game ends immediately! (see Winning the Game).

If not, the war continues. The Turn Tokens you claimed tonight become today's set, the other set becomes tomorrow's, and the next Day's Shadows Phase begins in the new Turn Order.

Winning the Game: The Final Reckoning

Every crew is grasping for the same brass ring, and the night one of you closes a fist around it, the war for New York is over. The crown falls to the boss who ends a Reckoning Phase holding all 10 Influence, 15+ Respect, and their honor intact. The books open only at Reckoning; a crown seized at noon can still slip away before nightfall.

1. Own the Commission (10 Influence)

Any thug can fire a Tommy gun, but it takes a true operator to buy the whole council. You must own all 10 Influence markers, the ceiling of your power. You start with 6 and buy the last four with the Bribe Play at $2,500 apiece, $10,000 to seal the deal, so the crown stays out of reach until your liquor economy is humming. Where the markers sit on the night doesn't matter, in Reserves, on the Ledger, staked on Jobs, or cooling on the Heat Track; what counts is that all ten are yours. Break your word on a staked deal, though, and that marker is forfeit to the box, one more bribe to pay all over again.

2. Earning Respect

Legitimacy means nothing if the streets don't fear you. You need 15 Respect to claim the crown. To calculate your Respect, tally the spoils of your empire:

  • Borough Turf: Gain 2 Respect for each Borough Deed you hold.
  • Titles: Gain 2 Respect for each of the Ward Boss, Harbormaster, Night Mayor, and Public Enemy No. 1 Titles you hold at the end of the game.
  • Criminal Legends: Gain 1, 3, or 5 Respect for each completed Job in your Respect pile. Turf and jobs are the surest roads to 15.

3. A Kingpin Must Have Honor

The council crowns no Rat and no debtor: while you hold the Rat Card or an unpaid Shylock's Mark, you cannot win, whatever your Respect. In the Blood Oath, neither can your Alliance. Shed the Mark, square the debt, then take the throne.

The Final Standoff

If multiple bosses own all 10 Influence and hold 15+ Respect on the same night, there's no war left to fight; the council simply opens the books and weighs one empire against the other:

  1. Total Respect: The highest Respect takes the crown.
  2. Cold Hard Cash: If deadlocked, the Kingpin with the fattest bankroll buys the throne (include the loose change printed on your Playbook).

There are no shared victories in Moonshine Kingdom. You either rule the city alone, or bleed out in the gutter.

Cooperation: Puppets in the Smoke

Trust is fleeting, but even sharp kingpins need rivals. Every favor granted might be a knife in the back.

Pulling the Strings (Puppeteering)

At any time during the Operations Phase, you may move Influence markers from your Ledger to a rival's Ledger (up to their 5 marker cap). This is not a Play and does not end your turn.

  • The Loan: You lend your name and network. The rival may use these markers as their own, now or later.
  • The Risk: If the rival spends your marker on a Play that generates Heat, your marker goes to the Heat Track. Otherwise, it returns to your Reserves once used.

A Binding Handshake

A Mobster's word is cheap. When a deal needs teeth, back it with the one thing you're grasping for: your Influence.

Making the Deal

  1. State the Terms & Deadline: The terms must be clear, and you must agree on a specific deadline (e.g., "before the Day ends," or "until you pay me $1,000").
  2. Stake a Marker: The player making the promise takes 1 Influence marker from their Reserves and places it between both players, where it sits locked until the deal resolves. Skin in the game, and everyone can see it.

Example: "My marker's on the table. If you let me pass through your Borough unharmed, I promise to give you $1,000 before Day's end."

Honoring & Breaking Deals

Deals resolve the moment their conditions are met or failed.

  • Deal Honored: The moment the terms are met, the deal is complete. The staked marker returns to its owner's Reserves.
  • Deal Broken: If the agreed deadline passes without the terms being met, or if a player violates a standing agreement for any reason (including third-party interference), their word becomes garbage. The staked marker is forfeit to the box, gone for good, dropping the breaker's Influence by one. In this town, a broken promise costs you a piece of your throne.

Deals on the Side

You may wheel and deal outside the rules, but only Cash can be physically traded off the board. Hand it over anytime, no Influence required.

Barrel Exchanges: Liquor stays on the board. To hand barrels to a rival, Move them into that crew's Connected District; the hooch is theirs on arrival. A mutual trade is simply two Moves, one from each crew.

Combat Rules: Blood on the Streets

Violence isn't a backup plan; it's the cost of doing business. Smart Kingpins know a drawn gun beats a fired one. When talk fails, step inside and clear the room.

1. The Rules of Engagement

Calculating Threat

Your Base Threat is Level 1 (kill on 5+). Bonuses stack, to a maximum of Level 4:

  • Boss +1 Threat: if your Boss is in the fight.
  • Safehouse +2 Threat: the Occupier defending Open Fire on their own Secured District (not on the Ambush shot).
  • Ambush +1 Threat: the Occupier's first shot, on the opening Ambush only.

2. The Standoff: Crossing the Line

Every firefight is just two questions, asked in order:

  • Step 1, the Occupier answers: Ambush, Hold Fire, or Fold.
  • Step 2, the Invader chooses (and keeps choosing until the Pin is resolved): Open Fire, Advance, or Fall Back.

When an opponent Moves into a District you Control, the game pauses on Step 1. Set each crew along the edge it crossed from, so it stays clear whose Mobsters and barrels are whose. As the Occupier, you must immediately choose one of three responses:

  1. Ambush: You greet the invaders with lead.
    • Free Shot: Only the Occupier rolls dice.
    • Bonus: Gain +1 Threat.
    • Casualties: Hits remove Invaders (Runners first). No Heat. Invaders are now Pinned.
  2. Hold Fire: You stand your ground but don't shoot. Maybe they're just passing through. The Invaders are now Pinned.
  3. Fold (Cost: 0): You surrender the District. You may immediately move your Mobsters to one Connected Safe District.
    • Panic: Fleeing Mobsters cannot carry Liquor; any barrels are left behind, and your Safehouse stays put.
    • The Takeover: The Invader immediately claims Control, captures dropped Liquor, and may take over or destroy your Safehouse. The Invaders are not Pinned; their Play ends.

3. Pinned: Fight or Flight (Step 2)

While Pinned, your Mobsters cannot make normal Plays. As the Invader you must resolve the standoff before your turn moves on: spend Influence to Open Fire or Advance, or Fall Back for free. Keep choosing until the Pin is resolved, or you run dry and must Fall Back. Hit, Torch, and Plunder still work while Pinned (the Irish Firepower Trait boosts Open Fire only).

The Cost of Pride: A real Kingpin knows an all-out firefight is bad business. Burning 3 or 4 Influence to keep opening fire on one stubborn alley may win it, but it leaves rivals free across the city. Talk is cheap; bullets cost time.

Open Fire (Cost: 1) Fight for the District. Both sides roll simultaneously.
  • Casualties: Both sides apply hits (Runners first). The Occupier loses the Ambush bonus but keeps Safehouse/Boss bonuses.
  • Heat: The law doesn't blame a man for defending his own corner, so the Occupier never draws Heat. The Invader gains Heat only if their own dice score 1+ kills.
  • Repeat: If both sides stand, remain Pinned. Spend another Influence to Open Fire again.
Advance (Cost: 1) Shoot your way out. Move Pinned Mobsters, with any barrels they hold, to any Safe Connected District, including across water via Docks. You cannot enter hostile turf. (You fought clear, so the hooch rides out with you.)
Fall Back (Cost: 0)
(Invader Only)		 Cut and run. Retreat to the origin District you entered from. Carried Liquor is left behind for the Occupier.
If you have 0 Influence remaining, you must Fall Back.

4. Victory & Consequences

Combat ends when one side is eliminated or the Invader retreats.

If the Occupier Holds: The District remains theirs. Liquor dropped by dead Invaders becomes the Occupier's property.

If the Invader Wins: The moment the last of the Occupier's pieces falls or flees, you claim Control of the District.

  • The Spoils: Any Liquor in the District is now yours.
  • Safehouses: A rival Safehouse in the District is destroyed (remove it), unless you take it over instead: immediately Secure the District for free by relocating your Safehouse there from wherever it sat before.

Heat: Playing with Fire

Heat measures how hard the law is breathing down your neck. The Heat Track is a 5-space powder keg: generate Heat and you place one Influence marker on it. Played right it's a weapon; played wrong, a noose.

Drawing Heat

These noisy Plays push the track up one notch (any Play marked with the flame icon ):

  • Blood on the Pavement: If an Invader's dice inflict 1+ kills in combat, add 1 marker at the end of the Play. A multi-round firefight draws Heat only once.
  • Shakedowns Get Loud: The Extort Power Play always draws Heat , however many Districts you squeeze.
  • The Greed Tax: Unloading 4+ barrels in a single Play draws Heat .

Police Raids: The Boiling Point

A Raid triggers two ways: the 5th marker lands on the track, or a rival makes the Rat Play. Either way, pause the turn; the city's on lockdown.

A Raid puts every Squad on the move. Resolve them one at a time, in the numbered Borough order printed on the board, each finishing before the next stirs. Four precincts kicking doors in, block by block.

1. The Most Wanted List

Each Squad hunts within reach and chases the freshest gunshot it can hear. Its reach is only the Districts directly Connected to it inside its own Borough. Find the target in two steps:

  1. Pick the mob (Follow the Smoke): Of the mobs holding those in-reach Districts, target the one whose marker sits furthest right on the Heat Track. If no District in reach carries any Heat, the Squad stays put. Quiet money always walks free.
  2. Pick the District (The Big Bust): If that mob holds one in-reach District, that's the target. If it holds several, the Squad kicks in the one with the most Barrels (cops follow the contraband); on a tie, the hottest boiler, ranked highest on its Pressure Strip. The louder the boiler, the harder the door comes off.

2. The Scatter

The Squad takes one step onto the chosen District and kicks the doors in:

  • Condemned: Any Safehouse there is destroyed and all Liquor seized (both to supply).
  • Run For It: The crew bolts to a single Connected Safe District, never back into the one the Squad advanced from, and no grabbing barrels on the way.
  • Cornered: If there's no Connected Safe District to flee to (every exit blocked by rivals, water, the Blue Wall, or the map's edge), the whole crew is arrested and returned to your supply. Leave a back door, or don't leave at all.

The Squad now holds the District as an impassable wall, padlocking any Speakeasy or Still beneath it until a later Raid. A crew fleeing one Squad may land in the path of the next; the night is young.

The Aftermath: Once every Squad has moved, all Heat markers return to their owners' Reserves and the turn resumes.

The Blood Oath: 4-Player Team Variant

When the heat boils over, enemies become brothers. The Feds are kicking down doors and lone wolves get slaughtered. The Blood Oath is the recommended 4-player mode: a cutthroat free-for-all where escalating police pressure eventually forces the city into two permanent 2v2 alliances.

Setup: The Ticking Clock

Set up as a standard 4-player match, with one addition: place the Federal Crackdown Tracker near the Heat Track with a token on space 1. Every time a Police Raid triggers, advance the token one space.

The Sit-Down

The moment the token reaches space 10 on the Crackdown Tracker, the underworld snaps. Pause the game. The four independent mobs immediately form two permanent alliances.

  1. Rank the Bosses: Tally everyone's current Respect (scoring held Titles and completed Jobs). Tiebreaker: Total Cash on hand.
  2. Forge the Families: The player in 1st Place joins forces with the player in 4th Place. The player in 2nd Place pairs with the player in 3rd Place.
  3. Set the Targets: Each Alliance adds 10 to its combined Respect: that number is its Target. From here, it's a clean race to earn 10.

New Rules of Engagement

One goal, two crews. You still run your own operations.

  • Separate Ledgers: You do not share Cash, Influence, or Jobs, and you still take individual turns in Turn Order.
  • Blood is Thicker: You cannot attack your partner or their property (no Ambush, Open Fire, Hit, Plunder, or Torch). All other rules stand: partner turf is still Hostile (Pinned there, your only outs are Advance or Fall Back), the Golden Rule of Turf means you cannot share a District at Day's end, and Liquor Moved into their District becomes theirs.
  • Puppeteering: Your strongest weapon. You may freely lend Influence to your partner's Ledger to fund their turn (standard Heat rules apply).

Claiming the Empire

The council only recognizes unified power. To win, an Alliance must reach its Target and have at least one partner owning all 10 Influence (one crew buys the council on behalf of the family). If met during the Reckoning Phase, the game ends immediately: first family to the finish takes the city.

The New Kings of New York

If both Alliances hit their Targets at the same Reckoning, the crown goes to the pair furthest over its Target; still deadlocked, the fattest combined bankroll buys the throne.

The Capo di Tutti Capi: There are no truly shared victories... even in a team. Within the winning Alliance, the player who contributed the most Respect to the final combined total is declared the Boss of Bosses and the true Kingpin of New York. Your partner doesn't get a crown; they just get the privilege of kissing your ring.
