The Brew Engine

How the dawn stills really make their money — and why the smart boss clumps his boilers, spreads his crew, and drafts last.

Every Day opens in the Shadows, when your stills fire and the barrels flow. It looks like luck. It isn't. Behind the dice sits a small, sharp bit of math, and the bosses who read it right out-earn the ones who don't — without ever rolling better. This is that math, in plain English.

The One Rule That Governs Everything

In the Morning Brew you draft one Red die and add it to the public White die. That sum — your Brew Number — is the only number your empire cooks on this Day. Every Still you Control whose boiler shows that number fires at once; every Still showing a different number sits cold.

Read that twice, because the whole strategy falls out of it: you only ever hit one number per Day. A boss with a 7-boiler and an 8-boiler never fires both on the same morning. A boss with two 7-boilers fires both, together, off the same lucky roll. Matching numbers stack; mismatched numbers take turns.

Three Principles

1

Clump your numbers, never spread them

Boilers that share a number fire as one. Boilers on different numbers mostly sit idle — and every idle boiler is still a live Blowback target. Spreading your Stills across 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 doesn't diversify your income; it just gives the leftover die five different ways to blow up your crew. Two matching boilers beat four scattered ones.

2

Spread your crew, don't pile it on

The Muscle Ratio is a staircase: 5–6 mobsters brew 3 barrels, but you need 9+ to reach the cap of 5, and every runner past that is dead weight. The best rate is around 5–6 mobsters on a boiler (3 barrels). Once a Still is well-crewed, the next runner earns far more opening a second matching boiler than deepening the first. Breadth of matching Stills beats depth on one.

3

Hold the match — draft position is everything

The last boss to draft chooses which Red gets left behind, and that leftover sets the Blowback. Against a boss stacking 7s, a sharp table denies the 7-Reds and tries to leave one behind to burst the whole stack. Your biggest engine is only as safe as your seat. When you're exposed, take the low turn-order token and lay low last.

Park the Boss: Crack the Whip

One lever the tables below leave out: Crack the Whip. Any Still that fires with your Boss standing on it brews +1 barrel. Since your matching boilers all fire off the same Brew Number, park the old man on any one of them and pocket a free barrel every morning the stack lights up. Keep that Still crewed and it's near-pure upside: in a Blowback the Runners take the shrapnel first, so bodies on the boiler shield the Boss from the blast. The one trap is sitting him on a lightly-guarded still that bursts with the Don beside it, so never park him where you can't protect him. A Boss brewing is a Boss earning; a Boss brewing alone is a Boss gambling.

Two things jump out. First, matching always beats mismatching at the same runner count — the mismatched 6/7/8 build earns less and bleeds three times the Recruit cost, because each extra number is another Blowback trap. Second, a determined table roughly halves the clumper's take (the $711 three-stack falls to $368): denial is real, and it's why the boom can't run away with the game on its own.

Why you can't just win with 7s. There are only four 7-boilers on the whole board — one in each mainland Borough, and none on Staten Island. Firing three or four at once means Controlling a district in three or four separate Boroughs at the same time: conquest, expansion, and a lot of scattered turf to defend against Raids and rivals. The engine is real, but every extra boiler is bought with blood and board presence. That cost is the balance.

So What's the Optimal Play?

There isn't one build that always wins — and that's by design. The right answer swings with how contested the board is:

Best build by board state

Contested
(the usual case)		 2–3 matching boilers, tight Enough boom to matter; little enough turf that you can still defend it.

(everyone's on you)		 Stay compact — 1–2 boilers, well-crewed Expansion doesn't pay when every new district invites a Raid. Bank the sure money.

The through-line: build the widest matching engine the board will let you defend, and not one boiler wider. The skill isn't finding the best build — it's reading when the table has made the next boiler cost more than it earns.

Kingpin's Note

The greenhorn spreads his Stills to "cover more numbers" and wonders why he's broke — he's firing one boiler a day and feeding the rest to the Blowback. The made man cooks one number, crews each boiler just enough, grabs the low token when his stack gets fat, and lets the last Red burn a rival instead. Same dice. Different fortune.

Want to test it yourself? Open the Brew Simulator and run a few hundred thousand mornings, or check the Turn Structure card for the Shadows sequence and the boiler numbers Borough by Borough.
